Stated by the operator 2026-08-05 (Playnet survey), correcting me after I wrote that we'd had
"vastly more resource" than a peer collective:

One developer with a full-time day job to maintain, paying out of pocket for an AI coding
subscription. That is the whole of it.

**The resource reality.** No team. No grant. No institution. A day job that must be maintained.
The only spend is a personal tooling subscription. By money, the Playnet collective (€13,945
lifetime, one paid developer for a few months) was **better resourced than Elohim is**. Never
frame Elohim as the well-funded party assessing a struggling peer — that comparison is factually
backwards and it insults both sides of it.

**The thesis this establishes.** The operator's read of why so much exists anyway:

> "the reality we've got as far as we have is the pudding proof that convergence on a performant
> architecture for a new type of coordination is now, and we're looking for friends who need a new
> social contract that can scale."

So the differentiator is **not** resources — it is architecture convergence plus the
human+AI collaboration model. One developer and an AI collaborator is the crew that peers like
Playnet never had; that is precisely the single-committer ceiling being broken (their 26
help-wanted issues went unmerged for want of capacity, not want of will). The body of work
existing at this resourcing is itself the evidence that the moment has arrived.

**How to apply:**
- Never assume budget, staff, ops capacity, or runway. Cost and operator time are real constraints;
  prefer solutions one person can maintain alongside a day job.
- In surveys and outreach, position as **peer at the same ceiling who found a lever**, never as
  benefactor, auditor, or better-resourced party.
- The one-line offer to aligned collectives is the operator's own:
  *we're looking for friends who need a new social contract that can scale.*
- Treat what already exists as proof-of-concept for the human+AI leverage claim — it is the
  strongest empirical argument the project has, and it is worth making explicitly.
