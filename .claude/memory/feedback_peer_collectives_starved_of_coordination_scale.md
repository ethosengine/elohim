Operator correction, twice (2026-08-05, Playnet survey). I framed Playnet's OpenCollective numbers
(€13,945 lifetime received, €77.92 balance, €15/month recurring, one committer at 77% of spend) as
"funding-cliff risk," then — after a first correction — kept the facts but slid into a
**prosecutorial audit register**: "the single most damning artifact," "nobody had spare capacity to
renew a domain," bus-factor-as-defect. Both wrong.

**The scale correction.** €13,945 *lifetime* is not a budget — it is roughly seven weeks of one
senior engineer's salary at a firm optimizing consumer funnels. Against that, this collective
produced a 93-page spec with 42 equations, ValueFlows lexicons on AT Protocol, a DDMRP planning
engine, ~8,500 lines of Guile/Goblins, a working distributed-IPF app, Interval Tree Clocks, a W3C VC
identity stack, and playlabs on two continents. **Output-per-euro is extraordinary.** Reading the
ledger as underperformance inverts the finding. Work at that output-per-euro is being carried on
resources an incumbent would treat as a rounding error.

**The structural asymmetry is the thesis, not a caveat.** The incumbent system pays €100k+ for
people who build more efficient consumer goods and rent schemes, because that pays back *inside*
existing network effects. An economic alternative has no payback until it reaches scale, and cannot
reach scale without capital that only flows to things already inside the incumbent's logic. A
coordination alternative therefore starves precisely where it most needs network effects. That trap
is structural and it is the field's, not any collective's.

**Why:** treating a peer's scarcity as their defect inverts the whole thesis. If p2p self-limits
scale AND capital self-selects against alternatives, a thin ledger is evidence they are *inside the
problem we exist to solve*. The wonder is what exists at all, not that it stalled.

**How to apply:**
- Report a peer collective's resourcing as **shared condition and structural asymmetry** — never as
  a durability demerit or a performance review. Lead with output-per-euro, not burn-down.
- **Watch the register.** Facts can be right while tone indicts. Ban prosecutorial framing
  ("damning," "failed to," "couldn't even") for peers. A stalled repo with 26 help-wanted issues is
  evidence of *demand they were starved of capacity to absorb*, not negligence.
- Keep dependency-risk language strictly scoped to "can we technically build on this artifact"
  (licence, API stability, maturity) and say so explicitly when you use it.
- The research payload is **what ceiling they hit and why** — that ceiling is also ours.
