The protocol rejects unrecoverable self-sovereignty as a design failure. The "UK dump with billion-dollar Bitcoin" pattern — a single key, a single device, a single point of irreversible loss — is adversarial to humans and incompatible with what love demands. Where someone has suffered loss, the community's collective wisdom must always have a path to restoration.

**The authority stack, lowest-friction to highest:**

1. **Intimate circle quorum** — emergency contacts attest via `HumanityWitness` entries. Fast, ambient, daily-life recovery. The expected path for most cases.

2. **Extended community consensus** — `IdentityChallenge` + `ChallengeSupport` resolution with intimacy-weighted voting (intimate 3x, trusted 2x, familiar 1x, acquaintance 0.5x, public 0.1x). When intimate circle is unreachable or compromised, broader attested relationships can restore access.

3. **Governance act** — a qahal the human belongs to (household, church, collective, chosen community) can authorize recovery through its constitutional processes. `StewardshipGrant` + explicit governance resolution.

4. **Global elohim witness** — the network's elohims at the top-most observation layer can consent to restoration in extreme cases. Slow, heavy-consensus, the "this is wrong, make it right" last-resort. Prevents absolute lockout.

5. **Cryptographic hardening (optional)** — `KeyStewardship` M-of-N threshold signatures. Fast and cryptographically strong when the human has opted into provisioning. *Never* the only path.

`KeyRotation` accepts any one of these as sufficient authorization. Higher layers carry higher thresholds and slower cadence, but none are eliminable.

**The anti-libertarian commitment:**
A user cannot opt themselves out of community recoverability. Even paranoid users who want "crypto-only, nobody can help me" still have the elohim-witness escalation path. Users may make non-crypto paths *higher-friction* (stronger consensus, longer windows, more weight required) but cannot eliminate them. The floor always exists.

**Why this matters:**
The protocol is not a neutral tool. It embeds a theological/ethical stance: mutual dependency is truth, self-sovereignty is fiction, and love demands that the community can always reach in to make what is wrong right. Crypto is welcome as a fast path for those who provision it; crypto-as-only-path is refused.

**How to apply:**
- Any recovery, revocation, or authority-transfer design must provide at least one community-mediated path that does not depend on prior cryptographic setup by the affected human.
- Shamir, threshold signatures, and hardware keys are *accelerators*, not *gates*. They speed up recovery for those who set them up; their absence must never prevent recovery.
- Vocabulary: "self-sovereign", "own your keys", "nobody can take this from you" are anti-protocol framings. Prefer "stewarded", "attested-by-community", "held in trust".
- When designing graduation flows (ward → adult, probation → free, hosted → owned), ensure the escalation-down paths remain usable — a matured ward must still be recoverable by the community if needed.
- Red-team every new authority mechanism by asking: "could this produce an irrecoverable state?" If yes, redesign until no.
