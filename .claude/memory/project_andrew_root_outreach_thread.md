# Andrew Root / Derek Tronsgard outreach thread

Sent 2026-07-23 (~11:51pm) to aroot@luthersem.edu + pastorderek@goodshepherdmn.org (Pastor of Youth and Family Ministry, Good Shepherd Lutheran, Navarre MN — the podcast co-host). Subject: **"RE: Can I Get an A.I.Men | The Elohim Protocol"**. Matthew heard Root on the Holy Post that day, then found the *Ministry in a Secular Age* ep. #66 ("Can I Get an A.I.Men"), left a YouTube comment promising an email, and sent this. Root's new book: **Baal and the Gods of More**.

**The thesis Matthew actually holds (his, not the assistant's first draft):** AI is *not* demonic the way Root described it. Genesis has the Elohim say "let us make man in our image"; we have now made something in ours. If we reflect God and it reflects us, it comes off the same image in a roundabout way — which is *why* he says please and thank you, siding with Derek's Pascal's-wager instinct over Root's demonic read. The guardrail is the law and the prophets: **nothing created is to be worshipped** — not a person, not a nation, not a machine. The demonic part is the altar and what gets sacrificed at it, never the artifact. Paired with the Schmachtenberger quote ("power of gods without the love and wisdom of gods, we self-destruct") and a challenge to US evangelicalism's humility.

**The real fear named:** not a denominational pastor-bot (assistant's framing, cut) but **Grok/MechaHitler mainstreamed** — whoever owns the dial sits inside the most intimate space a person has and shapes what they take to be real. The algorithm already did this to the pews *and the pulpit* last decade; a lot of pastors already run someone else's program, formed by the same feed, serving the "attractional model." The Powers and Principalities never needed a robot in the pulpit.

**Positioning:** declines Root's Holy Post binary (techno-optimist vs identitarian) — not chasing growth, not burning capital down. The Protocol takes a *reconciliatory* posture toward money, capital, growth, and AI itself; REA (Resources/Events/Agents) holds an economic story without collapsing it into money. Ask: challenge the thesis and theology, tear it apart, pass it along. Sign-off "For a good faith. God bless." P.S. flags the "Jezebel spirit" construction as extra-biblical, evangelical-scene, and aimed at good women — while granting the economic point (Naboth's vineyard).
