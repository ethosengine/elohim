A non-native consuming app (Jenkins, first case) serves the network and never controls it:
- **Gifts flow outward.** The network hands out recipes, plans, drift and reuse evidence. Refusing any of them costs the app nothing.
- **Observations flow inward.** The app's acts enter only as observation-plane Process/FlowEvent records, with `service:<app>` as provenance. They never become commitments, heads, grants or verdicts.
- **No dependency on the consumer.** The network never waits on the app or needs it.

The gift seam is a *negotiated* seam, because how the protocol gives value is the vector for coercion and abuse. Every gift is a governed offer:
- It is backed by a peer-validated `.epr-meta` governance relationship: the ethosengine collective. human:matthew is the only real human. human:adam is a TEST-FIXTURE human who co-stewards at Bootstrap stakes: the real primitives run, and the verdict is labeled `fixture`/`bootstrap`, never read as network validation.
- It is active only with a verdict from a Steward who didn't author it (anti-self-election).
- It can be withdrawn, and it creates no claims.

Resolving REA flows to a token or fiat settlement is an act of governance, because that value leaves the network. It is never an asserted right over value backed by an external network's money.

Every edge relationship also DISCLOSES what the EPR receives from outside the network (inflows, held external capabilities, and positive AND negative externalities), so no EPR uses external power to mask its capabilities. Disclosure is a claim, not truth. The network's felt sense of the edge is declared compared with observed, and a growing gap ("feels good, masks something corrupt") is the algedonic signal, routed to the Stewards as a concern with redress available, never an automatic penalty.

Governance must be legible AT THE POINT OF CONTACT: someone who finds the consumer (e.g. the Jenkins pipeline) can discover, explore, verify (CIDs, signers, fixture standing) and give feedback on the governing EPR's claims without being an insider. Anonymous feedback lands at low reach, counted and visible, and never withdraws an offer on its own.

Anyone may author spec-conforming EPR/REA claims outside a peer runtime (being legible needs no permission: spec + CID). Standing (reach, notarization, Steward verdict) is earned only inside. Projected-in external claims (Jenkins, Requests & Offers via hREA, an elohim agent acting outside) enter as low-reach external-claim observations with provenance and signature status stated, and are evaluated on entry. Inside standing doesn't carry over to claims made outside (anti-laundering). They take up governance attention only when someone with standing brings them forward.

External entities are imagined as UNCLAIMED PRESENCES: claims about them, disclosures, observed behavior and the collective's positions toward them build up there, and recognition for outside labor flows to them under commons stewardship until they attest (the developer guide's "value still flows" rule, extended to entities). Positions held about an absent entity must stay contestable when it arrives.

**Why:** the network can be generous only if its participants can decide collectively, both to defend against bad faith and to negotiate fair settlement of claims.

**How to apply:** any bridge, gift, offer or payout design first names the governing collective, its Stewards and the distinct-Steward verdict. It keeps settlement a separate Agreement, never implied by an offer or an observation. Settlement, and offers to consumers we don't own, need two NON-fixture Stewards; until a second real human exists, the collective is starved, not failing. Authority at a network boundary comes from a human grant, never the consumer's position.
