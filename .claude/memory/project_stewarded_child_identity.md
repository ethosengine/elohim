Stewarded children (Timothy is the canonical persona) are humans whose devices participate in the network without holding full signing authority over their own source chain. Their device holds a bounded, revocable capability grant issued by a guardian (Matthew, Jessica, or whichever parent/caretaker steward is in topology). The device commits within policy; anything outside the envelope requires guardian co-sign. This is architecturally distinct from the adult-identity pattern where the human's device holds their own keys.

Stewarded-child is **one station on the cradle-to-grave stewardship spectrum**, not a standalone feature. The same capability-grant primitives must also serve: legal custody (court-imposed, foster, divorced co-parents), criminal-justice stewardship (probation, parole, civil commitment), intellectual disability (partial capacity, permanent or fluctuating), and senior decline (memory loss, power of attorney, full guardianship). A human may be ward → self-sovereign → steward of others → ward again across a lifetime. The protocol's primitives must flow through all of it. See `project_stewardship_philosophy.md` for the six principles the protocol commits to across this spectrum.

Sync shape follows the stewardship shape: stewarded-child devices go offline on a predictable cadence (school lets out, Chromebook closes) and hit cold-start reconnection bursts the next time they come online. Resource budgets need to accommodate catch-up sync, not just steady-state contention — this is why Timothy's Chromebook-floor limits OOM on seed burst even though steady-state they'd be fine. Other points on the spectrum will have their own sync shapes (senior's tablet may be nearly always-on but light; probation-device may have scheduled check-ins), but the catch-up-burst vs steady-state distinction generalizes.

**Why:** Stewarded children are a real class of protocol participant (K–12 students on school-issued Chromebooks, young kids on family hand-me-down tablets) but they don't map cleanly onto either "full steward with own keys" or "pure thin browser client." The custodial-capability model is the Holochain-native answer — capability grants are already part of the DNA primitive set, and they give the guardian revocation + scope control without requiring the child's device to be cryptographically adult. If we treat stewarded-child as a one-off special case instead of one station on a lifecycle, we'll rebuild the same primitives five more times for custody, probation, disability, and elder care — and they'll drift.

**How to apply:** When touching anything involving Timothy, custodial/guardian identity flows in imagodei, capability grant issuance, or stewardship topology — remember that "stewarded child" is one visible station on a cradle-to-grave lifecycle, and design primitives that generalize. Flags to watch for: assumptions that every human holds their own signing keys, enrollment flows that don't have a guardian-issued path, lamad scenarios that treat Timothy's actions as self-authorized, special-case code that won't handle "senior with partial POA" when it arrives. A `humans-stewarded-child.feature` under `genesis/a2o/features/auth/` would be the right first scenario scaffold; anticipate that it'll be joined by `humans-ward-legal-custody.feature`, `humans-ward-elder-care.feature`, etc. Cross-references: `genesis/plans/2026-04-13-device-archetypes-design.md` (device archetypes), `project_multi_device_humans.md` (one human → multiple devices with different authority levels), `project_stewardship_philosophy.md` (the six principles).
