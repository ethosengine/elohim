The question Hank asked in his shelter-bench video — "will AI allow wisdom to flourish, or let the powerful route around wisdom?" — is widely held, rarely answered. The diagnosis: **wild success creates intellectual sunk costs**. People whose value has come from operating well inside current systems cannot easily imagine systems that don't extend their success. The innovator's dilemma applied to social architecture, not just product strategy.

The people most *expected* to answer shelter-bench-grade questions (AI lab leaders, VCs, established tech voices) are the ones with the most sunk cost in the answer being "more of the current system, but smarter." Their imaginative range is bounded by what extends their existing valuation. So the answer either has to come from outside the prestige economy, or from people inside it who are *consciously rejecting the optimization pull* (Hank converting Complexly to nonprofit is exactly this — he named the pull in his podcast).

**Why:** This is the empathetic explanation for why a widely-felt question goes mostly unanswered, and the rhetorical justification for writing to people like Hank rather than to "people who should be solving it." It positions the protocol's author (Matthew) as having less to lose — government applications by day, no equity in the current AI paradigm — and therefore freer to imagine. It validates the recipient (Hank) by naming a structural condition he's escaping, not by flattering him personally.

**How to apply:** Use near the end of any pitch where the recipient is asking "why isn't this obvious to people who should know?" Pattern: (1) the question is widely held, (2) the answer requires imaginative range that sunk costs prevent, (3) so we look to people structurally freer of those costs. Don't use this on people whose sunk costs you'd be naming — use it WITH them, when they're already showing they can think past their incentives.
