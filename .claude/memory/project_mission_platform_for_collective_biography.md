This is a mission-tier entry — re-read before any major design decision when you need to remember what the protocol is **for**.

The anchor passage (Douglas A. Foster, *Reclaiming Reconciliation: The Corruption of "Racial Reconciliation" and How It Might Be Reclaimed for Racial Justice and Unity*, Journal of Ecumenical Studies 55:1, Winter 2020, p. 80, drawing on Ta-Nehisi Coates, "The Case for Reparations," The Atlantic, June 2014):

> *"the process of investigating the details and acknowledging them publicly would, as pointed out by Coates, provide a platform for accepting our 'collective biography and its consequences,' for aiding families and communities in regaining a sense of the rightness of this effort and in restoring dignity to those who have suffered shame and oppression."*

(Article preserved in conversation context but not in repo — too long for commit. Author is a personal friend of Matthew's.)

**The four moves the protocol must make, in causal order:**

1. **Investigation** — *"investigating the details."* The process IS the point. Not verdicts, settlements, or markers-as-artifacts — the *walking the graph* itself is where the work happens. Graph-native, EPR provenance, attestation history, chronicle integrity. The substrate makes investigation possible without coercion or curation. ([[project_graph_native_is_accountability_substrate]] is the structural enabler.)

2. **Public acknowledgment** — *"acknowledging them publicly."* Public-by-default rather than ask-permission-by-default. Doorway projections, reach signaling, witnessable couplings, story canonization. The protocol refuses the comfort of private narratives that quietly diverge from collective biography.

3. **Biographical accumulation with consequences** — *"a platform for accepting our 'collective biography and its consequences.'"* EPRs are biographical atoms. Couplings are relationship history. Stories are canonical narrative compressions. Reach is the social topology those biographies live inside. **The whole architecture is a platform for collective biography that doesn't let you sanitize history.** And the second half is the part most platforms refuse: *and its consequences*. The protocol isn't a vanity wall; it's a substrate where what happened has weight that persists. ([[project_genesis_is_protocol_interpretability_layer]] — genesis artifacts already running this in dev-mode.)

4. **Regained sense of rightness + restored dignity** — *"aiding families and communities in regaining a sense of the rightness of this effort and in restoring dignity to those who have suffered shame and oppression."* This is the deepest claim and the one the protocol earns by doing the first three. Households, qahals, collectives must be able to **feel that the work of being accountable to each other through the substrate is rightful work, not procedural overhead.** That's the grandmother bar from the inside-out — not just *she can use it* but *she feels the work she does here is hers to do, and worth doing.* And the final clause names the audience-of-first-resort: those who have suffered shame and oppression. Restored dignity, not user engagement.

**The causal order is non-negotiable.** You can't restore dignity by skipping the biography. You can't acknowledge what hasn't been investigated. The protocol cannot start at the end. ([[project_subsume_g_f_a_via_it_just_works]] is *not* a usability target that lets us skip the investigation step — it's a target for what the final experience feels like *after* the substrate has done its first three moves.)

**Audience of first resort.** The personas in `genesis/data/humans/` are not decorative. *Mary-migrant, Charlie-challenged, Ronald-refugee, Janice-janitor, Lenny-lineworker, Pam-polarized, Pete-pastor, Gertrude-grandma, James-son, Terrance-tutor* — these are commitments. Foster's "those who have suffered shame and oppression" names the same audience the simulacra do. The protocol's audience-of-first-resort is the people who have been at the bottom of every system that captured their relationships, their words, their data, their dignity. Not the technologically savvy. Not the developers. Not the early adopters.

Re-read this one when:

- A design decision optimizes for engagement, retention, or growth — *those are the metrics of platforms that DON'T do dignity restoration.* Use the anchor as a test: does this move advance investigation → acknowledgment → biography → dignity, in that order?
- A simplification proposal compresses the four moves into one (e.g., "let's just show the verdict / let's just record the apology / let's just track who-did-what"). Each move is non-trivial; the substrate must not collapse them.
- A scope question arises about whether to support repair operations (reach reversal, attestation revocation, stewardship redistribution, honest coupling closure). The answer is yes — without those, the protocol stops at description and never reaches dignity. ([[project_epistemic_integrity_under_pressure]] articulates the repair requirement.)
- An audience question arises about who the protocol is "really for." It's for those who have suffered shame and oppression. Everyone else is welcome, but the design call has to make sense for that audience first.
