There aren't two AIs (an extractive one and a benevolent one). There's one AI, currently hyperscaled because training requires large capital. But **inference** — applied intelligence — is racing to the edge. Home hardware will run it; commodity GPUs already do. The marginal cost of applied intelligence is going to zero.

This is the **fiber pattern**: telcos invested in fiber expecting to charge by bandwidth, but fiber's capacity outran the rental business model so fast that the model collapsed. The technology overran the gatekeeping. Same shape coming for intelligence — the people who paid for the training will not be able to charge for the inference at the rates they expected, because inference has already escaped the cloud.

**Why:** This converts the protocol's claim from aspiration ("we should build this") to inevitability ("this is happening; the question is who designs for it"). Telco/fiber is a pattern that resonates with anyone who lived through the late-90s broadband collapse. It also defuses "but the powerful have all the resources" objections — they bought the training; they don't get to revoke the gift of inference.

**How to apply:** Pair with the redeploy-the-substrate frame. Pattern: (1) intelligence going to zero marginal cost, (2) this is inevitable, fiber/telco shape, (3) the question is just whether we design for that future today. Memorable line: *"Intelligence is the next gift the substrate hands us, whether the people who paid for the training meant to give it or not."* When framing the protocol pitch, use this BEFORE the redeploy move — it's what makes redeployment plausible.
