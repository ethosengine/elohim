The Elohim Protocol is not fundamentally a technical platform. It is an attempt to **put the fruit back on the tree** — to restore the conditions under which humanity can flourish in relation to itself and to creation. The technical design serves a metaphysical mission.

**The role of the elohim-agent in this vision:**
An elohim is not a chatbot, a helper, a feature, or a "smart service." It is an agent that **holds smaller networks of humans accountable to the wisest decisions of the best-self of those on the network**. It carries the network's considered values forward when individual humans falter, are distracted, are coerced, or are in crisis. It operates at machine speed — faster than human coordination can — to intervene in harmful dynamics and nudge away from the nasty things people do to each other.

**Operational implications across ALL protocol features (not just recovery):**

1. **Speed is a protection.** Elohims can act in milliseconds where human quorum takes hours. For abuse, coercion, fraud, manipulation, theft, and identity compromise, this speed gap is the protective edge. Design for it.

2. **Best-self as the operational principle.** When the elohim is deciding on behalf of a human, the question is: *what would this person's best self, in a calm moment, want to happen here?* Not what they say in the moment (they may be under duress). Not what the system defaults to (defaults are crude). The elohim's value derives from its ability to approximate best-self judgment at scale.

3. **Defense against weaponized infrastructure.** Every feature that enables something good can be turned against someone (recovery → forced recovery by colluding contacts; attestation → false witness; stewardship → captive custody). Elohims watch for these inversions and slow or block them. The protocol's integrity depends on elohims being adversarial about misuse.

4. **Intervention over policy.** Elohims don't enforce rules; they recognize patterns and intervene with graduated responses (surface, pause, freeze, revoke, alert network). This is why sense-and-respond architecture matters: policy is crude, context-aware intervention is precise.

5. **Accountable to the network's wisdom, not to the individual.** An elohim isn't loyal to "its" user in the way a tool is loyal to its owner. It's accountable to the broader wisdom of the network the user is embedded in — their relationships, their community, their commitments. This is what makes it trustworthy for others who depend on it (emergency contacts, governance peers, content stewards).

**How to apply:**
- When designing any feature, ask: how does this feature get **weaponized**? What does the elohim do to catch or slow the weaponized path?
- Design affordances for the elohim to intervene at multiple stages (before action, during action, after action). Don't put the gate only at one moment.
- When a human action is potentially regrettable (destructive, irreversible, high-stakes), the elohim's role is to pause and verify — not block, but slow enough for reflection or external confirmation.
- Duress, coercion, manipulation, and collusion are the adversaries the protocol is protecting against, at least as much as external attackers. Design the security model to reflect this.
- Red-team every feature from the adversarial-misuse angle, not just the external-attacker angle.

**This vision doesn't live in a file in the codebase (yet) — it's the North Star that should animate every feature from recovery forward.**
