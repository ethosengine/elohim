The Elohim Protocol seeks to subsume Google, Facebook, and Amazon. That
means peer-to-peer must reach the Apple-mantra bar of **"it just works"**
for *everyone* — including grandma, the Canadian podcaster, and the church
family that just wants its website to stay up.

That's why we are deep on substrate work right now. Foundational trust is
the prerequisite. Once the substrate's trust signals are mastered, power
can extend safely **from below** (humans, households, collectives) to the
hubs (federation, doorways, network-scale projection) — never the other way
around. The whole point is to maintain the integrity of a *different* social
contract in a world of godlike AI technology, where the dominant
architectures are designed to extract from humans, not steward them.

**How to apply:** When designing any human-facing surface that mediates
trust (resilience badges, distribution readouts, recovery affordances,
stewardship transitions, recruit-help signals), evaluate it against:

1. **Does grandma get the right confidence ambiently?** If she has to think,
   read documentation, or interpret jargon to know her stuff is safe, the
   surface fails the "it just works" bar.
2. **Does it scale TO humans, not away from them?** (Pairs with the
   "intelligence revolution scales to humans" memory.) The surface has to
   become *more* legible at scale, not less.
3. **Could a hub re-capture this surface to extract value upward?** If yes,
   the design has the wrong direction of power. Flow has to be human →
   collective → hub, with hubs serving as projection / amplification only.
4. **Is the foundational trust signal load-bearing for the next layer?** If
   the surface is wrong, downstream features (recovery, REA-credit, qahal
   recruit-help, stewardship release) inherit the failure. We can't fix it
   later — these are the load-bearing primitives.

This frame upgrades the bar on every design decision in the imagodei /
shefa / lamad surfaces. "Good enough for an internal demo" is not good
enough; the standard is "credible to a grandmother whose family photos are
on the line."
