[steward of record: human:matthew] Operator, 2026-11-10, during the host-compatibility foundation spec
(`genesis/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-host-compatibility-foundation-design.md`, branch
`spec/host-compatibility-foundation`):

"In contrast to most other programmed systems, diversity must become the elohim protocol's strength.
The network itself should design for sense-respond-repair cheaply, more like an organism than a
monolith with centralized firewall and security rules." The mature picture they described: bespoke
variants attested at the edges; vulnerabilities discovered and repaired; repairs flowing quickly to
the devices that benefit, "like how antibodies update the immune system".

They then widened it: the mature target is a system built soup-to-nuts to keep damage cheap and
restoration robust, with intelligence at the intimate edges. "Exploits will happen"; the network
itself, not its developers, discovers them and informs "a private plural commons" through the acts
sense / respond / quarantine / observe / experiment / adapt / repair / restore / update / propagate.
This covers the whole brit/rakia/ark/berth/lvi dimension. A never-seen device should be able to
install a primitive version, develop its own variant, and climb the attestation and reach ladders
until it accelerates later devices like it. They compared it to forking a repo and patching: the
protocol "is closing the loop on the evolutionary development cycle itself, in a trustful,
attributable, and governed way". Specs, contracts and seams written today must be robust enough to
reach that. Written into the spec as §7.4 (immune loop) and §7.5 (unseen device).

And named the actor: "elohim carry the diversity, and are the 'white blood cells' of our immune
system." So the edge agent, with its person, is who senses, adapts and repairs; it is not a
central service. (My reading, in the spec's §7.4 "Who acts": it works within its steward's
standing and its changes are judged like any other variant.)

Same conversation, two related corrections of me:
- I said "a plugin layer built ahead of need is its own failure mode". They disagreed: with abundant
  AI-adaptable software, code is cheap, so a robust device-level, ontology-aware native layer may be
  the smarter move. The real distinction is **disposable vs durable** code. Heavy testing and
  attestation of native code is acceptable when it parallelizes across many nodes.
- Treat hardware diversity as an **ecological system**, not a design problem to flatten with a
  virtual machine.

**Why:** the protocol's resilience thesis is that variety limits blast radius and that trust is
earned at the edges; a central rule set or a single levelled runtime removes both.

**How to apply:** do not reach for YAGNI/cost-of-writing arguments against adaptability layers; ask
instead what is durable (contracts, conformance suites, attestations) and design that carefully.
Do not propose levelling devices, a universal VM as the answer to variety, or a central security
authority. When designing anything that distributes binaries or rules, name how a problem found on
one device is sensed, withdrawn, repaired and propagated to the devices it affects, evaluated
locally. My interpretation (not their words): a new conformance vector is the natural "antibody",
and findings need their own attestation so a false one cannot withdraw healthy variants.
