The relentless pressure on the epistemic integrity of the context we're designing to hold is not a hypothetical. Words that name relationships of power and accountability — *consent, community, stewardship, reach, trust, attestation, contribution, household, recovery, reconciliation* — are under constant capture pressure from the parties who would prefer easier definitions. "Consent" is one click-through away from meaning a TOS. "Community" is one rebrand away from meaning users-on-a-platform. "Stewardship" is one quarterly objective away from meaning ownership with extra steps. "Reach" is one growth-team away from meaning follower count. **The capture isn't always malicious; often it's just the natural compression of language under load, market framing, or regulator framing.** The result is the same: the word becomes hollow and any party can refill it.

**The canonical worked example: Douglas A. Foster, "Reclaiming Reconciliation," *Journal of Ecumenical Studies* 55:1 (Winter 2020).** Foster's argument: "racial reconciliation" has been corrupted into a mutual color-blind handshake — implying a prior conciliation that never existed, implying mutual guilt where guilt was unilateral, letting white people off the hook with celebrate-difference framings. The hollowing-out is not accidental — it's what happens to a word when the powerful party prefers a definition that costs them nothing. Foster refuses to abandon the word (abandonment concedes the corruption) and names two non-negotiables for reclaiming it:

1. **Truth-telling** — the whole structural story of how white supremacy was constructed and is sustained, drawing on DiAngelo's framing that *racism is a structure, not an event*. Words about systems require structural exposure, not anecdotal balance.
2. **Reparations** — material repair, not symbolic apology. "Reparations — by which I mean the full acceptance of our collective biography and its consequences — is the price we must pay to see ourselves squarely" (Coates, quoted in Foster).

Walter Wink, also in Foster: *"Neutrality in a situation of oppression always supports the status quo."* This is the connective tissue. The system that claims to hold the meaning of a word while declining the work the word demands is — by its silence — on the side that captured the word. ([[project_values_forward_disclosure_accountability]] is the same claim: neutrality IS a side; the protocol takes the side of accountability.)

Foster's method itself is the third teaching: he opens with disclosure (*"I am a sixty-something white male... I had no significant relationships with any person of color for the first four decades of my life. All of this has to be on the table."*). The argument is unintelligible without that frame. **Provenance is part of the claim, not metadata about it.** That's the same shape as content-derived CIDs and signed EPRs — every claim carries the provenance that lets it be weighed.

His concrete close is the strongest single demonstration: the Equal Justice Initiative's lynching documentation, the Abilene Remembrance Project, the soil collected from Grover Cleveland Everett's 1922 lynching site, the police chief's unscripted "I stand here convicted... I'm sorry," the great-granddaughter's embrace. Reparation in microcosm: *pointable, witnessed, material, named, connected back to a specific harm at a specific place by specific people.*

**Why this matters for the protocol:** Foster's two reclamation moves map onto two non-negotiable protocol primitives.

- **Truth-telling → provenance, chronicle, attestation integrity.** The protocol must hold true stories about itself and its participants, *including the uncomfortable ones*. The 2026-05-17 correction of the "zero stale references" claim in `chronicle/2026-05-14-first-memory-team-ceremony.md` is a microscopic instance of the same discipline. The chronicle is a story in the same graph as every other; falsehood there has the same weight as falsehood anywhere else.
- **Repair → reparations-shaped primitives.** A graph that *describes* a harm but can't be walked back to *make material amends* is a museum, not a substrate. Reach can be returned. Attestations can be revoked. Stewardship can be redistributed. Couplings can be honestly closed rather than ghosted. These are reparation-shaped operations, and they must be **pointable, witnessed, and material** — not narrated and not symbolic. (See [[project_consolidation_events_economic_feedback]] — merge emits shefa/qahal/mishpat/imagodei; reach is bidirectional. That bidirectional structure is what makes repair possible at all.)

**Why "pointable structure" is the only defense against linguistic capture:** Careful language doesn't survive pressure; values statements don't survive pressure; "we mean it sincerely" doesn't survive pressure. The defense has to be a substrate where *the word is anchored in structure that breaks visibly when the word drifts from the thing*. "Stewardship" cannot be quietly redefined when stewardship is an EPR with attestation, reach, revocation history, and counterparties who can witness the change. The word becomes pointable; the structure becomes auditable; capture becomes locatable. ([[project_graph_native_is_accountability_substrate]] and [[project_genesis_is_protocol_interpretability_layer]] both rest on this — pointable beats inferable; counsel beats oracle; alpha-to-omega.)

**Theological ordering (because Foster makes it explicit and the protocol carries this seriously):** "Humans are incapable of effecting Divine-human reconciliation" (Foster, paraphrasing Romans/2 Corinthians/Ephesians). Reconciliation is initiated by God, with humans entrusted with the *ministry* — the ongoing work. Maps to [[project_substrate_floor_elohim_ceiling]] and [[project_no_sovereignty_stewardship_over_ownership]]: substrate handles deterministic allocation, elohim adds discernment, humans hold the meaning. The protocol must NOT pretend to *be* the reconciliation; it can only be the ground on which the work is possible. A protocol that thinks it *is* the reconciliation is the church-leader-neutrality Wink condemned — pseudo-neutral, supporting whichever status quo it inadvertently encodes.

**How to apply:**

1. **Audit the vocabulary periodically.** For each load-bearing word (consent, community, stewardship, reach, trust, attestation, household, recovery, contribution, reconciliation), check: does the structural definition match the lived definition? Has the surface narrative drifted from the graph? Treat drift as substrate failure, not language failure.

2. **Provenance is part of the claim, never metadata.** Every assertion the protocol surfaces — including its own chronicles, agent outputs, and recommendations — must carry the disclosure of where it came from. Foster opens with his disclosure because the argument can't be weighed without it; EPRs carry their signatures for the same reason.

3. **Repair must be a first-class operation, not just description.** When designing any view, ask: *how does someone use this to make a wrong right?* If the answer is "they don't, they just see what happened," the view is half-built. Reach can be returned, attestations revoked, stewardship redistributed, couplings honestly closed. The graph must support those motions or it's a museum.

4. **Truth-telling discipline applies to ourselves first.** The chronicle layer is the inward face. False claims about our own process (the "zero stale references" line) are *exactly* the shape of capture-pressure failures we're designing to resist at scale. Audit per surface; don't trust inspection-of-canonical-sources ([[feedback_persona_rename_completeness_checklist]]).

5. **Refuse linguistic neutrality on the load-bearing words.** Spec and content writing should resist passive constructions that hide agents (Foster's example: "church leaders" without "white" before it). Name who did what to whom, and when. ([[feedback_no_hebrew_pillar_names_in_narrative]] is a related discipline — accessibility of meaning to non-specialists is part of the integrity.)

6. **Don't claim the protocol does the reconciliation.** It's substrate for the ministry; the work belongs to humans (and the elohim agents that hold accountable positions on their behalf, per [[project_graph_native_is_accountability_substrate]]). A protocol that presents itself as solving relationship is the same category-error as a church preaching reconciliation without committing to justice. The grandmother bar ([[project_subsume_g_f_a_via_it_just_works]]) includes this: the substrate has to be honest about what it is and isn't.
