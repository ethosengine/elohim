Two /deliver verdicts, weeks apart, in opposite directions, both honest:

- **2026-05-07** — `2026-05-07-feedback-dialogue-panel.sprint-result.md` honored **Delivered** despite a known framework-gap in the rendered surface. The framework gap was disclosed in the sprint result; the verdict was not blocked by it because the delivered slice was discrete and the gap was scoped outside the slice.
- **2026-05-14 bootstrap** — `/deliver` honored **deferred-substrate-missing** for a story whose feature file was absent. The slice could not be attested to, so the verdict refused to call it Delivered even though substantial code had moved.

The verdicts shape inverse: one honored delivery in the presence of a known gap; the other refused delivery in the absence of an attestable substrate. Together they triangulate /deliver's authority floor — it is anchored on **what can be attested**, not on **what feels done**.

**Why:** Authority floors are easy to claim and hard to prove. A single verdict in one direction is unfalsifiable. Paired verdicts in opposite directions, both honest, are the first falsifiability evidence — /deliver could in principle dishonor either floor (call the May 7 case deferred-on-framework-gap; call the 2026-05-14 case Delivered-on-vibes) and didn't. The discipline held in both directions.

**How to apply:**

- When evaluating an authority surface's discipline, look for **paired opposite-direction verdicts**. One-direction discipline is unfalsifiable.
- /deliver's attestation floor is now load-bearing: future verdicts can be cross-checked against these two precedents.
- Pairs with [[project_three_layer_truth_model]] (DHT=notary anchor) and [[feedback_story_delivery_status_axis]] (the lifecycle gradient /deliver judges within).
