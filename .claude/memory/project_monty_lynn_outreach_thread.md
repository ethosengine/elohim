Outreach email to **Dr. Monty Lynn** (Abilene Christian University professor; had Matthew in Business 101 freshman year). Matthew = ACU BBA Mgmt '10. Met in person at "the Bean" (campus coffee shop); this email is the promised "longer version" follow-up, sent ~2026-06-18 (mtime). Hook: with **Stargate** (the AI datacenter) going up near Abilene, "what do we do with all this AI?" is a live local question.

One email carrying **three audience-tailored pitches** (forward-whichever, no obligation):
- **Theology faculty** — formation has outrun belief; build infrastructure that forms toward flourishing not extraction. Names the deliberate theology of "Elohim" (grammatically plural; agents named for the *lesser* elohim of the divine council, never God — a refusal to mistake tool for God). Points at `confession.md`, `theology.md`, and the Foundations-of-Christian-Technology learning path (a Christian bridge from CHT's Foundations of Humane Technology).
- **Business-school leadership** — incentives, not procurement, are the real AI question; nothing rewards passing AI gains to labor. The bet: build the economy around what AI can't replace (judgment, care, stewardship, standing). **REA (Resource-Event-Agent) is an ISO standard**; the missing piece below enterprise scale is *friction* (equitable distribution costs more admin than extraction) — a **local AI narrator** closes that gap, rendering a household's ordinary story into rigorous REA events. Makes Mondragon-style co-ops / B-corps / stakeholder ventures compete with VC on its own ground; test = "a retiree would feel safe moving a position into it."
- **CS lab** — Holochain + Rust, content-addressed, local-first (laptop = full node); each data atom couples story + value (ValueFlows/REA) + governance. The buildable piece = the **Observer**: a local AI ambiently witnesses a home's unpaid work (cooked, cleaned, restocked, cared) and narrates it into the REA ledger as exchangeable value. Pitched to **ANU's School of Cybernetics** as a grad project; an ACU lab could own its own version. Site is deployed from the p2p substrate today via two federated doorways (doorway = thin web bridge serving a projected cache; the p2p dataplane is authoritative).

Throughline framing = Stafford Beer's 1973 "freedom machine," now achievable because frontier-scale models will run on home hardware (NVIDIA DGX Station) — intelligence meeting a free society's full variety without the surveillance it once required, and no central console to seize. Ask is small: forward, cross-pollinate, send feedback; nothing to buy.
