This is the honest counterpart to [[project_consilience_higher_order_sensemaking]] and [[project_mission_platform_for_collective_biography]]. Those entries describe the protocol's patience and dignity-restoration mission. This entry names the population for whom patience-and-dignity-toward-everyone *would be the wrong move*, and the friction-by-design that follows.

**The values-forward stance has lived costs.** [[project_values_forward_disclosure_accountability]] establishes that the protocol *takes a side* against accountability-resistance. That stance is not a vibe — it's structural. The lived experience of being on the side the protocol opposes is friction. Specifically:

- **Reach is earned at authoring, not granted by default** ([[project_social_reach_nervous_system]]). An actor whose content depends on viral propagation without provenance hits a wall the moment they enter the substrate.
- **Reach-gating returns {Allowed, Blocked, Pending} with reasons** ([[project_reach_gate_is_elohim_mediated_matchmaking]]). The gate is not neutral; it is opinionated about what should flow at what reach.
- **Trust is an efficiency signal** ([[project_trust_as_efficiency_signal]]). Trustworthy content costs less to distribute; *un*trustworthy content costs more, structurally. The asymmetry is economic, not just moral.
- **Provenance is non-optional** ([[project_epistemic_integrity_under_pressure]], [[project_graph_native_is_accountability_substrate]]). EPRs are signed; content-derived CIDs are notarized; the DHT is the notary layer. Actors who require anonymity-as-shield-from-consequence don't get it on this substrate.
- **Validation rules reject invalid state** — the protocol exercises bounded control. Coercive moves that classify as harm-externalization don't pass validation.

**The asymmetric care:** *patience for PEOPLE, friction for harmful IDEAS.* A person formed inside a maladaptive context can come to recognize their formation (the Foster gift named in [[project_consilience_higher_order_sensemaking]]) — and the protocol stays patient with that journey, offers bridges, surfaces healthier vantage points when receivable. **But the propagation of the maladaptive idea does not get the same patience.** That's where the substrate exerts itself, by design.

This asymmetry is the protocol's actual care for the same person *as a person*. Letting their maladaptive idea spread unimpeded would be cruelty — to those it would harm, AND to the person whose growth is foreclosed by being rewarded for the harm. Bounded reach on the harmful idea is what makes the patience on the person possible. Without the friction on the idea, the patience on the person becomes complicity with the harm. (Wink, from Foster: *"Neutrality in a situation of oppression always supports the status quo."*)

**The honest corollary about people who will reject the protocol fully.** For some actors, the distinction between person-and-idea won't be navigable. Specifically:

- Whose business model depends on weaponized attention, accountability-evasion, or extractive data capture.
- Whose social position depends on the maladaptive idea spreading unbounded.
- Whose continued operations require externalizing harm onto others who lack the substrate's protections.
- Whose comfort requires the absence of the friction the protocol applies.

These actors will encounter the protocol as hostile *and they will be right that it is hostile to what they are doing*. The protocol does not owe them comfort, does not pretend to be neutral toward them, and does not promise to grow into a platform that accommodates them. **The social contract is explicit, public, and refusable in advance.** Anyone who reads it and opposes it is opposing what the protocol is, not misunderstanding it. That's coherence with the stance, not a usability failure.

**The grandmother bar applies here, too — in reverse.** [[project_subsume_g_f_a_via_it_just_works]] establishes that the substrate has to be credible to a grandmother. The reverse-direction test is also true: **the substrate has to be honestly unwelcoming to those whose operations would harm that grandmother.** If a substrate is comfortable for both her and her predator, the substrate is failing her. The asymmetry of care is the asymmetry of the threat.

**How to apply:**

1. **When designing surfaces that bound reach, attestation, or visibility** — name the friction explicitly in the social contract. Don't hide it behind algorithmic opacity. The protocol's stance is values-forward; the friction surface should be values-forward too. Reach gates returning *{Blocked, with reasons}* is the right shape; *{Blocked, no explanation}* is the wrong shape (becomes the same opacity the protocol exists to refuse).

2. **When tempted to optimize a friction surface for "user happiness"** — check whose happiness. If smoothing the friction would let harmful-idea propagation accelerate, the friction is doing its job and should *not* be smoothed. (This is the inverse of [[feedback_less_pushy_notifications]] — that lesson is for friction *on the receiving end*; this lesson is for friction *on the originating end of harmful propagation*.)

3. **When the system surfaces resistance to a user's actions** — distinguish *person-resistance* (the user is hitting a recognition gap; offer bridges, patience, alternate framings) from *idea-resistance* (the propagation of this content is bounded by design; the friction is correct). The same UI affordance handling both produces incoherent UX. Mishpat / qahal / shefa surfaces should be able to communicate which kind of resistance is occurring.

4. **When public-facing copy describes the protocol** — say it. The protocol takes a side. The protocol applies friction to accountability-resistant actors. The protocol is not for everyone, and the dimensions on which it's not-for-everyone are public. Soft-pedaling this in the manifesto would be the same anti-pattern that [[project_epistemic_integrity_under_pressure]] names — hollowing-out of the values-forward stance through comfortable redefinition.

5. **When elohim agents act as counsel** — the counsel relationship is asymmetric. An elohim representing a contributor under attestation pressure works to ensure the person is treated fairly *AND* doesn't smuggle through the harm-propagation the friction is designed to bound. Counsel for the person is not opposition to the friction. [[project_elohim_as_counsel]] needs to be read alongside this entry to avoid that conflation.

6. **When evaluating "will people like this?"** — yes, some won't. That's data about the protocol's coherence with its stance, not data about a design flaw. Some discomfort is the correct response to friction-on-harm. The metric that matters is: *do the people the protocol exists for (those who have suffered shame and oppression — [[project_mission_platform_for_collective_biography]]) experience the substrate as the patient, dignifying, repair-capable ground it promises to be?* That is the test. Comfort of harm-propagating actors is not the test.
