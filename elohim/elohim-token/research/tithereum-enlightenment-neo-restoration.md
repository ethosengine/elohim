4. **Economic-dependency feedback corruption** — pastors trapped by congregations, media trapped by audiences. This is exactly what three-leg coupling prevents: economic capture of governance (shefa capturing qahal). Germany's public news infrastructure model (fund independence from sensational corruption) is the same pattern as protocol-funded elohim independence.

5. **"Tie" — the proposed solution** — a platform for passively binding 10% of capital (not income) to communities, with velocity-constrained reallocation (10%/20%/40%/80%/full over 5 years). This gradual unwinding is the structural mechanism that breaks economic-dependency feedback corruption — and maps directly to the elohim-token's commitment velocity constraints and ResponsibilityDemandParam.

## Original Document

### The Problem

Inequality is not linear.

Wages (active income) income growth is linear. Capital appreciation (compounding growth) and passive income are exponential.

Wealth vs. Income Inequality: two individuals might have the same Income (wages), but are vastly unequal due to existing wealth.

### Relativity, Individualism and Conflicts of Self-interest

Common perceptions of wealth and security are more often than not, irrational.

(Example: A family who makes upwards of $100,000/year income self-reporting that they are middle class, when the median household income has been approximately $53,000/year for a decade).

American society has an irrational understanding of wealth inequality.

### Regressive Feedback of Inequality

Generally speaking, taxes on wealth are "regressive" in that we tax ordinary income (wages) greater than that of passive income (capital gains).

The common sermons of tithing, or of giving expectations, mission fundraisers and the like, which rely on a focus of a tithe of income, will always fail to directly address the distortions of wealth and perspectives individuals experience with wealth, privilege, and power in a highly-unequal society.

Furthermore, a focus on equal giving participation in a culture trending towards greater inequality demands a greater responsibility to address and correct the disparity, than to continue a quiet ignorance to, or reinforcement of the disparity. Where it would be acceptable if culture were trending to be more equal, and only marginally acceptable if the trend was static.

Lastly, this is a divisive problem for those that find themselves in positions of surplus. They can argue that they give the most within the community, and also still give at a deficit relative to their general wealth and/or income or both. This paradox also extends into issues of toxic-charity:

- Where transactional expectations of servile behavior of less resourced members, and favor from institutions to the personal preferences to those that give the most.
- Conscientious altruism vs effective altruism, generating extremely inefficient, wasteful, and/or (at worst) directly harmful forms of charity. (Anecdote about volunteers weaving "Dignity Blankets" from discarded plastic shopping bags, broad criticisms of missionary tourism)

This undue transactional influence is highlighted by Jesus himself, in telling the story of the Parable of the Widow's Mites.

Lastly, this is a divisive problem for those that find themselves in positions of scarcity. They are expected to give, regardless of if they should give. The exacerbations of hardships from the dues expected by organized religion, and acutely predatory behavior of televangelists, is a public disgrace of organized religion. How regular contributions, giving campaigns, and special donations campaigns are communicated and executed, must be tempered and burdened with sensitivity to the needs of the body, and of the public's trust in our obedience to the use of those funds. To continue without this sensitivity is to make vulnerable members of scarcity to the perspective that they are being taken advantage of by people of faith, and invites public disdain for church practices which affect the witness of the church.

### Economic-Dependency Feedback Corruption Patterns of Religion and Media

At an Evangelical Pastors conference, private after-hours dinner among pastors describe the corruption of the pulpit as follows:

> "As a pastor, what am I supposed to do? I have one hour a week to deliver a sermon to the congregation. When my congregants leave the building, my audience has access to the church of Fox, with pastors Hannity, Limbaugh, and Tucker Carlson on a 24x7, and on-demand, and on the radio, minus my hour. If I am convicted to preach a sermon that expresses God's love for the immigrant, and emphasize the oppression of immigrants by the policies and politics — how do I witness to that without angering the congregation that they split, or put me and my family in hardship by being replaced with a preacher who won't offend them, or validate how they feel, even when in complete contrast to the weight of sound theology, scripture and interpretation?"

Many church-leaders wanting to do the right thing have found themselves trapped by their own congregations, forced to either avoid subjects, or approach them in such a manner as to avoid conviction or confrontation with congregations.

The difficulties and pressures scale with the size, complexity of the church, or even demand for growth. Think of the unquestionable financial success of the health and wealth gospel. Church-leaders are incentivized by their pay, position, and reputation, begin to primarily reflect back the strong feelings, influences, and positions on topics that affirm their congregations. To the detriment of spiritual character.

#### In Media

It should be noted that a similar phenomenon is identifiable in media and news.

When the news is selected by publishers to be run, stories that might be of high-value to the public interest, like city-council meetings and permit reporting, are under-reported, relative to sensational drama that might sell the most ad-revenue.

If tribal partisanship and click-bait draws the audience for news, an echo-chamber develops, similar to our reflective congregation, which skews public misperceptions of trends in public governance, public health, politics or crime, to serve the audience a true reflection of what they want — other than the objective perspectives, honesty, and truths they might not be as entertained to hear.

### Responses to Economic-Dependency Feedback Corruption Patterns

A possible solution would be to take a cue from high-quality, local, non-polarizing news in Germany. Germany's public news infrastructure was partially a product of post-WWII concerns that the propagandizing machine of the media before and during the war would re-establish itself to instigate a further conflict. To mitigate this, the Allied Forces in West Germany instituted strong public funding of independent local public news, at $135/yr spending per capita on Public News as public information infrastructure — one of the highest in the world. When independent news organizations are well-funded to provide quality news, without search for a profit, there is appropriately low competition from ad-driven sensationalism in German media.

This concept of economic-information infrastructure protecting institutions from sensational, populist corruption, is completely applicable to reforming the infrastructure of the church. Our preachers, often beholden to serve their congregations what they want to hear, need to largely have economic independence from the congregations they serve. How do you do that?

Some solutions to this issue could be considered:
- Traveling preacher circuits/associations
- Monastic Practices
- Tenure and Retirement programs
- Reasonable recommended term limits for preachers

### Suggested Interpretation of Tithes and Giving

**Parable: The Widow's Mite** — a woman gave mites, and Jesus highlights that it was a greater sacrifice for her because it represented all that she had. Being of greater value to God than those who had wealth and were able to give quantitatively large amounts.

**Interpretation:**

Tithings: Tithing is to give 10% of the production of one's fields. In an agrarian sense of wealth (ownership in the field itself). Workers who would have been paid fractional value of that production are receiving a wage, which is disassociated from ownership of that field.

In a modern sense, control of assets (cash, stocks, real estate, and other equities) better represents the "agrarian flocks or fields" that form the basis of a 10% tithe.

Wages are a modern invention, and are not directly based on an underlying asset store of value, other than the individual's intangible value in labor.

Thereby, basing tithes in the modern day on 10% of one's wages (income) is a modern distortion of the original intent of tithing.

### Proposed Solution — Tie

**App idea: "Tie"** — Passively support the people (institutions) you love by binding a tithe of tangible wealth to their use.

> *"Blest be the tie that binds*
> *our hearts in Christian love*
> *Blest be the fellowship of kindred hearts"*

**User Story:** As a user, I need a platform to passively collect and automatically distribute 10% of the capital I control to the communities and causes I am a part of.

**Example:** I control $100,000 in capital. My Tie must hold $10,000.

#### Rule 1: Harvest-Only Distribution

The fund retains a percentage (up to all gains) to cover inflation/losses, but disburses any gain or "harvest."

- Fund loses $1,000 → no funds are disbursed
- Fund gains $1,000 → $20 is retained for inflation, $80 is disbursed to institutions "Tied" in my account

#### Rule 2: Gradual Reallocation (Anti-Capture Velocity Constraint)

The institutions "tied" in my account cannot change allocation more than:
- Year 1: +/- 10%
- Year 2: +/- 20%
- Year 3: +/- 40%
- Year 4: +/- 80%
- Year 5: Full disbursement

**Example walkthrough** (assumes simple 10% annual ROI):

**Year 1 Begin:**
- $1,000 is allocated toward current church "A". I leave on the first day.
- By year end: $19 covers inflation, $70 is disbursed to church "A"
- Equity still "tied" to church "A" is now ~$916 (-10% reallocation) by year-end
- I join church "B" first of the year — through year end it gains any funds released by other institutions
- Amount tied to Church "B" will be ~$102 by year end

**Year 2 Begin:**
- $916 is still "tied" to church "A"
- By year end: $15 covers inflation, $60 is disbursed to church "A"
- Equity still tied to church "A" by year end is ~$750 (-20% reallocation)
- Church "B" ends the year with ~$371 and ~$20 is disbursed to church "B"

**Years 3-5:** Repeat for 40%, 80%, and at the beginning of year 5, whatever remains at church "A" is fully rebound to the user's current institutions.

#### Why This Matters (the anti-capture mechanism)

The gradual unwinding means:
1. **Institutions can't be punished instantly** for faithful action that displeases donors
2. **Donors can't weaponize withdrawal** — leaving is a slow process that gives institutions time to adjust
3. **New institutions earn trust gradually** — funding ramps up as relationship deepens
4. **The economic feedback loop is broken** — the pastor has 5 years of cushion, not 1 week
