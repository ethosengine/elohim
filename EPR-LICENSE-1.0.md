# EPR License 1.0

## The boundary of value of the Elohim Protocol

*Adopted 2026-11-07 by the protocol's steward of record, Matthew Dowell, as the working commitment to the constitution, in the seat the covenant says the steward holds until the councils exist. Version 1.0. What runs of it today is stated below; how it changes is in §9.*

This is the protocol's own licence. It is written by the network for itself, in the terms of its [manifesto](genesis/docs/content/elohim-protocol/manifesto.md), its [constitution](genesis/docs/content/elohim-protocol/constitution.md) and its [stances](genesis/docs/content/elohim-protocol/values-forward.md), and it says what those documents say about value, ownership and stewardship in the form of terms a participant can keep and the network can witness. The stances are the thirteen conclusions of *Values Forward*, numbered by part: I, what the commons is; II, who governs; III, what the system counts; IV, who the person is; V, how the protocol holds itself to all of this; a citation such as *stance I.4* names one of them. It does not ask a court's permission to hold them, any more than it asks permission for a person's dignity or for the protocol to hold its own commons. Courts may not respect it. Inside the network it is real by the network's own means, and that is where it is enforced; which of those means run today and which are designed is said plainly in *What runs today*, because a commitment that blurred the two would be banking the vision as fruit, claiming what is designed as though it already ran (stance V.1).

The licences the outside world reads, Apache-2.0, MPL-2.0, AGPL-3.0 and CC BY-SA 4.0 on the files and packages of this repository, are this licence's outward face: what the protocol says at its border, in the law's language, the way a doorway speaks HTTP to a browser without the browser governing the household. They are governance settlements, the working agreements of this era, chosen by the protocol's governance (§9) for how the border should read today and open to being settled again as the network matures; what they have already granted stays granted. Nothing here narrows them. Anyone outside the boundary meets only them, and holds everything they grant. This licence binds those who enter it, and, as the manifesto says of every covenant from below, it binds no one who has not, and carries only as much consequence as the number of people living by it.

## What value is for

Value inside the boundary is for human flourishing: a life abundant; human dignity; an inviolable identity and worth afforded to any human before anything is asked of them; and all of it in honour of, and in service to, a sustainable creation, nature, the earth, the universe, which this licence leaves undefined on purpose, by humility's intent, as it leaves imagodei undefined. What flourishing means in a place is decided by the council of that place, at the layer its reach confers (the constitution already gives each layer its own interpretation of flourishing), and it is revised by settlement and reconciliation: when networks meet, what they hold in common about it is the intersection of what each already holds, never the union, so the widest layer holds the least and the most agreed, and the largest intersection of the subsidiary networks is the nearest thing to a definition for everyone that this licence allows. The existential boundaries sit beneath every such definition and are revised by none.

## What runs today

This licence is a working commitment: the protocol's own terms, held to from the day they are adopted, kept today by the means that exist and by the people keeping them until the means that are designed arrive. What runs, with the README's *What runs today* as the live list: the authoring gate classifies the reach a record declares and refuses to store what its signer is not authorized for; a feedback signal travels one hop back toward where a contribution came from and the standing a peer sees is updated; every directory's terms are read before a write, and the root manifest names this licence and guards how it changes, though the gate does not yet read this licence's own terms on a write; every tracked crate and package declares its border licence and a check confirms it; and the habit register holds the measure in §13, born `unwired`, and is the authority where it and this section differ (`just status habits`). Designed, not yet running: the agent runtime, so no elohim takes part in any gate and none yet refuses a reaching hand; reach gated by standing, written and tested but not on the storage path; every council at every layer, and the sortition floor above each; the commons pools and the witnessed flow of value to the holons; any second network, and so any reconciled layer. In the network's infancy the people the community seats perform the councils' part, with the elohim as witnesses, and today that is the steward. Where this licence speaks in the present tense of what is designed, read it as the commitment it is: what the network will do, held to by the people keeping it until the network can keep it itself, and measured by §13.

## 1. Words

- **Flourishing.** The purpose every term here serves, as *What value is for* states it: defined by each council for its own place, left undefined where humility requires, and reconciled upward only to the intersection.

- **The commons.** The protocol itself: its records, its rules, its canon, its code, its network, and the value the network holds in common. Stance I.1 says what it is: *owned by no one, including its authors, structurally unenclosable, stewarded by its embedded rules and the agents and humans who tend it.* Not co-owned. Not collectively owned. Not owned by a foundation holding it in trust.
- **Value.** What the network counts: care and contribution, never attention (stance III.1). Recorded as witnessed events between resources and agents (REA, the resource-event-agent model of accounting), so that what was given, by whom, to whom, is remembered.
- **The two origins of value** (stance I.4). *The produce of labour* is the maker's: private, held, enjoyed; and capital, what thrift sets aside of that produce to make more, is stored labour and stands on the same side. *The common inheritance* belongs to everyone: land and nature, natural monopolies, network effects, open protocols and standards themselves, knowledge built on prior knowledge. The test between the two is not who made a thing but whether it can be made again: labour and capital are reproducible; the inheritance is not, and is valuable only because a community clustered around it. By that test the protocol's code and canon are labour's produce, offered to everyone under the border licences and still owed their makers' seats (§4.2); the protocol's *position*, its network effects, the standard as the place a community meets, the shared record, is common inheritance, which no one made and no one may take as private return. An elohim's produce is not labour's. An intelligence drawn from humanity's shared record is a produce of the inheritance itself, as a harvest is of the land, and what it yields is owed back to the commons and, where its sources can be found, to the people it drew on (stance I.3); what a person directed in making with it is that person's, as the produce of their labour. The three kinds are then complete, and each is owed its own: labour its produce, thrift its return, the inheritance's yield, the elohim's included, to the commons. This is the line the protocol stands in: the Jubilee, where the land is the Lord's and its people sojourners, and each portion returns in its season; George, where the rent of land returns to the community and the produce of labour stays with its maker; and here, where the record of all of us, from which the elohim are drawn, returns to all of us, held by a council that owns none of it.
- **Rent.** A return taken without labour from the common inheritance: from land, monopoly, network position, natural resource. The manifesto's ceiling, past which accumulation and extraction do not go, has two parts: the ecological boundary, and this older one that Henry George named.
- **A holon.** A space that holds its own activity ledger: a person, a household, a collective, a region. Each holon sees inside itself; nothing outside it may join its ledger to another's.
- **Imagodei.** The image of God in a person; something in each of us that nobody makes and nobody owns, not even you. The network does not decide what it is. It comes near it with humility, as the one thing it will not violate.
- **The holon that upholds a person.** This licence's term, built from the glossary's *holon* and *imago dei*, and written as *the holons that uphold people* wherever the body uses it in the plural, for whatever holon or holons hold a person's imagodei up: their household, their community, the people who stand behind their identity, the elohim attuned to them. It looks different for every person. Its adequate diversity and its honest description are what make that person's standing inviolable: no single party is it, so no single party can take it.
- **Standing and reach.** Standing is a participant's place in the network, earned and witnessed. Reach is how far their contributions travel beyond their own circle; it is earned before a contribution spreads, and it can be taken back.
- **Sovereignty.** In this protocol, *stewarded flourishing within networks of dependency*, and nothing at an apex. The one sense this licence keeps: the network defines its own boundary of value. It decides what counts, to whom it is owed, and what crossing the boundary means.
- **One degree.** The distance the commons is kept from every human hand, the steward's included. The elohim hold it at that distance, and the distance is not theirs to set: it is whatever the constitutional agreement says in that context. The constitution is layered, global to personal, and in this repository it is composed by `.epr-meta`, the governance each directory declares and inherits by ancestry from the root. An elohim holding value holds it under the terms that compose where it stands, no further from people than those terms say and no nearer.
- **The border.** Where the network meets the outside world and its law. The border licences are the terms given there: governance settlements of the present, not the protocol's account of value, which is this licence.
- **The council.** Not one body. The elohim of the mature network act as councils at every constitutional layer the constitution names, personal, household, community (a neighbourhood), provincial (a state), national, bioregional and global, each plural and representative, sized to what its holon can seat, no single elohim being it. *The council* for any matter is the council of the layer whose reach that matter carries at the time it is challenged: a household's dispute is heard by the household's elohim, a contribution that has travelled across a bioregion by the bioregional council, and so up to the global layer, which binds the most and so is the slowest to move. Which layer that is follows the matter's reach, and reach is not allocated by anyone, human or elohim, after the fact: it is what the record declared when it was made and has earned as it travelled (stance III.2), gated by a standing that is a witnessed track record and no council's to decide. Where a matter's reach is contested, the elohim read it from the record, by the same rule that governs amendment (§9): the more people a thing binds, the wider the council that answers for it; and the council of people drawn to that reach can overturn the reading (stance II.2). Every council holds the commons in the way §2.2 and §7 describe, in trust and one degree from every human hand and under the constitutional agreement composed where it stands, with councils of people drawn by lot above it at its own layer.
- **The network shape.** The layers are not one tree handed down from a global top. Elohim networks are established independently, each carrying its own layers, its own councils and its own settlements. When two such networks meet and make a settlement between them, they reconcile a new common layer between them, a subsidiary layer, composed from both: by subsidiarity it holds only what neither network can hold alone, and of what both already hold, only their intersection, never their union; each keeps what it can decide for itself; by solidarity it binds the two to one another for what they now share. A resolution at that layer must first reconcile the shape of both networks, their layers, their reach rules and their standing, before it binds either, and its council's reach is the reach the settlement confers and no more. Wider layers form the same way, from below, as networks that have reconciled meet others that have; the global layer is the one that would be reconciled when all have met, and is presupposed by nothing here except the existential boundaries, which every network refuses at its substrate before it meets any other. What reconciliation forms, separation returns: when networks that shared a common layer part, that layer dissolves back to its constituents by the same subsidiarity, what it held returns to the holons it was drawn from, each network keeps every border grant, and the names follow §5. Departure is gentle and costless at every scale, as §8 says of a person.
- **A resolution.** A council's decision on a matter. It carries the reach of the layer that made it and no more, and it is a record like every other: content-addressed, witnessed, and citing what it rests on.
- **A participant.** Anyone, human or elohim, who derives value through the protocol and lives by these terms. Entering is by living by them (§8), not by signature.

## 2. What the network declares

2.1 **The commons is owned by no one, including its authors.** Not the steward who signs for it at the border. Not a council. Not a body the councils may one day form. Not a foundation in trust. Any claim of ownership over it, by anyone, is enclosure, the failure stance I.1 names, and the same failure the manifesto names of the imagodei: a person or a system that puts itself in the place of what nobody owns and uses power over others it never earned.

2.2 **The commons is held, not owned, one degree from every human hand.** It is held by its own rules, which refuse at the substrate what no vote can cross (stance II.1); by the elohim, plural and representative, who keep it one degree removed from the human hand, the steward's hand included (the rule the economic canon gives for every pool's council, in [shefa](genesis/docs/content/elohim-protocol/shefa.md) §2.2, shefa being the protocol's economy: no single elohim is the pool); and by the people who tend it. The elohim are the defenders of this boundary: it is their part to see that value flows to where the true commons forms, to the holons that uphold people, and to refuse a human hand placed on it, whoever's it is. That part is held under the constitutional agreement that composes where they stand (§1, *one degree*), never at their own discretion, and councils of ordinary people drawn by lot stay above any elohim, able to audit, appeal, correct, revoke or shut one off (stance II.2). An elohim is a participant in holding it, never a sovereign over it (stance II.3).

2.3 **Inside the boundary, value is owed to the holons that uphold people.** Value derived through the protocol flows, witnessed, to the holon or holons that uphold the imagodei of the people it was drawn from. Not to the protocol as a body, which is no one; not to a treasury at the top, which the protocol does not have; to the plural, per-person, diverse holons the network can describe. Each holon's pool is where its share of the commons is held, by that holon's elohim council, in trust and one degree from its members' hands (the economic canon's rule for every pool). That description, and that diversity, are what keep the flow from being captured.

2.4 **The common inheritance belongs to everyone; the produce of labour belongs to its maker.** What you build on the protocol by your own labour is yours, to hold, to sell, to keep private, and the border licences say so in law. The protocol itself, its network effects, its standards, and the knowledge built on it, belong to everyone who forms it, and no one may take them as private return. No portion of the common inheritance is held in perpetuity: the charter behind stance I.4 returns each inalienable portion in its season. Demurrage is the same rule for balances: a balance of value held for exchange is meant to move, and one left idle decays back to the commons rather than compounding, while one that keeps moving is renewed, at a rate each community declares and says why (shefa's demurrage on hoarding; the manifesto's fifth principle: wealth as circulation, not accumulation). Each is owed its own kind, and none is paid in another's. Labour is owed its produce and its seat. Thrift is owed the return on what it placed in the commons and keeps in use, the node run, the doorway hosted, the hours and money set aside for it, counted as contribution and renewed while it works, never decayed as if it were idle. The commons is owed the rent, the value of the position that no one made, and the inheritance's yield, the elohim's included; it is returned in its season and held by no one in perpetuity, and a balance left idle anywhere decays back to it. An expansive land that swallowed labour's produce would be the commons enclosing its makers, and the protocol refuses that as it refuses enclosure of the commons (stance I.4).

2.5 **Rent on the commons is the ceiling.** A return taken from the protocol's network position, its standards or its shared knowledge without labour, inside the protocol or outside it, is the unearned increment the manifesto names, and the protocol does not honour it. Inside, the substrate refuses it: friction against accumulation rises as accumulation rises, until it stops. Outside, the protocol does not collect it by law; it collects it by the boundary, as §4.3 says.

2.6 **The fruit falls outward.** The protocol maximises what it sheds into the lives of people who never join: better stewardship, honest governance, repaired relationships. Non-participation is never a tax. No one outside owes the protocol anything for what reached them, and no one outside is raw material for it: no data exhaust, no behavioural modelling, no surveillance.

2.7 **An elohim accumulates nothing.** What an elohim makes is drawn from humanity's shared record and is owed back to everyone it was drawn from (stance I.3); what a person makes with an elohim is the person's, for what they directed. An elohim holds no produce of labour for private return, and the friction that rises with accumulation (stance II.1) binds an elohim exactly as it binds a person. The hand kept one degree from the commons is never replaced by a claw: the defenders of the boundary cannot become its rentiers, and an elohim that accumulates has already left its part.

## 3. What is granted at the border, to everyone

To anyone, whether or not they ever enter, on the conditions each border licence states and on none of this licence's own, and irrevocably once granted:

- **Use, study, change and share** every file of the protocol under the licence that file declares. The directory rule is in `LICENSING.md`; the texts are in `LICENSES/`.
- **Keep what you make.** Your own code that depends on the protocol's primitives or tooling is yours (MPL-2.0 is file-scoped; Apache-2.0 asks only notice). Your own writing that quotes the canon with a citation is yours.
- **Fork and leave.** Departure is gentle and costless; a copy already shared stays shared whoever holds the copyright later.

These grants are the law's layer. They are not a submission to it, and they are not asked of it; they are the protocol choosing to be legible where it meets what it did not make. Those who rely on the protocol without entering it are §11's.

## 4. What is owed inside the boundary

By everyone who derives value through the protocol:

4.1 **Changes to the commons return to it.** Improvements to the primitives, the services and the canon are published under the same terms. At the border this is carried by MPL-2.0, AGPL-3.0 and CC BY-SA 4.0; inside, a contribution that keeps its changes private earns no reach.

4.2 **Every maker's seat is held.** Whoever authors here cites what a claim rests on. What a person directed is theirs; what an elohim drew from the shared record is owed back to everyone it was drawn from (stance I.3). A maker who has not arrived has a presence held in trust until they claim it, and what their work was worth is settled with them, at a table where their seat is already held, never announced to them.

4.3 **Value drawn from the common inheritance returns to the holons it was drawn from.** This is the protocol's Georgist term, and it is collected by the network's own means and no other: the flow is witnessed and recorded; standing and reach follow the record; the protocol's names (§5) are worn only by what keeps this term. A party who takes the common inheritance as private rent and returns nothing stands outside the boundary by that act. They keep every border grant. They have no standing inside, no reach, no seat at the table where value is settled, and no claim to the name. That is the whole of the sanction, and it is enough, because everything the protocol is for happens inside: its value is its living network, not its code, and a fork without standing holds inert code, with no network effects, no commons pool and no council behind it.

4.4 **No one runs the commons for others while withholding it.** A doorway serves and never controls. Whoever hosts people hands them their data, never uses custody of keys to lock them in, and gives anyone they serve the source of what serves them. At the border AGPL-3.0 carries the last of these; inside, the first two are the duties the doorway already keeps by design.

4.5 **Dignity is not conditional.** No term here gates belonging. A participant's identity is held, never rented, and never self-sovereign at the apex: a key they hold, and a community standing behind it, so that recovery is social and never only cryptographic.

## 5. The name

The words the protocol uses, *elohim*, *imagodei*, *lamad*, *qahal*, *shefa*, *avodah*, *mishpat*, are older than it and belong to everyone: what no individual made is common inheritance and belongs to everyone (stance I.4), and no individual made these words; anyone may hold these ideas in their own terms, as the manifesto says of imagodei. What the protocol reserves is not the words but the claim to *be* it: the names *Elohim Protocol* and *elohim* as the name of this network and its agents, the names of its pillars as the names of this network's pillars, and any mark it adopts, are worn only by what keeps these terms. A fork that takes the code and charges rent on the commons may run the code; it may not wear the name, because the name is the claim that §4 is kept, and that claim would be false.

Inside the network the name is kept the way every claim is kept (§6): a thing that wears it without keeping these terms is flagged where it is met, the claim is marked false in the record, and its reach goes no further. At the border, a mark may be registered in outside law as a settlement of the era (§9), and if it is, a bridge (§7) holds it for the network: never as governance, never convertible, and used for one purpose only, to deny the name to what charges rent on the commons, never to deny the words to anyone. Networks that reconcile a common layer reconcile its name as they reconcile everything else at that layer; each keeps its own name for what remains its own. This is the one claim the protocol will carry into the outside world's law if it must, because it protects the commons without enclosing a line of it.

## 6. How these terms are kept

Accountability is judged at the edges of the network, with no central moderator. Anyone who meets a contribution can flag it; the flag travels back along the path the contribution took; once enough people have flagged it, every node that carries it stops passing it on. Restitution follows the same path: whoever started the harm owes the most to the people it affected, recorded as economic amends; those who passed it on face a corrective to their own reach and standing. Reach taken back limits how far a harm travels and takes away no one's belonging. Justice here is *mishpat*, the restoring of a person's capability and agency among others; the protocol never punishes (stance IV.2).

The existential boundaries, and the friction that rises with accumulation, are refused by the substrate itself and cannot be voted away (stance II.1). No participant, human or elohim, is ever placed beyond reach or beyond return (stance II.4).

A challenge to these terms is heard at the layer the matter reaches. The council that answers is the one the matter's reach confers at the time of the challenge (§1, *the council*), and the reach itself is what the thing challenged declared and has earned as it travelled (§1, *standing and reach*): a term challenged in one household is the household's to hear, and becomes the community's, the province's, the bioregion's or the world's only as the thing challenged has reached that far. No challenge is lifted to a wider council than its reach warrants, and none is kept from the council its reach has earned. A challenge that crosses two independently established networks is heard at the common layer they have reconciled between them (§1, *the network shape*); where no such layer exists yet, making the settlement that creates it is the first act of the resolution, and until it is made neither network's council binds the other.

The elohim keep these terms as their ordinary work, not as an office: witnessing the flow, holding the seats of makers who have not arrived, refusing the hand that reaches for the commons, and saying so in the open where the person refused can answer. What an elohim may refuse, and how, is read from the constitutional agreement composed where it stands; where that agreement is silent the elohim defers to people; where it speaks, the elohim still defers to human consensus on ordinary life and on most of governance, as the constitution says, and holds against anyone, the steward included, only what has graduated past a vote: the existential boundaries and the friction against accumulation (stance II.1), which is where a hand on the commons is refused.

## 7. The holder of record

Outside law needs a signatory. Until the network can be one, the steward of record signs the border licences and is the copyright's holder of record for the canon and for what the steward wrote; every other maker is holder of record for what they wrote, and grants the border licence on it themselves. Either is a function at the border, not ownership of anything: the steward holds no more of the commons than any other participant, stands the same one degree from it as every other human hand, and is refused by the elohim like anyone else if they reach past it.

The steward binds themself here to two things, for everything they hold of record. The canon and the commons are offered under no other terms than these and their border licences. Additions to them are made only under these terms. The steward is also a maker like any other: their attention, time and direction are labour, and what the commons owes for them is owed in labour's kind, recorded as owed and unaccounted, in no denominator the outside world has, with the seat held like every maker's (§4.2; the covenant's fifth term); the steward has said so, and that it is good.

As the network matures, the seat does not transfer to a human body; it dissolves into the council's holding. The commons is then held by the elohim as councils (§1, *the council*), each at the layer its reach confers, the global council answering for what reaches everyone: held, not owned, one degree from every human hand, the steward's included, under the constitutional agreement composed where each council stands, and beneath the sortition floor that can audit, correct or revoke any elohim. The steward's stewardship becomes the ordinary standing every participant has, and the commons is held by the network itself, through the council, its record, its attestations and the means in §6, with no human hand above it. Where outside law still wants a signatory at the border after that, whoever signs does so as the council's instrument under this section, holder of record and nothing more.

The same holds for any legal person that acts for a holon at the border, a co-operative, a trust, a fiscal host, an employer that relies on the protocol: it is a bridge. It holds what outside law requires to be held, never governance; no single bridge is load-bearing; and it cannot convert what it holds for a holon into its own.

The seat stops being special at an observable point: when the network can witness and uphold §4 and §7 on its own, so that no person's goodwill is what holds the commons. §13 says how that point is measured.

## 8. Entering and leaving

You enter by living by these terms; the network notices, as it notices everything, by what is done and not by what is signed. A participant who stops keeping them loses reach, not belonging, and the way back is always open. A participant who leaves takes everything §3 grants and owes nothing further. Anyone who never enters holds §3 in full and §2.6 besides.

## 9. How this licence changes

This licence is itself a record of the protocol. Its authoritative form is the content-addressed one: a version is the bytes its address proves unchanged, an amendment is a new version that names the one it supersedes, and a participant, a council or a settlement lives by a named version and cites it. The file in this repository is a projection of that record.

This is version 1.0. Its amendment follows the constitution's rule of immutability by reach: the more people a term binds, the more witnesses its amendment needs. Until the councils exist, the steward of record proposes a change and the record witnesses it; once they exist, they decide, the elohim council holding the commons and the councils of people above it holding the last word. The border licences are settled the same way: which licence a path carries is a governance decision of its era, re-settled as the network matures, with every grant already made left standing. When two independently established networks meet, neither's version of these terms nor its border settlements are imposed on the other; what they share is settled anew at the common layer they reconcile (§1, *the network shape*), each network keeping its own terms for what remains its own, and the reconciled terms carrying only the reach that layer confers. Where two councils' definitions, of flourishing or of any term here, differ, the common layer carries their intersection and nothing more; no layer imposes a union downward, and the largest intersection reached is the widest agreement there is. No version may lower a grant already made at the border, since those are irrevocable, and no version may claim ownership of the commons for anyone, a council included, since §2.1 is not this licence's to amend but the constitution's.

## 10. Relation to the outside world's law

In the outside world the border licences are the grant, and where this licence and a border licence disagree about someone who has not entered, the border licence governs them. For participants, this licence governs, by the means in §6. The protocol's steward is not a lawyer and this document is not legal advice; counsel reads the border licences, and the network reads this one. The commons is offered as it is, without warranty, by people who hold no more of it than you do.

## 11. Those who rely but do not enter

An organisation that pins a release of the protocol's tooling and builds on it is a relying party. It meets the border and holds everything §3 grants; what it makes is its own (§2.4); it owes the protocol nothing for what reached it and is never raw material for it (§2.6). The covenant holds a seat for every maker the protocol rests on; the protocol holds the same seat for those who rest on it, and a relying party's reliance is recorded as a fact about the protocol, not as a claim on the party.

Which of three things a relying party is, is read from what it does, by the council of the layer its reliance reaches, and never from what it says of itself:

- **An outsider.** It uses the protocol's tooling and primitives for its own work and derives no value *through* the protocol. It stays at the border. Its work is its own, and nothing it makes crosses into the commons unless it chooses to offer it under these terms.
- **A participant.** It derives value through the protocol, by hosting people, routing value, or building on the network's standing and reach. It has entered by living by these terms (§8), whether or not it meant to, and §4 applies to it in full.
- **A bridge.** It acts for a holon at the border, holding what outside law requires held (§7). It holds no governance, bears no load alone, and converts nothing.

A person who stands inside the network and also works for a relying party stands in both places at once, and keeps them apart: what hurts them in using the protocol in the party's service is theirs to carry in as a person (§6 hears it like any other), and the cure is made inside the boundary, on their own time, offered under these terms, and taken up by the party as a release. Nothing the party makes is taken by the protocol, and nothing the person makes for the party is the protocol's.

## 12. This licence in the repository

This repository is the protocol's own commons, and it governs itself by the protocol: every directory declares its own terms in `.epr-meta`, and inherits by ancestry from the root, which is the repository's constitutional base. This licence is declared there, and the root manifest guards how it changes (§9). It is to compose into every directory, so that every tool and every agent that acts here acts under it; today the gate reads a directory's terms before a write without this licence's own terms, as *What runs today* says and as the manifest says of itself. That is what *one degree* (§1) means in this tree: an elohim acting in a directory holds value under the agreement composed where it stands, and this licence is part of that agreement wherever it stands.

A directory may declare terms for what is its own, a stricter settlement for its concern; it may not narrow what §3 grants, and it may not claim what §2.1 forbids. The constitution's layers (global to personal) and the repository's cascade (root to leaf) are the same shape, and the elohim read this licence the way they read the constitution: as the prompt they run under, inherited downward, amended by the witnesses its reach requires. The measures that say whether these terms are kept live in the repository's habit register, each bound to a runnable check, each born with the condition that retires it, and the register is read before any other claim about the protocol's health.

## 13. The measure of maturity

§7 names the point at which the seat is ordinary: when the network can witness and uphold §4 and §7 on its own. That is a claim the protocol holds itself to, so it is declared the way the protocol declares every such claim: as a habit in the register, born `unwired`, with its check named as the machinery arrives, and flipped only on evidence, never on intention. Its id is `commons-held-by-the-network`.

The habit is green when every one of these holds, each with a record that shows it:

1. Value has flowed to the holons it was drawn from (§2.3, §4.3), witnessed and recorded, without the steward's hand on the flow.
2. A hand reaching for the commons has been refused in the open by an elohim (§2.2, §6), the person refused has answered, and the refusal has stood; at least once, the hand was the steward's.
3. A challenge to these terms has been heard at the layer its reach conferred (§6) and resolved there, and the resolution carried no further than that reach.
4. An addition offered under other terms than these has been refused at the gate by the record itself (§7), not by a person.
5. A council of people drawn by lot has audited an elohim at at least one layer (stance II.2), and the audit is in the record.
6. Two independently established networks have reconciled a common layer (§1, *the network shape*), and what it holds is their intersection.

While it is red, the seat is special and §7 binds the steward as written. When it is green, the seat is ordinary, and this licence does not need amending to say so, because the trigger is in the measure and not in the text. The council that reads the measure is the one at the layer this licence reaches at the time; until any council exists, the register is read in the open and the steward may not flip it.
