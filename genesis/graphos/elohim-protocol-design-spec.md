# Elohim Protocol — Brand Design Specification

**Version:** 0.1 · Draft
**Last updated:** April 2026

---

## 1. Design Philosophy

**"Infrastructure for the table, not the tower."**

The Elohim Protocol exists to make cooperative economics tangible, local, and relational. Its visual identity must communicate that this is not a fintech product, not a crypto speculation tool, and not a Silicon Valley disruption play. It is a *commons* — digital infrastructure that serves the household, the neighborhood, the congregation, the cooperative.

The design philosophy draws from **communitarian solarpunk**: a future where technology is warm, present, and subordinate to human relationship. Like the world of Chobani's *Dear Alice*, the aesthetic should feel like something your grandmother would recognize as good even if she doesn't understand the technology underneath. The tech is *furniture*, not *spectacle*.

### Core Design Principles

1. **Hospitality over onboarding.** Every interface should feel like being welcomed to a table, not enrolled in a platform. The metaphor is the shared meal, not the dashboard.

2. **Legibility over cleverness.** Cooperative economics is already conceptually demanding. The design must reduce cognitive load, not add to it. Nothing should require a tooltip to understand.

3. **Rooted, not rootless.** The visual language should evoke place, soil, season, and craft — not the placeless minimalism of SaaS products. This is software that belongs somewhere.

4. **Provisional infrastructure.** Following Terra Nil's insight: the protocol is scaffolding for human cooperation, not a monument to itself. The technology should feel like it could be composted — that if the community outgrew it, the relationships would remain.

5. **Sabbath rhythms.** The design should communicate that rest, limits, and enough-ness are features, not bugs. No growth metrics on the dashboard. No engagement optimization. No infinite scroll.

## 2. The Constellation Mark

### Description

The primary logo is a human figure rendered as a **constellation** — nodes (stars) connected by thin lines, arms extended in an open, welcoming posture. The word "ELOHIM" appears below in clean uppercase, with "PROTOCOL" beneath it in a lighter weight.

### What the Mark Does

The constellation figure is not decorative. Every element carries conceptual load:

- **Nodes as agents.** Each star/dot is an autonomous participant — a household, a person, a cooperative. This directly maps to Holochain's agent-centric architecture, where there is no central server, only peers.

- **Lines as relationships.** The connections between nodes are not wires or data pipes — they are *bonds*. In REA accounting terms, each line represents a flow of resources, commitments, or obligations between agents. The graph IS the economy.

- **Human form as body.** The constellation resolves into a recognizable human shape. The network is not an abstraction — it is a body. This echoes Paul's "body of Christ" metaphor (1 Cor 12) and the Hebrew plural-unity of *Elohim* itself: many members, one form. The protocol is shaped like a person because it exists to serve persons.

- **Open posture as hospitality.** Arms extended, chest open. This is not a defensive or proprietary posture. It is an invitation. The figure faces the viewer as a host faces a guest.

- **Stars as covenant.** Genesis 15:5 — "Look toward heaven, and number the stars, if you are able to number them... So shall your offspring be." The constellation mark roots the project in the Abrahamic promise of a people too numerous to count, connected not by platform but by covenant.

### Logo Usage

- **Primary mark:** Constellation figure + "ELOHIM PROTOCOL" wordmark, stacked vertically.
- **Icon mark:** Constellation figure alone, for app icons, favicons, and compact contexts.
- **Wordmark only:** "ELOHIM PROTOCOL" in the brand typeface, for inline text contexts where the figure would be too small to read.

### Clear Space and Sizing

- Minimum clear space around the full mark: the height of the "E" in ELOHIM on all sides.
- Minimum icon size: 32px (below this, the constellation lines become illegible).
- The constellation lines should never be thinner than 1px at rendered size.

### Color Contexts

- **On dark backgrounds (primary):** White or Starlight (`#E8E4D9`) nodes and lines on deep dark (`#0F1A12` or `#1A1A2E`).
- **On light backgrounds:** Vineyard (`#2D5F3B`) or Hearthstone (`#6B6157`) nodes and lines on Linen (`#F5F0E8`).
- **Monochrome:** Single-color rendering is acceptable. The mark should be legible in pure black, pure white, or any single brand color.

### What the Mark Must Never Be

- Rendered with a glow, lens flare, or "tech" visual treatment. It is a constellation, not a hologram.
- Animated to spin, pulse, or "load." If animated at all, the stars should appear one by one, as if being traced by hand — a drawing gesture, not a computation.
- Enclosed in a circle, shield, or badge shape. The figure stands open, not contained.
- Combined with blockchain/crypto visual language (chains, blocks, coins, locks).

## 3. Brand Voice

### Tone

Warm, direct, second-person, unhurried. The voice of a neighbor explaining something at the fence line, not a founder pitching to investors.

### Register

**Prophetic-practical.** The Elohim Protocol carries theological weight (Kingdom economics, jubilee, mutual aid as worship), but the voice should never be preachy. It should sound like someone who has read both the prophets and their local zoning code — grounded conviction expressed through practical action.

### Vocabulary Inclinations

| Instead of... | Use... | Why |
|---------------|--------|-----|
| User | Household | The unit of participation is relational, not individual |
| Network | Neighbors | Proximity and obligation, not abstract connectivity |
| Transaction | Provision | Exchange is embedded in care, not extraction |
| Platform | Commons | Shared stewardship, not rented access |
| Growth | Enough | Sabbath economics rejects infinite accumulation |
| Ownership | Stewardship | Temporary custodianship, not permanent possession |
| Terms of service | Covenant | Mutual obligation with weight and memory |
| Onboarding | Welcome | Hospitality, not enrollment |
| Engagement | Participation | Presence by choice, not by design pattern |
| Scale | Spread | Organic growth like seed, not mechanical replication |
| Sovereignty | Agency | Self-determination within community, not autonomous dominion. Sovereignty implies a king; agency implies a person in relationship. |

### Vocabulary Avoidance

- "Disruption," "leverage," "optimize," "monetize," "unlock"
- Crypto/web3 jargon (even though Holochain is the substrate — the substrate is infrastructure, not identity)
- Corporate-pastoral tone ("we're on a journey together," "our community")
- Any language that centers the technology over the relationships it serves

### Sample Brand Sentences

> "The Elohim Protocol doesn't have users. It has households, and those households have neighbors."

> "You don't need to understand Holochain to share bread. You just need to know who's at the table."

> "This isn't a platform. Platforms extract. This is a commons — it only works if we tend it together."

> "Every node in the constellation is a person. Every line is a promise kept."

## 4. Color System

The palette bridges two worlds: the warm, agricultural, golden-hour tones of communitarian solarpunk (*Dear Alice*), and the dark-sky constellation space where the logo lives. The system needs to work in both registers — the sunlit interface and the starlit mark.

### Primary Palette

| Token | Name | Hex | Role |
|-------|------|-----|------|
| `--el-green-deep` | **Vineyard** | `#2D5F3B` | Primary brand color. Headers, primary actions, trust. The color of a working vineyard, not a manicured lawn. |
| `--el-green-light` | **New Growth** | `#7FB069` | Secondary actions, success states, confirmation. Spring growth, not neon. |
| `--el-amber` | **Harvest Gold** | `#D4A03E` | Accent and warmth. Highlights, active states, the golden-hour glow of *Dear Alice*. |
| `--el-clay` | **Terracotta** | `#B8664F` | Earthy grounding. Warm alerts, emphasis, handmade craft. |
| `--el-cream` | **Linen** | `#F5F0E8` | Primary light background. Breathing space. The color of unbleached cloth, not sterile white. |
| `--el-stone` | **Hearthstone** | `#6B6157` | Body text, quiet UI chrome. Warm gray with brown undertone — never cool/blue gray. |

### Secondary / Semantic Palette

| Token | Name | Hex | Role |
|-------|------|-----|------|
| `--el-sky` | **Morning** | `#7BAFCB` | Informational states, water/flow metaphors, links. |
| `--el-plum` | **Sabbath** | `#6E4B6B` | Rest states, reflective moments, "enough" indicators. |
| `--el-starlight` | **Starlight** | `#E8E4D9` | Constellation lines on dark backgrounds. Warm white, never blue-white. |
| `--el-night` | **Deep Sky** | `#0F1A12` | Dark mode background, logo primary background. Forest-dark, not void-dark. |
| `--el-night-alt` | **Indigo Night** | `#1A1A2E` | Alternative dark. Slightly cooler for contrast with warm starlight lines. |

### Palette Rules

1. **No pure black, no pure white.** `#000000` and `#FFFFFF` are banned. All darks carry a green or brown undertone (rooted in earth). All lights carry warmth (rooted in linen, not screens).

2. **The golden-hour rule.** In any composition, at least one element should carry `--el-amber` warmth. If a screen feels cold or clinical, it needs more Harvest Gold.

3. **Dark mode is constellation mode.** When the interface inverts to dark, it enters the visual register of the logo — starlight on deep sky. Nodes and key elements glow with `--el-starlight` or `--el-amber`. The metaphor shifts from "working in the garden" to "reading the stars."

4. **Color as moral signal.** Growth/health states use the green spectrum. Sabbath/rest/pause states use `--el-plum`. Warning states use `--el-clay` (earthy caution, never aggressive red). There is no "danger red" in this system — urgency is communicated through terracotta warmth and clear language, not alarm colors.

## 5. Typography

### Design Intent

The type system must feel **crafted and literate** without being precious. It should evoke a well-set book, a hand-lettered sign at a farm stand, or a community bulletin board — not a SaaS dashboard or a crypto whitepaper.

### Recommended Type Stack

| Role | Font | Fallback | Notes |
|------|------|----------|-------|
| **Display / Headlines** | **Fraunces** | Georgia, serif | An "Old Style" variable font with optical size axis, soft serif warmth, and visible personality. Has a "wonky" axis that adds handmade character. Evokes craft, warmth, and theological seriousness without stiffness. |
| **Body / Reading** | **Source Serif 4** | Georgia, serif | Highly legible at small sizes, warm without being casual. Good for longer-form content (documentation, covenant text, learning modules). |
| **UI / Interface** | **DM Sans** | system-ui, sans-serif | Clean, geometric, friendly sans-serif for buttons, labels, navigation, and data. Warm enough to not feel clinical. |
| **Monospace / Code** | **JetBrains Mono** | monospace | For technical documentation, protocol specs, Holochain references. Developer-facing only. |
| **Logo Wordmark** | Custom or **Outfit** | sans-serif | Clean, wide-set uppercase with generous letter-spacing. The wordmark should feel architectural — spaced like letters carved in stone, not printed on a screen. |

### Type Rules

1. **Serif-first for reading.** Any content that a person will actually *read* (not scan) should be set in serif. The protocol is making an implicit argument: this is literature, not content. This is covenant, not copy.

2. **Sans-serif for doing.** Buttons, navigation, form labels, status indicators — anything action-oriented uses sans-serif. The shift from serif to sans signals the shift from reflection to action.

3. **Headlines earn their weight.** Fraunces at display sizes (28px+) with the "wonky" axis engaged slightly. This gives headlines a hand-set, almost woodblock quality that resists the flatness of screen typography.

4. **Generous line height.** Body text at 1.6–1.75 line height. The text should breathe. Reading should feel unhurried. This is a Sabbath principle applied to typography.

5. **No ALL CAPS in body copy.** The wordmark uses uppercase. Nothing else should. Uppercase in running text reads as shouting, which violates the hospitality principle. Small caps are acceptable for abbreviations (REA, DNS, etc.).

## 6. Constellation Design Language

The constellation mark isn't just a logo — it's a **design system primitive**. The visual language of nodes-and-lines should permeate the interface at every level, creating a coherent visual grammar that connects the brand mark to the product experience.

### Nodes

- Nodes are **circles**, never squares or diamonds. Circles are the most hospitable shape — no sharp edges, no hierarchy implied by orientation.
- Node size indicates **significance in context**, not rank. A household node is large on the household screen, small on the neighborhood screen. No node is inherently "bigger" than another.
- Nodes have a subtle **inner glow** or soft fill, never a hard border. They are stars, not buttons.
- Active/selected nodes use `--el-amber`. Resting nodes use `--el-starlight` (dark mode) or `--el-stone` (light mode).

### Lines (Edges)

- Lines connecting nodes are **thin, slightly translucent**, and optionally curved. They should feel like threads or tendons, not wires or pipes.
- Line weight communicates **relationship strength or recency**, not hierarchy. A thicker line means "these agents interact frequently" or "this obligation is active," not "this connection is more important."
- Lines should never cross if avoidable. When they must cross, one passes behind the other with reduced opacity. The visual should feel like a well-drawn constellation chart, not a tangled wiring diagram.

### The Constellation in UI

- **Neighborhood views** render the local graph as a constellation — each household as a node, each mutual-aid relationship as a line. This is the primary "map" of the commons.
- **Resource flow** can be visualized as light traveling along constellation lines — a gentle pulse from one node to another when provision occurs. Not a flashy animation — more like watching a firefly move through a field.
- **Empty states** (no neighbors connected yet, no provisions made) show a single node — the user's household — with faint dotted lines radiating outward, suggesting connections waiting to be made. The visual promise: "you are the first star. Others are coming."
- **Loading states** trace the constellation figure from the logo, one node at a time, as if drawing it by hand. Never a spinner. Never a progress bar.

## 7. Illustration & Imagery

### Style

If and when illustration is used, it should draw from the *Dear Alice* / Studio Ghibli tradition: **hand-painted, warm, human-scale**, with visible brushwork or texture. Characters (if any) should be diverse and nondescript enough to feel universal — Chobani's *Dear Alice* handled this well with ambiguously brown, simply-drawn characters that read as "anyone's family."

### Subject Matter

Illustrations should depict:
- **People in proximity.** Sharing meals, tending gardens, working together, teaching children. The fundamental unit is always relational — two or more people in a scene, never a lone user at a screen.
- **Technology as background.** If tech appears, it is integrated into the scene the way a well-designed tool integrates into a workshop — present, useful, not the subject. A device on a kitchen table, not a glowing interface floating in space.
- **Seasons and place.** Illustrations should have weather, light direction, and a sense of time of day. Nothing should float in a void. Solarpunk worlds have dirt, rain, golden light, and nightfall.
- **Food.** Bread, produce, shared meals. The protocol is about provision. The visual language should make you hungry.

### Photography (if used)

- Documentary style, not stock photography. Real people in real places.
- Warm color grading consistent with the palette — golden hour bias.
- Never: people staring at screens, isolated individuals in modern offices, handshake photos, or anything that reads as "corporate."

### What imagery must never include

- Abstract geometric patterns (reads as crypto/fintech)
- Blockchain visual metaphors (chains, blocks, mining)
- Globe/earth imagery (reads as grandiose; this protocol is local-first)
- Lone hero figures (this is a commons, not an entrepreneurship story)

## 8. Iconography

### Style

Icons should feel **hand-drawn or woodcut-inspired** — a consistent stroke weight with slightly rounded terminals and occasional imperfection. Not pixel-perfect geometric icons. The reference is a well-carved stamp or a careful pen illustration, not a Figma component library.

### Stroke Specifications

- Stroke weight: 1.5–2px at 24px icon size
- Corners: Rounded (2px radius), never sharp
- Style: Outlined, not filled (filled variants for selected/active states only)
- Color: `--el-stone` default, `--el-green-deep` active, `--el-amber` highlighted

### Core Icon Concepts

| Concept | Icon Metaphor | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Household | House with smoke/warmth rising | Not a user avatar — a dwelling |
| Neighbor | Two houses with a line between | Constellation language at icon scale |
| Provision | Open hand with grain/bread | Giving, not transacting |
| Request | Cupped hands, empty | Receiving without shame |
| Commitment | Two hands clasped or a knot | Mutual obligation |
| Learning (Lamad) | Open book with a branching path | Knowledge as graph, not linear curriculum |
| Sabbath / Rest | Crescent moon or candle | Cessation as sacred |
| Commons | Table seen from above | The shared surface |
| Stewardship | Seedling in soil | Tending, not owning |

## 9. Motion & Interaction

### Principles

1. **Organic, never mechanical.** All motion should use easing curves that feel natural — ease-in-out with slight overshoot for arrivals, gentle deceleration for departures. Nothing should move linearly. Nothing should snap.

2. **Drawing, not loading.** Transitions between states should feel like a hand drawing the next frame, not a computer rendering it. The constellation loading animation (tracing nodes one by one) is the template for all motion in the system.

3. **Celebration without spectacle.** When a provision is made or a commitment fulfilled, the feedback should be warm and brief — a soft pulse of `--el-amber` through the relevant constellation line, a gentle bloom on the receiving node. Not confetti. Not a medal. Not a streak counter.

4. **Stillness is a feature.** Screens that have no new information should be *still*. No ambient animations, no pulsing dots, no "you have no notifications" nudges. Stillness communicates Sabbath — nothing happening is a perfectly acceptable state.

### Specific Transitions

- **Screen transitions:** Soft crossfade (200–300ms), never slide or push. Screens don't compete for space — they yield to each other.
- **Node appearance:** Fade in with subtle scale (0.8 → 1.0, 250ms, ease-out). Stars emerging.
- **Line drawing:** SVG path animation, 400–600ms per line segment, ease-in-out. Constellation connections should feel traced, not rendered.
- **Provision pulse:** A single soft wave of `--el-amber` opacity traveling along a constellation line from giver to receiver, 800ms, then fade. Once. Not looping.

## 10. Layout & Spatial Composition

### Grid System

- **12-column grid** for desktop layouts, collapsing to 4 columns on mobile.
- **Generous margins.** Minimum 48px horizontal margins on desktop, 24px on mobile. The interface should feel like a well-margined book page, not a data-dense dashboard.
- **Vertical rhythm:** All spacing in multiples of 8px, with a preference for larger increments (24px, 32px, 48px) over tight spacing. Air is a design material.

### Card & Container Treatment

- Containers use a subtle warm shadow (`0 2px 8px rgba(107, 97, 87, 0.08)`) rather than hard borders. Elements float slightly above the linen background, like paper on a table.
- Border radius: 8px for cards, 4px for inputs and small elements. Rounded enough to feel soft, not so rounded as to feel bubbly.
- No hard divider lines between content sections. Use whitespace to separate. If a line is absolutely necessary, use `--el-starlight` at 40% opacity — a whisper, not a wall.

### Information Density

- **Low by default.** The protocol resists the impulse to show everything at once. Each screen should answer one question: "What is in front of me right now?" Not "What is the state of the entire system?"
- **Progressive disclosure** over information architecture. Details emerge as the person asks for them, not as the system decides to display them.
- **No dashboards.** The word "dashboard" implies a vehicle moving at speed that requires constant monitoring. The Elohim Protocol is a garden, not a vehicle. The home screen should feel like looking out a window, not monitoring instruments.

## 11. Sound (if applicable)

If the protocol ever includes audio feedback:

- **Acoustic, not synthesized.** A wooden knock, a bell tone, a breath. Not a digital chime or a notification ping.
- **Sparse.** Sound should be rare enough that when it occurs, it carries meaning. If everything makes a sound, nothing does.
- **Reference:** The quiet environmental audio of Terra Nil — wind, water, birdsong — not the composed score of a mobile game.

## 12. Anti-Patterns — What Elohim Protocol Must Never Look Like

| Anti-Pattern | Why It's Wrong | What To Do Instead |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Crypto/DeFi aesthetic (dark mode + neon + angular type) | Signals speculation, exclusivity, and complexity | Warm darks, organic type, constellation language |
| SaaS dashboard (metrics, KPIs, engagement graphs) | Centers system health over human flourishing | Constellation views, provision stories, Sabbath stillness |
| Social media patterns (feeds, likes, follower counts) | Drives comparison, extraction, and addiction | No feeds. No counts. No streaks. |
| Corporate nonprofit (stock photos, blue gradients, "impact" language) | Signals institutional distance from actual community | Documentary imagery, direct voice, household-scale language |
| Gamification (badges, points, levels, leaderboards) | Commodifies participation and creates hierarchy | Celebration without ranking. Participation is its own meaning. |
| Material Design / iOS defaults | Reads as generic app, not as a specific commons | Custom components following the constellation design language |

## 13. Lamad (Reference Implementation) — Design Notes

Lamad is the first application built on the Elohim Protocol — a learning graph platform ("the LMS Khan never bothered to give us"). Its design should be a **living demonstration** of the full brand spec, with additional considerations:

### Learning-Specific Patterns

- **Knowledge graph as constellation.** The learner's progress through a topic graph should render as a personal constellation — nodes lighting up as concepts are understood, lines connecting related knowledge. This is gamification through *beauty*, not through points.
- **Attribution as connection.** In the REA backbone, every piece of contributed content traces back to its contributor. Visually, this should feel like constellation lines connecting the learner to the people who made the knowledge — not a credits page, but visible relational threads.
- **No linear progress bars.** Learning is a graph, not a line. Progress is represented by constellation density — how many nodes are lit, how richly connected they are — not by a percentage fill.
- **Forking as remix.** When a community forks a learning path, the visual should show the new constellation branching from the original — a new cluster of stars emerging from an existing one. Genealogy, not competition.
