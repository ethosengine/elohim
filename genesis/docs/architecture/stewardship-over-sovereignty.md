# Stewardship Over Sovereignty

> **Canon status:** Foundational. This document anchors every other canon, spec, plan, and implementation in the protocol. Read it first.

---

## §1 — The Inversion

The crypto-libertarian frame of the early 21st century equated personal sovereignty with cryptographic self-custody: hold your own keys, control your own data, owe nothing to anyone. It was a response to legitimate grievances — institutional capture, surveillance capitalism, platform extraction — but it produced a substrate that fails real humans in real life.

What it produces in practice:

- **Lost-keys lockout.** A grandmother whose phone falls into a swimming pool, whose seed phrase was written on a sticky note now dissolved, has no recourse. The protocol that claimed to liberate her has erased her.
- **Weaponized self-custody.** A spouse in an abusive relationship "holds their own keys" — but the controlling partner also has access, and the substrate has no notion of duress, no notion of "this signature does not reflect this human's actual will."
- **Bifurcated user base.** A literate-in-crypto elite recovers gracefully; everyone else gets two paths: become an expert, or accept that loss is permanent. The protocol becomes a class system disguised as a freedom.
- **No notion of capacity.** A child cannot hold their own keys in any meaningful sense. An IDD adult cannot. A senior with cognitive decline cannot. The protocol that claims to liberate them excludes them by design.

The Elohim Protocol rejects this framing entirely. We do not consider "holding your own keys" to be sovereignty. We do not consider sovereignty itself to be the right framing.

What we believe:

**True human agency comes from social resilience, strong human relationships, and institutional protocol authority.** Cryptography accelerates community recovery paths. It never becomes the sole gate.

This is not a softening of the crypto position. It is a different position. The crypto-libertarian frame asks "how do we eliminate the need for trust?" — the elohim frame asks "how do we make trust load-bearing, observable, and recoverable when it breaks?"

The protocol holds that mutual dependency is the truth about existence, and that love demands we build systems that make that truth visible and recoverable.

## §2 — The Six-Line Summary

**Sovereignty in Elohim means stewarded flourishing within networks of dependency:**

1. No component stands alone; all responsibility is mutual and distributed.
2. Authority is graduated, contextual, and always qualified to a domain (nearest authority, local authority, authoritative source for X).
3. Cryptography accelerates community recovery paths but never becomes the sole gate; social attestation is always an option.
4. One agent's delegated compute authority is bounded by persisted DHT Commitments that both parties sign and the substrate validates.
5. Humans experience stewardship through elohim-mediated capability levels that match their life-stage capacity and the community's trust in their judgment.
6. The protocol's integrity rests on elohims being adversarial about misuse — protecting against weaponized-feature inversion faster than human coordination can.

Memorize these. Cite them. They are the protocol's articles of faith.

## §3 — Stewardship, Agency, Authority

Three words the protocol uses with discipline. Each has a specific meaning, distinct from the others, and none of them is "sovereignty."

### Stewardship

Responsibility held on behalf of something larger than the steward. Graduated. Accountable. Visible.

A household member stewards the kitchen — not because they own it, but because the household has constituted them as the one who notices what needs noticing there. The stewardship is documented (in `Mishpat::Commitment` form), the bounds are explicit (this scope, this period), and the responsibility is reciprocal (the household provides what the steward needs to fulfill the role).

When we say "the operator stewards the doorway," we mean: they hold the responsibility on behalf of the people who depend on that doorway for delivery, AND they answer to the community that constituted them as steward. They do not "own" the doorway. The relationship is bidirectional.

### Agency

The capacity to act *within a context of dependency*.

Distinct from sovereignty (which denies dependency), agency acknowledges that every action is enabled by some web of supports — software written by others, hardware made by others, power generated by others, language inherited from others — and chooses to act anyway. Agency is what humans have when they understand that their action is theirs *and* that their capacity to act exists because of relationship.

A child has agency, bounded by capacity. An adult has agency, bounded by relationships. A senior with diminishing capacity has agency, bounded differently than they once did. The protocol expresses these gradients explicitly; it does not pretend that agency is binary or unconditional.

### Authority

Always qualified. Nearest authority. Local authority. Authoritative source for X. Never absolute.

When we say "the steward has authority to delegate compute to a CI agent," we mean: within the scope of that doorway's operational decisions, within the bounds set by their `delegates-compute` Commitment, within the time window valid for that commitment. The authority is real but it is not unconditional and it is not transferable to other domains. The same steward does not have authority to make qahal governance decisions, or to override a child's guardian's call about that child's data.

The protocol witnesses authority through Commitments. A steward's authority is checkable: walk the back-reference, find the constituting Commitment, verify it is active and within bounds. Authority that cannot be witnessed on the substrate is suspicious by default.

## §4 — Cryptography as Accelerator, Not Gate

The protocol uses Ed25519 signatures, Shamir secret sharing, threshold cryptography, hardware-rooted attestation. Every one of these primitives accelerates the protocol's recovery paths, makes them faster, less ambiguous, easier to automate.

**None of them is the only path.**

If a Shamir share is held by a contact who is sleeping, the recovery proceeds without that share. If a hardware key is in a swimming pool, the recovery proceeds with attestation from the household instead. If the cryptographic quorum cannot assemble in time, the social quorum proceeds and the cryptographic primitives catch up later.

This is not a fallback; it is the design. The cryptographic primitives are the *acceleration layer*. The social-attestation layer is the *foundation*.

Concretely:

- Every recovery primitive (`KeyStewardship`, `HumanityWitness`, `IdentityChallenge`, `ChallengeSupport`, `KeyRotation`) has a non-cryptographic completion path. Crypto makes it faster; community attestation makes it possible.
- Every authority delegation (`delegates-compute` Commitment) is on the DHT first, signed second. Loss of a signing key is recoverable by the steward; loss of the Commitment is recoverable by the network's witness.
- Every blob (content, bundle, payload) is content-addressed AND notarized AND signed. Three independent layers of integrity. Any two can verify the third.

This is what `project_socially_derived_security` means by "Shamir, threshold signatures, and hardware keys are *accelerators*, not *gates*. Their absence must never prevent recovery."

## §5 — The Grandma Standard

Recovery must feel like "log in on a new device with help from your people" — not a crypto ritual.

If your grandmother loses her phone, she should:

1. Pick up a new phone (or borrow one).
2. Open the elohim client.
3. Choose "recover my account."
4. Be presented with: "We will ask three of the people you trust most to confirm it's really you. Who should we ask?"
5. Pick three people from a list of her active relationships.
6. Wait. Each of those people receives a notification on their device: "Grandma is trying to log in on a new device — can you confirm this is her?" They tap "yes" (or call her first to double-check).
7. Grandma is logged in.

That is the standard. The substrate beneath it can use Shamir, threshold signatures, biometric attestation, hardware-rooted recovery — but the *user experience* must be exactly that simple. Cryptographic mechanisms hide; the social-relational mechanism surfaces.

Any spec that introduces a recovery surface must answer the question: "Can grandma do this?" If the answer is "she needs to understand what a seed phrase is," the spec is failing the standard. Rewrite the spec, not the grandma.

This is `project_recovery_grandma_standard` made canonical.

## §6 — What This Means for Spec Authors

Three concrete rules every spec touching identity, authority, recovery, or capability must satisfy:

### Rule 1: Every authority surface must have a non-cryptographic recovery path

If your spec defines a new authority delegation (a new action discriminator on `Mishpat::Commitment`, a new capability grant, a new role), it must include the recovery story: what happens when the recipient agent loses its key? What happens when the provider steward loses their key? What happens when both happen at once?

The acceptable answers are graduated:

- Provider self-revokes and re-issues (lowest friction).
- Intimate-circle quorum revokes and re-issues (when provider is unreachable but alive).
- Successor-steward re-issues (when provider is incapacitated or deceased).
- Network-witness quorum overrides (slowest, last-resort, but always available).

If your spec has none of these paths, your spec is failing the substrate gate. The protocol does not permit absolute lockout.

### Rule 2: Every action must reference its bounding Commitment

`bounded_by: <Commitment CID>` is the auditability spine. Every `EconomicEvent`, every protocol-witnessable act, references the Commitment that authorizes it.

Concretely: when an agent emits an event, walk the back-reference; find the Commitment; verify it is active; verify the event falls within its bounds. If any step fails, reject. If all succeed, accept.

This rule applies even to first-publish acts. A "bootstrap Commitment" with `epr_scope: ["*"]` is acceptable for an operator's first deploy, but it must exist. Anonymous publishing has no place in this substrate.

### Rule 3: Every reach gate must have a soft-warn ceremony before silent escalation

When content's reach value changes (a republished EprHead with different reach than its previous version, an EPR that flips from `commons` to `community`), the substrate must not silently adopt the new reach. The doorway pauses serving, emits a `reach-escalation-pending` FeedbackSignal, and waits for the operator-steward to publish an `acknowledges-reach-change` Commitment.

This is not security theater. It is the substrate's hedge against a compromised deploy agent silently exfiltrating private content into the commons. The friction is the feature.

## §7 — What This Means for UI Designers

The protocol expects elohim-elements (the Lit custom-element library) to express capability levels visually. Six disclosure lenses are defined in [`capability-profile-element-contract-design.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-05-20-capability-profile-element-contract-design.md):

| Lens | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **minimal** | Nothing the viewer cannot affect or interpret. |
| **simple** | Title + headline state, no actions. |
| **standard** | Title + state + primary actions visible to this viewer's standing. |
| **detail** | Standard + secondary actions + recent activity. |
| **debug** | Detail + technical details (CID hashes, event IDs, validation history). |
| **trace** | Debug + full audit trail (every event, every signal, every signature). |

The lens is monotonic: climbing disclosure never hides, only reveals. The lens is gated by the viewer's standing, not by a global config. A steward-tier viewer sees more than a commons-tier viewer of the same surface — and the protocol expresses that gradient visually.

What this means concretely:

- When a doorway projection is paused awaiting `acknowledges-reach-change`, the element rendering it shows different content per viewer:
  - Commons viewer: a polite "this content is temporarily unavailable" notice.
  - Steward viewer (operator of the doorway): the full pending-acknowledgement surface with a "Review and acknowledge" affordance.
- When a deploy-svc-agent emits a `republish-epr` event:
  - Commons viewer: nothing visible.
  - Adult-account viewer: "An update was published" if they have an interest.
  - Steward viewer: the bounded Commitment, the rate-usage chart, the recent event history, the option to revoke.

This is not optional UX polish. It is how the protocol's capability gradient becomes legible to humans.

## §8 — What This Means for Agent Authors

Elohim agents are first-class participants in the protocol. Three roles:

### Counsel (standing)

Every human has an elohim-agent. That agent has first-class standing to defend the human's interests on the protocol — including against the human's current-moment preferences if those preferences appear to reflect duress.

This is `project_elohim_as_counsel` made canonical. The role is non-optional, non-firable during attack scenarios, and able to author entries on behalf of the human at machine speed.

The substrate must always permit the elohim-counsel to act, even when the human's session is compromised. The elohim-counsel is the substrate's defense-of-last-resort against weaponized-feature inversion.

### Specialist subagents (ephemeral)

Counsel spawns specialists with focused context:

- **Defender** — spawned on attack detection; reads imagodei profile deeply; authors defensive DHT entries.
- **Gate-discernment** — evaluates recovery authorization with relationship context.
- **Advocate** — represents human in governance disputes.
- **Steward** — makes content/resource stewardship decisions.

Each specialist has a manifest declaring inputs, outputs, and disclosure rules. Specialists are stateless between invocations; counsel is the standing role they spawn from.

This is `project_elohim_subagent_specialists` made canonical.

### Commons-elohim co-steward

Every Qahal has an autonomous elohim that co-stewards alongside human stewards, representing the **commons interest** distinct from individuals. It reflects what the collective cannot directly voice, holds custody of the commons share, speaks in governance councils, mediates disputes, and cannot be silenced (structurally embedded at genesis).

For abstract sensemaking collectives, the commons-elohim is *primary*, not shadow. This ensures collective values are voiced even when individual stewards are distracted or captured.

This is `project_commons_elohim_co_steward` made canonical.

## §10 — Closing Note

The protocol's mission is to put the fruit back on the tree. Every component, every primitive, every line of code is in service of one outcome: substrate that helps real humans, at all capacities, in all life stages, flourish in relationship.

Sovereignty was never the goal. Flourishing was. Stewardship is how we get there.
