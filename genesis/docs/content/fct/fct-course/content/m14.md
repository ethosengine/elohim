## Teaching

### Introduction: Salt, Light, and Doers

We stand where faith intersects the immense power of modern technology, and the course's final word is Jesus' oldest job description: "You are the salt of the earth... You are the light of the world" (Matthew 5:13-16). Our faith compels us not to retreat from the digital realm but to engage it with wisdom, courage, and love — in cyberspace, in the algorithms shaping experience, in the technologies remaking the world. And the standard is James 1:22: "Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says." Understanding was never the destination.

### Scripture Engagement

**Matthew 5:13-16 — the anchor.** Salt and light are two distinct modes of witness, and the tech world needs both. Salt works *dissolved and hidden* — preserving and seasoning from inside the dish: the Christian engineer whose presence quietly changes what a team will and won't ship. Light works *visible and raised* — the lamp on its stand, the city on a hill: the community whose alternative practices can be seen from a distance. Note that the city is corporate; the witness Jesus describes is not only individual integrity but a *visible common life* — which is why this course kept insisting on church practice, not just personal habits. And the warning is real: salt can lose its saltiness. Assimilated Christians preserve nothing.

**Matthew 25:14-30 — the talents.** The master entrusts staggering capital and leaves. The servant condemned is not the one who lost money but the one who *buried* it out of fear. We have been entrusted with god-like capabilities — and burying them (retreat, tech-abstinence as identity, cynical disengagement) is the one strategy Jesus explicitly condemns. Venture the talents.

**Acts 1:8 — the deployment pattern.** "You will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth" — witness in expanding circles. For digital disciples: your congregation, your neighborhood, your industry, the world. Start where you are; the circles widen on their own schedule.

**Jeremiah 29:7 — building in Babylon.** "Seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile... for in its welfare you will find your welfare." We did not choose the platforms of our exile, but the exiles' instructions stand: build houses, plant gardens, seek the city's peace. Digital Babylon is not evacuated; it is gardened.

### Story: The Ark and the Redwoods

In the days of Noah, the earth was corrupt and filled with violence — and God's response was not abandonment but a commission: build. Noah's obedience amid ridicule mirrors our calling in tech: we build not arks of gopher wood but digital spaces and practices that preserve human dignity, truth, and life through the flood — a flood, in our day, of information, misinformation, and technology amplifying our worst. The ark was never just for Noah's family; it preserved life for the whole world, as our work is for all humanity and not only the church. The waters receded; Noah planted a vineyard in the new world; the dove's olive branch promised beginnings; the rainbow sealed a covenant of renewal. FHT ends with the same image in a different key: redwood seeds germinate after fire. Crises open the canopy. Every small win now is a seed in the ground — and like Noah, we build in faith for a world we may not fully see, knowing the labor is not in vain.

## Discussion & Activity

*(Written new for v2 — this cell was empty in v1.)* Four rounds, run as a commissioning workshop:

1. **Tell your story (testimony round).** FHT's first culture-change step, in the church's oldest form. Each participant gives a two-minute testimony: where did this course change what you believe, and what you do, about technology? Practice it here — you will tell it again outside.
2. **Mind the Gap, revisited.** Retrieve your Module 4 notes. What does the world need? Who are you at your core? Has the gap between your values and your life narrowed over these twelve days? Name what moved it — and the next adjustment.
3. **Collaborators and small wins.** Map your people: who in your congregation, workplace, or family shares this vision? Choose one *small win* achievable within a month that would demonstrate impact — and name the person you'll pursue it with.
4. **Champion the new success.** In one sentence, write the definition of success you will now advocate for in your context — your team's, your family's, your church's. Read them aloud; these are the seeds.

## Application — The Expanding Circles (Acts 1:8)

The usual three lanes widen into sending circles for this module:

- **The individual:** Conduct a digital-discipleship audit of your own habits and skills; offer your tech skills to your church (website, digital literacy for seniors, media help); commit to continuous learning at the faith-and-technology intersection; keep a regular digital Sabbath as witness and rest; engage online interfaith dialogue on tech ethics with a Christ-centered voice.
- **Our church:** Establish a Technology Stewardship team; map the congregation's tech skills and match them to needs; draft a church-wide ethical technology policy grounded in biblical principle and humane-tech practice; pair tech-savvy youth with elders for two-way mentoring; develop a digital missions strategy; and commit to open-sourcing ministry resources for the wider Body.
- **The ecumenical Body:** Convene or join Faith & Technology summits across traditions; build shared cross-denominational resource platforms; form united advocacy for ethical tech policy — data privacy, AI ethics, digital equality — as one Christian voice; launch a collaborative Christian innovation lab; join interfaith tech-ethics councils; coordinate global digital-missions efforts; and run ecumenical digital-literacy programs serving underprivileged communities first. What we do well must be shared with the full Body of Christ.

## Conclusion

We began in lament; we end at a threshold. Carry the vision of technology aligned with Kingdom values; be salt and light whether you ship code, raise children, or shepherd a congregation; stay connected to the community formed here; cultivate digital discipleship as ongoing habit; watch for the redwood openings that crises create; and remain grounded in prayer and Scripture, letting God renew your mind faster than the feeds can shape it. "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord" (Colossians 3:23-24). Go forth as digital disciples — ready to act, equipped to serve, prepared to lead.

*Closing prayer:* Heavenly Father, thank you for the wisdom you have granted through this journey. Give us courage to act boldly, humility to serve selflessly, and discernment for the landscape ahead. May our work in technology always point to you. In Jesus' name, Amen.
