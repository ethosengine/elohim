**Movement IV — Rebuilding Common Life · Day 9**

*Arc: from private discernment → to communal truth-practices and unity in the Body.*

*Anchor text: John 17:20-23 · Supporting: 1 Corinthians 1:10; Ecclesiastes 4:9-12; Hebrews 10:24-25 · Story: Trustville and Skeptica*

Module 10 named the distortions; this module does the rebuilding. Recognizing bent glass is a private skill — but truth, biblically, is a *communal* achievement: we never think alone, and we were never meant to. The people who follow the one who called himself the Truth ought to be the world's most practiced community of shared sensemaking. This module takes up that vocation under a working title the course now adopts: **Kingdom Developers**.

## Opening — That They May Be One

Read Jesus' prayer in John 17:20-23 — for those "who will believe in me through their message," that is, for us: "that all of them may be one... so that the world may believe that you have sent me." Notice what unity is *for* in Jesus' economy: it is the church's evidential infrastructure — the sign by which the world evaluates the message. Fragmentation is not just painful; it is counter-witness. Pray: *Father, your Son prayed for our oneness with his last hours. Forgive us for treating unity as optional and division as content. Make us one, that the world may believe.*

## Humane Tech Concepts in View

This module pairs with FHT's *Creating Shared Understanding, Part 2 — Rebuilding*:

- **The three-part repair:** curtail the causes of distortion (limit weaponized virality and microtargeting, add platform liability, realign business models); address the ongoing crises (rapid response to misinformation, blackout periods at critical moments, harms made transparent); and heal toxic mental habits (mutual understanding practiced as a learnable skill).
- **Practical interventions:** viral friction that slows inflammatory spread; algorithm adjustments that shrink perception gaps; empathy-building features; small, facilitated conversations across divides; and the living proof-of-concept of open digital democracies such as Taiwan's civic-tech initiatives.
- **The perception gap:** partisans dramatically misestimate what the other side actually believes — and the gap widens with certain media diets. Shared understanding is partly a measurement problem: we are fighting caricatures.
- **Minimally Viable Truth and Wikipedia's lesson:** collaborative truth-seeking sustains public trust through transparency, adaptability, and humility about bias — a working epistemic commons at internet scale.

## Teaching

### Introduction: Curtail, Address, Heal

Speaking "the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15) in a hyper-connected world is an ecosystem project, and the repair runs on three fronts at once. *Curtail the causes*: confront the attention race at its roots — advocate limits on weaponized virality and microtargeting, liability for platforms profiting from division, and business models aligned with flourishing. *Address the crises*: the current ecosystem wounds people daily, and the Body binds wounds — rapid-response to misinformation in our own communities, pastoral care for those harmed online, advocacy for the marginalized who absorb disproportionate abuse. *Heal the habits*: attend to the renewal of minds (Romans 12:2) — disciplines of discernment, charitable interpretation, empathetic listening, and digital fasting that reset what the feed has conditioned. None of the three substitutes for the others; Kingdom Developers work all three.

Carry Module 10's deepest recognition into the repair: the culture war is a manufactured formation running on inequality's fuel — Amos 8's dishonest scales ending in a famine of the word. Repair that ignores the generator is redecoration. That is why the facilitated conversations below aim at the perception gap the product inflates, why this module's church-leader lane ends in re-rooted economics, and why tomorrow turns to justice: sensemaking cannot be rebuilt on dishonest scales. What is manufactured can be declined — Kingdom Developers refuse the product.

### Scripture Engagement

**John 17:20-23 — the anchor.** Jesus' longest recorded prayer spends its climax on epistemic community: "that they may be brought to complete unity. Then the world will know..." Unity here is not agreement on everything; it is the visible oneness of a people whose love survives their differences — precisely the capacity the distortion machine destroys. The church's sensemaking is therefore mission-critical infrastructure, not an internal luxury.

**1 Corinthians 1:10 — mended nets.** Paul appeals "that all of you agree with one another... that there be no divisions among you, and that you be perfectly united in mind and thought." The word behind "perfectly united" (*katartizō*) is the mending of torn fishing nets — Matthew uses it for James and John repairing nets with their father. Unity is not manufactured uniformity; it is patient repair work on torn fabric so the community can hold weight again. That is exactly what facilitated small conversations, perception-gap corrections, and viral friction are: net-mending tools.

**Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 with Proverbs 15:22 — communal epistemology.** "Two are better than one... a cord of three strands is not quickly broken"; "plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed." Scripture assumes what cognitive science confirmed: we never think alone. Knowledge lives in the community, which means the *quality of the community* is the quality of the knowing.

**Hebrews 10:24-25 — the sensemaking liturgy.** "Consider how we may spur one another on... not giving up meeting together... but encouraging one another." Regular, embodied assembly is the original epistemic technology: repeat interactions (Module 4's first trust condition) institutionalized as worship.

### Story: Trustville and Skeptica

Imagine two neighboring villages, identical in size, resources, and challenges. In **Trustville**, doors go unlocked and tools are freely borrowed; the market runs on an honor system; community projects finish quickly with volunteers to spare; conflicts resolve in open dialogue; the online forum hosts respectful, collaborative debate; town meetings are full; the lone police officer is a helper. In **Skeptica**, houses are fortified and lending is rare; the market needs surveillance; projects stall for lack of trust in leadership; conflicts escalate to lawsuits; the forum burns with attacks and misinformation; meetings are empty and every decision suspect; a heavy police presence breeds more tension. Over the years the villages diverge completely: Trustville compounds a *trust dividend* — enterprise, innovation, thriving schools — while Skeptica pays a *suspicion tax* on every single interaction.

The fable is Module 4's trust conditions grown to civilizational scale: repeat interactions, win-wins, and low miscommunication either compound or collapse. The question it leaves with us: which village do our digital spaces resemble — and which are we, as Kingdom Developers, building?

## Discussion & Activity

**Building Trustville.** In groups of 4–5, act as Kingdom Developers assigned to transform a digital "Skeptica" into a "Trustville" — a church's online community, a Christian platform, a faith-based forum. Work the questions: (a) What features of your Skeptica most need addressing? (b) How do Trustville's principles translate to this space? (c) What specific tools, practices, or policies would you implement? (d) How do you balance openness with safeguards against genuine misuse? (e) How does trust built digitally transfer into embodied community? (f) How will you measure success? Present each plan; close with full-class reflection on the common themes.

## Application — Three Lanes

- **Tech workers:** Implement the interventions where you build: viral friction before resharing; perception-gap-aware ranking; empathy and context features; harms made transparent; a crisis playbook with blackout options. Study Wikipedia's model and Taiwan's civic tech as existence proofs that collaborative truth-seeking scales. Humility is the engineering requirement here: systems that admit their bias outperform systems that deny it.
- **Households:** Practice mutual understanding as a learnable skill at your own table: charitable interpretation as the house rule, one small facilitated conversation across a real divide this month (invite them to dinner — embodied beats threaded), and connection as the household's first response to a struggling member, remembering that the opposite of addiction is not sobriety but connection.
- **Church leaders:** Rebuild a well-moderated, hyper-local, *embodied* media culture, in three clusters. **Become the commons:** an inclusive third space (co-working, café, recreation, transit-accessible); church facilities as community information hubs; media-literacy workshops open to all; a civic-journalism initiative and youth media corps covering local events and government; a fact-check hotline; a moderated digital town hall; support — including financial — for local news outlets. **Weave the generations:** intergenerational wisdom exchange and community storytelling projects that build empathy across age and difference — with the exchange running both directions, for sometimes the word comes to the youngest in the house (Samuel under Eli, 1 Samuel 3; young Jeremiah, Jeremiah 1:6-7). **Re-root the economy** (extending Module 7's regeneration): exit-to-community investing, land trusts, micro-lending, time banking, Sabbath-economics education, simplicity and "do less" challenges, and support for members intentionally downshifting. Trust is rebuilt where information, relationship, and economics are re-localized together.

## Conclusion

Kingdom Developers build Trustvilles. We have the three-front repair, the net-mending tools, and the theological ground: unity is what Jesus prayed for and what the world reads as evidence. Humility is the keystone — the Wikipedia lesson and the Philippians 2 lesson are the same lesson — because an epistemic commons scales only among people who consider others above themselves and hold their own certainty lightly before the Truth himself.

But a commons has a boundary question we have not yet faced: *who gets in?* Shared understanding among the already-included is just a larger private club. The banquet Jesus describes will not tolerate empty seats or unpapered invitations — the house must be *full*. Tomorrow, the refrain's hardest clause: act justly. **Module 12 — Fairness & Justice: Fill My House.**
