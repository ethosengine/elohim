**Movement I — Waking Up · Day 2**

*Arc: from admiring wisdom → to building the trust conditions and practices that let wisdom scale.*

*Anchor text: Matthew 10:16 · Supporting: 1 Kings 3:16-28; James 3:13-18 with Matthew 5:9; Micah 6:8 (refrain — first appearance) · Story: Righteous leaders who scaled wisdom*

Module 3 ended with the real deficit named: not information but wisdom — and the trust that lets wisdom travel between people. Technology's power has grown exponentially; our wisdom has not. That distance is the Wisdom Gap, and closing it is not an individual achievement. Wisdom scales only through trust, and trust has conditions that can be deliberately built. This module closes Movement I: having lamented, learned to see systems, and stated the problem, we now name what a faithful response requires.

## Opening — The Refrain Begins

Read Micah 6:8 aloud together: "He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the LORD require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God." This verse will return throughout the course until all three clauses resolve at commissioning. Today we take up its final clause first — *walk humbly* — because humility is where wisdom starts (Proverbs 11:2). Pray: *Lord, you gave Solomon wisdom when he confessed he did not know how to carry out his duties. We confess the same. Teach us to walk humbly before we try to walk far.*

## Humane Tech Concepts in View

This module pairs FHT's *Setting the Stage* with the Evolution of Trust lab:

- **The Wisdom Gap** — runaway technology outpacing the human capacity to guide it; the need for technology that strengthens rather than undermines that capacity, and for a paradigm shift in how we build.
- **Mind the Gap** — FHT's reflection exercise: what does the world need? Who are you at your core? How far is your current life from those priorities, and why?
- **The three conditions for the evolution of trust** (ncase.me/trust): repeat interactions; possible win-wins (non-zero-sum games — what Paul will name isotēs in Module 7); low miscommunication — with forgiveness covering the little miscommunication that remains.
- **The game defines the players.** Game theory's biggest lesson: our problem isn't just that people are losing trust — our environment now acts *against* the evolution of trust. In the short run the game defines the players; in the long run, the players define the game. And we are each other's environment.

The Christmas Truce of 1914 is the lab's closing image: soldiers crossing No Man's Land because, for one night, the conditions for trust briefly held. The church's calling is to be the place where those conditions permanently hold.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Explain the Wisdom Gap and why wisdom, unlike information, only scales through relationships of trust.
2. Name the three conditions for the evolution of trust and locate each in the practices of a healthy congregation.
3. Analyze Jesus' "shrewd as snakes, innocent as doves" as the posture for building trust among wolves without becoming one.
4. Trace the traits by which righteous Old Testament leaders scaled wisdom — humility, obedience, justice, leading by example, renewal.
5. Complete the Mind the Gap exercise and identify one cause of the gap between their values and their current life.
6. Design one concrete change to their community's "game" that improves the conditions for trust.

## Teaching

### Introduction: Wisdom Travels on Trust

Information moves at the speed of light; wisdom moves at the speed of trust. A congregation can stream a sermon to thousands and still be unable to have one honest conversation about a controversial topic without breaking. That is the Wisdom Gap in miniature — and it is why the trust lab matters theologically. If trust needs repeat interactions, win-wins, and low miscommunication, then a church's calendar, economics, and communication habits are not logistics. They are the game board on which trust either evolves or dies. We do not merely play the game we are given; as the Body of Christ, we are called to redesign it.

### Scripture Engagement

**Matthew 10:16 — the anchor.** "I am sending you out like sheep among wolves. Therefore be as shrewd as snakes and as innocent as doves." Jesus sends his people into a low-trust environment with a double command: full clarity about the game being played (shrewd), and refusal to play it by predation (innocent). This is trust-building among wolves — neither naivety, which gets devoured, nor cynicism, which becomes a wolf. The tension between those two words is the whole art of Christian engagement with rivalrous systems.

**James 3:13-18 with Matthew 5:9 — peacemaking is coupled to wisdom.** "The wisdom from above is first pure, then peace-loving, gentle, full of mercy and good fruits... and the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who cultivate peace." Blessed are the peacemakers. Scripture will not let us separate being wise from making peace — which is exactly what game theory found from the other direction: forgiveness and clear communication are what let trust survive noise.

**1 Kings 3:16-28 — wisdom in public.** Solomon's judgment between the two mothers scaled precisely because it was seen: "all Israel heard of the judgment... and they stood in awe." Wisdom exercised transparently becomes a public good; it raises the trust level of the whole system.

**Micah 6:8 — walk humbly.** The refrain's first clause for this course: humility is the precondition for collective wisdom, because it makes us teachable by God and by one another.

### Story: Leaders Who Scaled Wisdom

The righteous leaders of the Old Testament form a composite blueprint: they sought God's wisdom above all else (Solomon, 1 Kings 3:5-14; Daniel 2:17-19); acted with humility (Moses, Numbers 12:3; David's repentance, 2 Samuel 12:13); prioritized obedience over strategy (Abraham; Joshua at Jericho); led by example rather than decree (Nehemiah on the wall, Nehemiah 4:21-23; Josiah's personal reforms, 2 Kings 23); promoted justice (Samuel, 1 Samuel 12:3-5; Jehoshaphat's courts, 2 Chronicles 19:4-11); relied on prayer (Hezekiah, 2 Kings 19); and led renewal through returning to God's word (Ezra 7:10; Josiah, 2 Kings 22). None of them expanded influence through treaties and chariots. Every one of them expanded it through faithfulness — trust built by repeated, visible, costly integrity.

## Discussion & Activity

**Jesus' Kingdom wisdom.** Survey five moments briefly: the Sermon on the Mount (spirit over letter, inner transformation over compliance); the Good Samaritan (wisdom as compassionate action across boundaries); servant leadership in Mark 10:42-45 (power inverted); the Parable of the Talents (responsibility scaling with gifts); and "give to Caesar what is Caesar's" (navigating a political trap without joining either side). Then discuss:

1. How can we design digital and church systems that reward inner transformation and compassionate action rather than mere compliance or engagement?
2. Where does Jesus' servant-leadership model directly contradict how our platforms and programs currently distribute power?
3. Given Jesus' shrewd navigation of rivalrous traps, what would technology that helps ordinary people do the same look like?

**Activity — Mind the Gap.** In silence: What does the world need? Who are you, at your core? Imagine a life that matches those priorities. How big is the gap — and what is causing it? Share one cause with a partner. (This exercise returns in Module 14; keep your notes.)

## Application — Three Lanes

- **Tech workers:** Sketch one *wisdom amplification* feature for something you build — reflection prompts that interrupt mindless flows, metrics that measure discernment rather than time-on-site, designs that surface diverse perspectives, or tools that make consequences visible before actions are taken (Galatians 6:7). Bring the sketch to Module 8, where values get centered formally.
- **Households:** Audit your home against the three trust conditions. Repeat interactions: is there a daily table, a weekly ritual? Win-wins: do chores and decisions have a shape where everyone can gain? Low miscommunication: is there a regular, unhurried time when misunderstandings get repaired? Choose the weakest condition and strengthen it this week.
- **Church leaders:** Redesign the game board, not the players. Treat communication as a whole-body discipline with a clear path for anyone to create, review, and publish a story — not one minister's job. Give small groups real platform support for discoverability, scheduling, and invitation. Treat transition times between services and classes as key invitational moments — extend them, extend children's pick-up, release kids to the playground. Prioritize high-quality communal meals that make staying easy and childcare flexible. Each of these is one of the three trust conditions made concrete. *(The institutional-standards material — exceeding legal minimums on misconduct, tax, and equity — now lives in Module 7 as binding scale to responsibility.)*

## Conclusion — Movement I Closes

A meta-statement gathers what the righteous leaders, the teachings of Jesus, and the trust lab have shown us: Christians are called to design systems of heart and mind that cultivate humble seekers of truth, who grow in wisdom through obedient action, and who shoulder expanding responsibility with increasing grace — communities where knowledge flows freely, where power serves rather than dominates, and where each member stewards their influence for the flourishing of all.

Movement I is complete: we have lamented honestly (Module 1), learned to see systems rather than scapegoats (Module 2), stated the problem precisely (Module 3), and named what a faithful response requires — wisdom, scaled through deliberately built trust (Module 4). Now the course turns from seeing to repenting. And repentance begins at the exact place the attention economy attacks first. Trust grows through repeat interactions — through attention, given again and again. What we attend to, we become; and our attention, it turns out, is sacred. That is Movement II's opening claim: **Module 5 — Our Attention is Sacred.**
