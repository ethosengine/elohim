**Movement V — Abundant Life & Sending · Day 13**

*Arc: from course participant → to covenanted digital disciple with a rule of life and accountability.*

*Anchor text: Philippians 1:6 · Commissioning text: Matthew 28:19-20*

The final session is not another module of content — it is consecration. Deliberately, there is no FHT concepts column here: the pairing chain completed yesterday, and today belongs entirely to the cohort. The format is a retrospective — the agile ceremony adapted for a Christian technology context — because a practice this course has commended all along (reflect on outcomes, adjust the system, commit together) deserves to be how the course itself ends. Time: 60–90 minutes. Materials: sticky notes (physical or digital), a whiteboard or shared workspace, and the action plans drafted as yesterday's homework.

## Opening — He Who Began a Good Work

Read Philippians 1:6: "being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus." Fourteen days cannot finish anything — and they don't have to. The work begun here has a guarantor. Pray, inviting God's wisdom and guidance for honest reflection and courageous commitment.

## Purpose & Objectives

To reflect on the learning journey, identify key takeaways, and create covenanted action plans for implementing humane technology principles in our Christian walk and tech engagement. Participants will leave having: named where God was at work in their fourteen days; identified their remaining tensions between faith and technology; committed to three concrete actions (personal, congregational, professional); contributed to a shared Digital Discipleship Manifesto; and entered an accountability relationship with a follow-up date on the calendar.

## The Retrospective Liturgy

1. **Opening prayer** (5 min) — as above.
2. **Set the stage** (5 min) — the facilitator recaps the five movements in a few sentences (the Overview tab's map serves well) and explains the retrospective process.
3. **Gather data** (15 min) — on sticky notes, each participant answers: *What key insights about faith and technology have you gained? Where have you seen God at work in your engagement with technology during this course? What challenges or tensions remain in aligning your tech use or development with your faith?*
4. **Generate insights** (20 min) — in small groups, share and cluster the notes, looking for patterns. Discuss: *How do these insights reflect our calling as digital disciples? Where do we see opportunities to be salt and light in the tech world?*
5. **Decide what to do** (20 min) — individually, then in pairs, refine yesterday's drafted action plans into three commitments: one immediate change in personal tech habits; one initiative to start or join in church or community; one way to advocate for Christ-centered approaches in professional tech engagement.
6. **Close the retrospective** (15 min) — each person shares their top action item with the whole group. Then, together, compose the **Digital Discipleship Manifesto**: a short list of commitments this cohort makes to steward technology in a way that honors God and serves humanity — this group's own "song of witness" (Module 6), written down and kept.
7. **Closing prayer of commissioning** (5 min) — end with Matthew 28:19-20: "go and make disciples of all nations... and surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age." The promise of presence covers the digital age too. Ask God's strength for what each person now carries.

## Follow-Up — The Covenant Holds

Each participant pairs with an accountability partner from the course and shares their action plan. Schedule a follow-up gathering in one to three months to check progress, tell stories of impact, and pray again — the first repeat interaction (Module 4) of the community this course has become. He who began a good work will carry it on to completion.

*The course ends where it began, transformed: Day 0 opened with a groan God heard; Day 13 closes with a people God sends.*
