**Movement V — Abundant Life & Sending · Day 11**

*Arc: from engagement as success → to abundant life in the Kingdom as the telos of everything we build.*

*Anchor text: John 10:10 · Supporting: Leviticus 19:9-10 with Deuteronomy 24:19-21 (the gleaning laws); Matthew 6:33; Joshua 24:15 · Story: The feeding of the five thousand (John 6:1-14)*

Movement V begins at the destination. Every prior module cleared ground or built structure; this one names what it was all *for*. Humane technology's stated goal is supporting the thriving of life; Jesus stated it first, and stronger: "I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly." The course's remaining work is to define thriving truthfully — against the industry's counterfeits and, where necessary, against the church's — and to build toward it.

## Opening — The Door and the Thief

Read John 10:9-11. Two figures stand at the sheepfold: the thief, who "comes only to steal and kill and destroy," and the shepherd, who lays down his life so the sheep may "go in and out and find pasture." These are the two design paradigms of our age — extraction and abundance — and every product, program, and platform is discipled by one of them. Pray: *Good Shepherd, you came that we may have life in abundance. Teach us to know your voice among the thieves, and to build only folds with pasture.*

## Humane Tech Concepts in View

This module pairs with FHT's *Helping People Thrive*:

- **Thriving as the aim.** The ultimate goal of humane technology is supporting the thriving of life — a complex, evolving concept, not a KPI. It involves balancing pleasure, pain, and meaning; capacities for gratitude, creativity, and learning; wise response to circumstances; quality relationships; and a life of minimal regret, true to oneself.
- **Thriving in systems:** basic needs and capabilities for all, within ecological limits (the Doughnut model returning from Module 7).
- **False thriving.** Addictive experiences *mimic* thriving — stimulation impersonating meaning, parasocial feeds impersonating relationship — while undermining the real thing. Discernment between the two is the module's core skill.
- **Design principles for thriving:** protect attention; design for intentionality (support conscious choice); create deeply valuable experiences (challenge and satisfaction, not just stimulation).
- **Practical shifts:** user *regret* as a metric; designing for off-screen life and relationships; accountability between designers and users; technology that deepens rather than flattens what it means to be human.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Define thriving in both FHT's terms and Scripture's — abundant life — and explain why engagement metrics cannot measure it.
2. Trace the biblical progression from Promised Land to Kingdom of God as the theology of flourishing, including the gleaning laws as designed margin for the vulnerable.
3. Distinguish genuine thriving from its addictive mimics in specific products and church practices.
4. Apply the three design principles — protected attention, intentionality, deeply valuable experiences — with their Christian groundings.
5. Evaluate the "thriving stories" of major platforms and of their own church's mission statement for authenticity and drift.
6. Derive design principles from the feeding of the five thousand and apply one to something they build or lead.

## Teaching

### Introduction: From Promised Land to Kingdom

The Bible's theology of thriving runs from a land flowing with milk and honey to a Kingdom where God's will is done on earth as in heaven — and the digital age is our generation's frontier of that same question: will we enter it as stewards or as conquerors? The Promised Land came with a design specification for flourishing, and its most striking feature was *mandated inefficiency*: "When you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not reap your field right up to its edge... you shall leave them for the poor and for the sojourner" (Leviticus 19:9-10; likewise Deuteronomy 24:19-21). God legislated margin — un-optimized edges, forgotten sheaves left where they fell — so that the vulnerable could thrive inside the same economy as everyone else. An economy harvested to 100% efficiency was, by divine definition, unjust. That is a design principle our optimization culture has entirely forgotten: leave the edges of your field. And the reason given is memory and empathy: "you know the heart of a sojourner, for you were sojourners in Egypt" (Exodus 23:9) — Module 1's story, completing its arc.

### Scripture Engagement

**John 10:10 — the anchor.** The thief's operations — steal, kill, destroy — map with uncomfortable precision onto the extraction economy: attention stolen, time killed, communities destroyed. The shepherd's alternative is not moderation of theft but a different telos entirely: abundant life. Note that abundance in John 10 is pastoral, not maximal — pasture, going in and out, a voice known and trusted. Thriving is relational abundance, not accumulated stimulation.

**Matthew 6:33 with Joshua 24:15 — ordering and choosing.** "Seek *first* the kingdom" is a priority architecture: thriving follows right ordering, never precedes it. And "choose this day whom you will serve" is the intentionality principle in covenant form — thriving requires conscious choice, which is exactly what dark patterns are engineered to bypass.

**The witness of the whole canon.** FHT's components of thriving turn out to be catalogued in Scripture with remarkable coverage:

| Thriving involves | Scripture's witness |
| --- | --- |
| Balancing pleasure, pain, and meaning | Ecclesiastes 3:1-8; Romans 5:3-5 |
| Gratitude, creativity, learning | 1 Thessalonians 5:18; Exodus 35:31-32 (Bezalel, the Spirit-filled craftsman) |
| Accepting circumstances, responding wisely | Philippians 4:11-13; James 1:5 |
| Care, kindness, quality relationships | 1 Corinthians 13:4-7; Proverbs 17:17 |
| Minimizing regret, living true | Galatians 6:4-5; Matthew 6:33 |
| Basic needs and capabilities for all | Acts 4:32-35; Proverbs 31:8-9 |
| Protected attention and time | Ephesians 5:15-16; Psalm 90:12 |
| Intentionality and conscious choice | Joshua 24:15; 1 Corinthians 10:31 |
| Deeply valuable experiences | Colossians 3:23-24; Philippians 4:8 |

One line surfaces the difference between the feed's thriving and the Bible's: Romans 5 puts *suffering* inside the flourishing life — endurance, character, hope — while frictionless design promises thriving with the pain surgically removed. A definition of thriving that cannot include suffering is a mimic.

### Story: Five Loaves, Two Fish, Twelve Baskets

The feeding of the five thousand — the only miracle in all four Gospels (John 6:1-14 and parallels) — reads as a masterclass in design for thriving. Jesus **recognizes real need** (hunger, not manufactured desire) and addresses the whole person, physical and spiritual. He practices **stewardship**: five loaves and two fish, what is actually at hand, transformed rather than externally sourced. The miracle is **communal** — distribution through participation, not broadcast. He designs for **mindfulness**: the crowd seated in ordered groups, a meal experienced rather than consumed on the move. And the economics are **abundance without excess**: everyone satisfied, and twelve baskets gathered so nothing is wasted.

From this story, the course's ten **Christian design principles**: servant-hearted design (serve needs, never exploit vulnerabilities) · stewardship (sustainable, resource-wise) · community-centric (strengthen real-world relationship) · Sabbath-honoring (rest and disconnection built in — and Sabbath bent toward doing good, Matthew 12:12) · truth-promoting · justice-oriented (the marginalized designed for first) · attention-respecting (Module 5's sacredness) · virtue-fostering (Module 9's fruits) · creation-affirming (deeper engagement with the physical world, not escape from it) · Kingdom-advancing (more of heaven brought to earth through what we make).

## Discussion & Activity

1. **Examining flawed stories.** Tech companies tell compelling thriving stories. Weigh three stereotypical ones: "We value creators and want a place where they can show off their brilliance, grow their audience, and make real money" (TikTok-style); "We're a place for fun, inspiration, and ideas that matter... so people can lead more informed and meaningful lives" (YouTube/Google-style); "We help small business owners find customers who'll love their products so their businesses can thrive" (Instagram-style). In what ways is each authentic? In what ways does each *mimic* thriving toxically? Notice which of your own definitions of thriving are tangled up in these stories — then test a product you use or build: does it define thriving accurately?
2. **Examining our church's story.** Churches have mission statements too, and the same drift. Find yours (or default to Matthew 28:19-20 / Acts 1:8) and ask: what does it claim about human thriving? Where is it authentically lived? Where might practice mimic thriving — a global-missions identity that neglects the neighborhood; "love God, love others" expressed mainly as production-value worship; "all are welcome" with unspoken socioeconomic barriers? Which metrics (attendance, giving) quietly steer us away from the mission? And where does our technology *simulate* spiritual growth or community without fostering the transformative reality?

## Application — Three Lanes

- **Tech workers:** Adopt regret as a metric: instrument one honest signal of whether users are glad, a week later, for the time they gave you. Design one feature *for the off-screen life* — something whose success is measured by what happens away from the product. Then grade your product against the ten Christian design principles and pick the weakest to improve.
- **Households:** Take the 30-day Sacred Attention challenge as a family — digital fasting rhythms, contemplative prayer, mindful use — and extend Module 12's lifestyle audit toward thriving: do our schedules and screens produce deeply valuable experiences or merely full ones? Choose one "less but better" substitution and keep it.
- **Church leaders:** Build the thriving-oriented church: redefine success metrics away from attendance and giving toward relationship, formation, and neighborhood impact (Module 9's dashboard, now operational); redesign gatherings for deep interaction over passive consumption; adopt a "less is more" program philosophy and a *Slow Church* culture — depth over speed, process over immediate results; develop a digital-discipleship framework where tools enhance rather than replace embodied fellowship; implement collective resource stewardship (tool libraries, carpools, skill exchanges); and root the congregation ever deeper in its actual neighborhood.

## Conclusion

The three design principles now have their full Christian grounding: attention protected because "my sheep hear my voice" (John 10:27) and technology must not crowd out the Shepherd's; intentionality designed for because "the heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps" (Proverbs 16:9); deeply valuable experiences cultivated because Jesus intends "that my joy may be in you, and that your joy may be full" (John 15:11). And one warning stands over all of it: "see to it that no one takes you captive through hollow and deceptive philosophy" (Colossians 2:8) — the same verse that opened this course in Module 1, now revealed as a design review. Empty promises of fulfillment apart from Christ are the thief's signature, however beautiful the interface.

We know the telos. What remains is to *go*. Tomorrow the course turns from understanding to commissioning: **Module 14 — Ready to Act: Embodying Christ in the Digital Age.**
