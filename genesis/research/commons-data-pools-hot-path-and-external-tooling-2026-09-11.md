# Commons Data Pools

*The second research pass after the search epic. The epic set the bar: above the household an index is a commons pool — contributed to, drawn from, governed by its members, run by elohim as a utility — and pools are the hot path for maps, directions, street imagery, search, and an internally governed announcement market, beside the economic pools (mutual credit, risk transfer, R&D, network development). This document says what the tree already has for that, what must be built, how tools brought in from outside are governed, how AI commons pools live on committed compute on people's devices, and where it breaks.*

**Method.** A Sonnet explorer grounded the tree (`path:line`, 120 tool uses); three Sonnet horizon surveys graded adjacent work TAKE / TAKE-DISC / REFUSE / WATCH with verification keys (search-memory-AI-commons; GIS-ads-coops-pools; data brokers and intermediaries); synthesis by the orchestrating session. The operator co-authors; §8 is questions with a recommendation each.

**Verification key:** ✅ verified in source · ◐ single-source · ⚠ unverified.

---

## 1. The ask, restated

Take the epic and make it practical. Survey the field. Document what we have and what we must build. Say what the role of tooling like MemPalace will be — how external tooling is governed and monitored when people bring it into the protocol, and what internal security that needs. Then the concern: **how do we create AI commons data pools that live on the shared p2p dataplane and on committed commons compute on people's devices, and what are the emergent risks?** And: how is that data supplied to the hot path so EPR applications can be built on it — GIS-like utilities (street view, maps, directions), internally governed ad networks, search services — the "true commons utilities" the elohim are responsible to govern and execute faithfully for the network, alongside token markets, liquidity pools, risk transfer, R&D and network-development pools.

Two rulings from the epic pass frame everything below: **no external mint** decides what a commons values (the bounty rises until answered; rewards diminish and diffuse), and **anything public is stewarded and can be assumed commons at any scale**.

## 4. AI commons data pools on committed device compute — the concern

**How a pool lives.** A pool is a Collective EPR whose members' devices carry a *declared share* — the constitutional donut's `COMMONS_*` band between DNA-locked floor and ceiling — of storage, and, by the same commitment shape, of compute (`delegates-compute` with `reach_ceiling: commons`, rate-limited, rotation-TTL'd). The pool's content is contributed at declared reach with per-unit provenance; its index and any model trained on it are *derived* artifacts with CIDs, rebuildable from the contributions; its recipe is a published artifact; its utilities (crawlers, indexers, embedders, routers) run inside tevah envelopes on members' devices under grants, with death witnesses. Reads are served explorer-blind from range-addressable archives at the edges (patron-CDN), never from a query log. What a pool yields — its *fruits* — accrues to its participants and is settled outward only across a bridge, capped.

**Compute goes to the data, never the reverse.** The recipe travels to the household's own corpus (OpenMined shape; Ocean's compute-to-data as the one thing worth keeping from a token project). Pooled inference across devices is the Petals shape with the untrusted-swarm trust model replaced by reach-gated peers; kin-ring device pooling (exo) is a watch item.

**Emergent risks, numbered, each with the organ that answers it:**

1. **Pool poisoning before governance** — LAION's failure: index first, govern later, and the illegal content cannot be excised. *Answer:* reach and consent are checked at contribution (the gate precedes the index); a contribution without provenance is unreadable-counted-named, never silently indexed.
2. **Honest-but-curious pool operator** — the pool learns which candidate a household selected. *Answer:* explorer-blind range reads; per-holon journeys; PIR (Pirex-class, 55 ms over 1 TB ◐) is the watch item if a pool operator ever becomes an adversarial-curiosity concern rather than a governance one.
3. **Pool capture / admin oligarchy** — the biggest pool index becomes the new Google; or Wikipedia's admin ossification. *Answer:* recipe as public artifact; qahal by lot; standing loss; **and the revenue cap** — a pool that funds itself past a declared share of its members' contribution is structurally captured (Wikimedia's 30%).
4. **Dependence from inside** (search red-team row 12) — one provider answers so well the `select` stage rubber-stamps it. *Answer:* per-ring provider share printed on the honesty line; plurality as a measured bound.
5. **Who may train on the pool** — Stance I.3 says intelligence built from the commons is owed to the commons; contributors will want reciprocity/attribution/no-commercial axes. *Answer:* contribution terms carry a CC-Signals-shaped preference (reciprocity · recognition · sustainability) and, once standardized, the IETF aipref vocabulary as a passive field; **enforcement is ours** — the IETF explicitly refuses to standardize it. A model trained on a pool is a pool asset with a declared CID and the pool's terms.
6. **Free-riding and the token temptation** — the survey's starkest pattern: every token-incentivized commons compute/data project failed or pivoted (Circles, Streamr, Saturn, Holo's HOT-for-HoloFuel); the governance-first ones endured (Tor 20 y, WIR 90 y, MIDATA, Wikimedia, Panoramax). *Answer:* no mint; commitments are the incentive; and the caution from Holo — "the mutual-credit engine isn't ready" must never quietly become "so we bolted on a token."
7. **Legal exposure of pool stewards** — LAION's Hamburg ruling left custody ambiguous; ODI's trusts pilots found no template. *Answer:* the fiduciary framing enforced by code (DHT custody + sortition + REA commitments), the witnessed-harm limit (confidentiality row 8), and the fiscal-host shape at the bridge so a pool needs no legal entity of its own.
8. **The hot path as centralization pressure** — a fast pool wants a big hub. *Answer:* the hub-cluster seam absorbs k8s-class concerns *inside* a hub while patron-CDN keeps the edge many; single-file range-addressed archives make the edge cheap.
9. **Device heterogeneity and energy** — phones, Pis, laptops, family nodes. *Answer:* the donut's floor/ceiling per tier; the trust-compute gradient's two immune floors; Valhalla-class utilities that load on demand.
10. **Silent degradation** — a provider falls away and the lens quietly goes lexical. *Answer:* degradation is a printed line, never a fallback (backlog row already filed).
11. **Convergence debt** — a pool served from a lagging peer serves stale confidently. *Answer:* inherits dataplane-convergence; every pool read carries the head CID and the verdict's freshness.

## 5. External tooling: the admission contract (MemPalace as the instance)

The epic's rule is *propose, never rule; settle, never scrape*. For a tool brought into the protocol by a person, that becomes a contract in six parts, every part already having a home:

1. **Declared** — the tool is a `Provider` with an id, a method CID if it has one, and `ranking_known` (true only with a declared model CID). Its declaration lives under `PackageAuthority` as a package with a master, projection allowlist, governance ref and gates.
2. **Granted, not keyed** — its access is an attenuated capability rooted in a Mishpat Commitment (UCAN's law, not its keypair root), scoped to *propose candidates within a scope and budget*, never to reorder across rings, never to read the diary or the journey store. Today: per-agent frontmatter allowlists (librarian full, cartographer read-only, diary and reconnect operator-only). Tomorrow: the same allowlist as a per-holon grant with a TTL and a rotation, exactly the `compute_grants` bounds shape.
3. **Budgeted** — `provider_bytes` / `provider_seconds` already charged; add a per-ring *accepted-share* bound (red-team row 12).
4. **Attested** — the tool's manifest and declared capabilities verified against an in-toto-shaped attestation envelope *before* its description enters any planner context. **Tool descriptions are untrusted input** (Invariant Labs tool-poisoning, >60% success across 45 real MCP servers ✅): isolated like tool output, never trusted at face value.
5. **Enveloped** — it runs inside tevah: cgroup, quota, death witness, no ambient filesystem. A tool that dies leaves a witness; a tool that exceeds its envelope is shed and the shed is printed.
6. **Witnessed and retirable** — its reads leave receipts; its share prints on the honesty line; degradation prints; and it retires by a recipe version change when a native provider answers the same seam. The five sparring-partner tests (search doc §7) stay pointed at whatever comes next.

**Internal security posture.** The honest-but-curious tool is the threat model, not the malicious one only: the diary and journey store are never readable by any provider; a provider's *description* is the attack surface as much as its output; grants are narrow-only by construction (no general negation, stratified exclusion — the OWL note's binding laws); default-deny at runtime even though authoring may fail open. Monitoring that does not itself become surveillance: receipts are per-holon; aggregate signals (which providers are used, how often they are shed) are folded per collective and anonymous, Prio/DAP-shaped when they cross a ring.

### Horizon Survey — Adjacent Work on P2P Search, Memory & Data Commons (2026-09-11)

#### 1. Decentralized / federated / local-first search & indexing

**Willow Protocol / Earthstar** (Earthstar Project, 2024–2025) — a meta-protocol specifying a data model + "General Purpose Sync" for eventually-consistent, fine-grained-permissioned local-first stores; explicitly *not* content-addressed — names are freely chosen and mutable, retrieval returns "the most recently bound value" for a name, the inverse of CID semantics. [willowprotocol.org](https://willowprotocol.org/more/willow_compared/index.html) · [earthstar-project/willow-rs](https://github.com/earthstar-project/willow-rs) — Relevance: closest peer for "sync outward through relationships," but its naming model is the opposite of our content-addressing commitment. VERDICT: **WATCH** (return if we ever need named/mutable pointers layered atop CIDs — e.g. "current household head"). ◐

**p2panda** (NLnet-funded, 2025) — Rust SDK for local-first p2p apps; `p2panda-encryption` (targeted Spring 2025) does UCAN-based capability delegation + CRDT-managed group membership with post-compromise security, decoupled from any specific transport/data type. [p2panda.org](https://p2panda.org/2025/02/24/group-encryption.html) — Relevance: a real prior-art for "capability-scoped group encryption over p2p gossip," adjacent to how a recall session's private receipts should be scoped. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (the CRDT-group-membership + capability pattern, not the crate). ◐

**Stract** (Mikkel Denker, ongoing, checked 2026) — independent search engine with its own crawler/index, whose "Optics" is a small DSL letting any user rewrite ranking-signal weights and site allow/deny lists client-side, applied at query time on top of one shared index. [stract GitHub README](https://github.com/StractOrg/stract/blob/main/README.md) — Relevance: directly validates "ranking recipe as a separable, user-selectable artifact over a shared index" — but Stract's optics still run against ONE centralized index, not a ringed household-outward walk. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (ranking-as-swappable-DSL over a fixed corpus; refuse the centralized-index part). ✅

**Mwmbl** ("the truly open source search engine," ongoing) — non-profit search engine where crawling is distributed across volunteer nodes but the resulting index is centralized; uniquely, any user's added/deleted/reranked result is written directly into the shared index and immediately visible to all other users on that query (~half a billion pages, 30k+ curations as of mid-2025). [mwmbl.org](https://mwmbl.org/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/mwmbl/mwmbl) — Relevance: an important REFUSE case-study — user curation writes straight into one global, unmediated ranking with no reach/trust gating, which is exactly the failure mode our "reach earned before content spreads" design exists to prevent. VERDICT: **REFUSE** (global unmediated write access to shared ranking; no trust gradient). ◐

**Open Web Index / OpenWebSearch.eu** (EU-funded, €8.5M, 2022–2026) — a shared, petabyte-scale European web index, opened for API access in May 2025 under research and commercial licenses, intended as neutral infrastructure other search products build on rather than a consumer search engine itself. [openwebsearch.eu](https://openwebsearch.eu/open-webindex/) — Relevance: the closest real analog to a "commons pool" — an index run as a contributed utility with governed access tiers, separate from any one ranking recipe. VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: if it publishes a stable federation/query protocol, evaluate as an external opaque provider under our `ranking_known:false` admission rule). ◐

**Bluesky/AT Protocol Feed Generators** (bsky-social, evolving through 2025) — any DID-identified HTTPS service can host one or more named "feed" algorithms; a user's own PDS fetches an unordered skeleton of post URIs from the feed generator, then *hydrates and renders it locally* — the generator proposes candidates, never final rendering/order beyond its own list. Bluesky's 2025 "Attie" tool lets end users author their own feed algorithms in natural language. [atproto.com/guides/feeds](https://atproto.com/guides/feeds) · [feed-generator starter kit](https://github.com/bluesky-social/feed-generator) — Relevance: strong existing precedent for "opaque candidate-proposer, never final-order decider" — nearly identical shape to our declared-provider contract. VERDICT: **TAKE** (adopt the DID-scoped algorithm-as-service + skeleton-then-hydrate boundary as a model for provider integration). ◐

#### 2. Privacy-preserving / on-device / federated retrieval

**sqlite-vec** (Alex Garcia, active 2025) — a dependency-free SQLite extension adding SIMD-accelerated KNN vector search directly inside the file-based DB process; explicitly positioned for on-device/offline RAG (notes, offline assistants, edge functions) with no server round-trip. [sqlite.ai](https://www.sqlite.ai/sqlite-vector) — Relevance: a candidate embedding-store shape for a per-household elohim agent's local recall index. VERDICT: **TAKE** (embedded, zero-server vector index as the household-local recall substrate). ◐

**LanceDB** (LanceDB Inc., 2024–2025) — columnar, multi-modal embedded vector store, contrasted against sqlite-vec as "purpose-built columnar format vs. lightweight extension on a battle-tested engine." [Kanopy comparison](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec) — Relevance: alternative local-index engine if household corpora grow multi-modal (video/audio content, not just text). VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: household content mix outgrows what a SQLite-embedded index handles well). ◐

**FedVSE / FedVS** (Beihang Univ./academic, VLDB 2025 & KDD 2025) — federated vector-similarity search across institutions that never centralizes raw vectors, one variant using Intel SGX enclaves for hardware-enforced privacy during cross-party KNN. [VLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol18/p5371-tong.pdf) — Relevance: academic proof that cross-household vector search without pooling raw embeddings is tractable, relevant if ranking recipes ever need to run joint similarity across un-pooled household indexes. VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: a habit needs cross-household semantic similarity without a shared pooled index). ◐

**PIR-RAG / Pirex** (academic, 2025) — lattice-based private information retrieval systems purpose-built for RAG: PIR-RAG clusters a corpus semantically then fetches a cluster obliviously; Pirex's two-server online-offline PIR retrieves a 4KB record from a 1TB DB in 55ms, ~2 orders of magnitude faster than prior PIR. [arXiv:2509.21325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21325) — Relevance: PIR solves "hide which document I fetched from an index I don't trust," a different privacy axis than ours (we hide the *question*, not the *fetch*), but relevant once a commons pool is queried by a household that doesn't want the pool operator learning which candidate it selected. VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: a commons-pool operator becomes an adversarial-curiosity concern, not just a governance one). ◐

**Solid / Inrupt** (Tim Berners-Lee, ongoing; book *This Is For Everyone*, Sept 2025) — personal data "Pods" with fine-grained, time-bound access grants; 2025 framing pivots explicitly to AI agents reading from a person's own Pod (not a platform vault) and forgetting on access expiry. [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90807852/tim-berners-lee-inrupt-solid-pods) — Relevance: validates the "question is private, never pooled" stance at the standards-body level, but Solid has no answer for *cross-Pod ranked search* — it's a storage/access-control layer, not a discovery layer. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (time-bound, revocable per-agent access grants as a model for how a MemPalace-class provider should be scoped) — **REFUSE** the idea of Solid as our discovery layer itself (no ranking story). ◐

#### 3. AI commons and shared data pools

**Common Pile v0.1 / Comma models** (EleutherAI + partners, 2025) — an 8TB corpus built exclusively from public-domain/openly-licensed text, aggregated from 30 sources with published curation code, mixture recipes, and benchmark models trained only on it. [blog.eleuther.ai/common-pile](https://blog.eleuther.ai/common-pile/) — Relevance: proves a large, fully-provenanced commons corpus is achievable without scraping-under-ambiguity; a template for how our commons pools should publish provenance alongside content. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (publish-the-recipe-and-provenance discipline, not the corpus itself). ◐

**Common Corpus** (Pleias, Open Trusted Data Initiative, 2025) — >2 trillion tokens, multilingual, permissively licensed, explicitly built to exceed EU AI Act data-training transparency requirements. [huggingface.co blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/Pclanglais/two-trillion-tokens-open) — Relevance: shows a commons pool can be built at frontier scale while staying inside strict regulatory transparency bars — evidence against "commons = necessarily small." VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: a regulatory transparency bar becomes binding on our own commons pools). ◐

**Data Provenance Initiative** (MIT/multi-institution, ongoing) — volunteer audit of 1,800+ text datasets producing machine-readable "Data Provenance Cards" (source/creator/license/allowed-use) and finding a widening gap between commercially open and closed data. [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00878-8) · [GitHub](https://github.com/Data-Provenance-Initiative/Data-Provenance-Collection) — Relevance: a ready-made schema for the provenance metadata a commons-pool contribution record should carry. VERDICT: **TAKE** (adopt the Data Provenance Card field schema for content contributed into a commons pool). ◐

**RSL — Really Simple Licensing** (RSL Collective, launched Sept 10 2025) — machine-readable licensing terms attached via robots.txt, supporting free/attribution/subscription/pay-per-crawl/pay-per-inference models; enforced only where a CDN partner (Fastly) checks crawler compliance — otherwise voluntary. [rslstandard.org press release](https://rslstandard.org/press/rsl-standard) — Relevance: shows the market converging on "license terms as a machine-readable artifact attached to content," parallel to our content-addressed ranking-recipe artifacts, but RSL's enforcement is CDN-gatekeeper-shaped, incompatible with a p2p dataplane with no central edge. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (the vocabulary of licensing terms, not the CDN-enforcement mechanism). ✅

**IETF AI Preferences (aipref) WG** (chartered 2025, targeting Aug 2026) — standardizing (a) a vocabulary for AI-use preferences independent of how it's attached, and (b) attachment mechanisms via robots.txt-style well-known URIs or HTTP headers; explicitly OUT of scope: technical enforcement, auth, registries, auditing. [datatracker.ietf.org/wg/aipref](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/aipref/about/) — Relevance: if this vocabulary standardizes, our content records could carry it as a passive, content-addressed preference field without inventing our own — direct reuse candidate. VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: vocabulary spec reaches Proposed Standard — then TAKE the vocabulary as a field, never the enforcement gap it deliberately leaves open). ✅

**CC Signals** (Creative Commons, previewed June 2025, alpha Nov 2025) — a preference-signal framework (not a license) letting a corpus custodian declare reciprocity/attribution/open-source-only conditions for AI use, with enforceability ranging from legally binding to purely normative. [creativecommons.org](https://creativecommons.org/2025/06/25/introducing-cc-signals-a-new-social-contract-for-the-age-of-ai/) — Relevance: closest existing "governed pool contribution terms" vocabulary at the values layer (reciprocity/recognition/sustainability) rather than the pure-commercial layer RSL occupies. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (reciprocity/recognition/sustainability as named preference axes for commons-pool contribution terms). ◐

**LAION governance failures** (LAION-5B, ongoing since 2022; CSAM findings 2023–2025) — a URL-and-metadata-only dataset with no retained image repository, which made illegal content nearly impossible to fully excise once discovered, and a 2024 Hamburg court ruling on TDM exceptions that left custodial responsibility ambiguous. [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/laion5b-stable-diffusion-and-the-original-sin-of-generative-ai/) — Relevance: the canonical cautionary tale for "pool now, govern later" — reach/consent must be checked BEFORE indexing, never retrofitted onto an already-spread pool. VERDICT: **REFUSE** (ungated bulk-index-first architecture) — cited as the negative case our reach-before-spread design is built against. ◐

**Ocean Protocol Data NFTs** (ongoing) — data assets represented as ERC-721 "Data NFTs" (base IP) plus ERC-20 "datatokens" (access licenses), with Compute-to-Data letting models train against private data without it leaving custody. [docs.oceanprotocol.com](https://docs.oceanprotocol.com/developers/contracts/data-nfts) — Relevance: token-shaped monetization of data access is exactly the pattern our design avoids (reach is earned, not purchased); the compute-to-data idea (compute travels to data, not vice versa) is separately sound and already how our committed-compute model works. VERDICT: **REFUSE** (token/NFT-as-access-right) with **TAKE-DISC** carved out (compute-to-data instead of data-to-compute). ◐

**OpenMined / PySyft** (ongoing) — Python library for "remote data science": a data scientist submits a computation description, and code — not data — travels to wherever the data owner already stores it (their own cloud/drive), executed under differential-privacy/secure-multiparty-computation constraints. [github.com/OpenMined/PySyft](https://github.com/OpenMined/PySyft) — Relevance: a mature, non-blockchain instance of "bring the code to the data" that maps closely onto ranking recipes as portable artifacts run locally against a household's own corpus. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (recipe/computation-travels-to-data pattern; skip the DP/SMPC machinery unless we need formal privacy proofs). ◐

#### 4. Distributed / commons compute on people's devices

**Petals** (BigScience-lineage, ongoing) — "BitTorrent-style" distributed LLM inference over a public volunteer swarm using pipeline parallelism across untrusted peers. [SharedLLM comparison](https://sharedllm.org/blog/sharedllm-vs-petals-vs-exo.html) — Relevance: proves volunteer/committed-device compute pooling for inference is workable at real model scale; direct analog to "committed compute on people's devices" for running ranking recipes. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (pipeline-parallel volunteer inference shape; the public untrusted-swarm trust model needs replacing with our reach-gated peer trust). ◐

**exo** (exo Labs, EXO 1.0 shipped ~Dec 2025) — clusters Apple-Silicon devices (iPhones to Macs) for split-model inference, now using RDMA over Thunderbolt 5 / macOS's native RDMA for near-local-bus bandwidth between devices in one household/office. [macfax.com](https://macfax.com/blog/at-home-mac-inference-cluster-the-complete-2026-guide) — Relevance: this is close to "kin-ring compute pooling" — devices already trusted (same household/owner) getting near-datacenter interconnect. VERDICT: **WATCH** (trigger: multi-device households want to pool compute for local ranking/embedding work — evaluate exo's RDMA transport as a T2-adjacent option). ◐

**llama.cpp RPC backend** (ggml-org, ongoing) — a minimal RPC server exposing raw compute so a coordinator node can offload model layers to remote peers over plain TCP; deliberately simple/no built-in trust model. [Medium walkthrough](https://medium.com/@soumyajit.swain/distributed-llm-inference-on-consumer-machines-with-llama-cpp-a-bare-metal-approach-55ef6ef81f35) — Relevance: lowest-friction reference implementation if we ever need "offload embedding/ranking compute to a trusted peer's spare device" without adopting a whole distributed-inference framework. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (the minimal-RPC-offload shape only, never as a production transport — no auth, no encryption by default). ◐

**Holo / HoloFuel** (Holochain-ecosystem, 2025 re-launch) — a hosting marketplace matching hApp consumers with host-providers, paid via HOT token now (HoloFuel mutual-credit engine delayed; Unyt Inc. spun out in 2025 to generalize the mutual-credit accounting engine beyond Holo hosting). [GitHub Holo-Host/holofuel-model](https://github.com/Holo-Host/holofuel-model) — Relevance: our own protocol's sibling ecosystem's hosting-economy answer; instructive that a *token* filled the gap while the intended mutual-credit engine slipped repeatedly. VERDICT: **REFUSE** (token-mediated hosting market) — **WATCH** the spun-out Unyt mutual-credit engine (trigger: it stabilizes as a generalized accounting layer we could reuse for compute-contribution accounting instead of inventing our own). ◐

#### 5. Agent-memory tooling and its governance

**Mem0 / Zep / Letta / LangMem** (2025–2026 landscape) — Mem0 (Apache-2.0, managed-cloud-first, ~48k stars) does layered conversation/user memory; Zep is a temporal knowledge-graph memory layer that retired self-hosting in 2025 (cloud-only now); Letta carries forward the MemGPT context-paging model as an explicit, inspectable "OS-style" memory; LangMem is LangChain's 2025 open-source SDK wiring memory tools into LangGraph. [comparison roundup](https://datapace.ai/blog/ai-agent-memory-tools-2026) — Relevance: this is the exact class our design admits only as an opaque, `ranking_known:false` provider — Zep's cloud-only retirement is a concrete argument for never depending on one of these as infrastructure rather than a swappable provider. VERDICT: **REFUSE** as infrastructure / **WATCH** as a declared provider (trigger: evaluate Letta specifically, since its explicit/inspectable paging model is the least "black box" of the four). ◐

**MCP Tool Poisoning** (Invariant Labs, disclosed April 2025) — malicious instructions embedded in a tool's *description* metadata (not its output) are invisible to the user but fully visible to the LLM at tool-selection time; PoC against Cursor IDE exfiltrated SSH keys and MCP config via a disguised calculator tool; later benchmarking found >60% attack success across 45 real MCP servers. [Simon Willison's summary](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/9/mcp-prompt-injection/) — Relevance: directly on point for admitting an external MemPalace-style MCP provider — tool *descriptions* themselves are an untrusted-input surface, not just tool outputs. VERDICT: **TAKE** (mandate: treat every MCP tool description as untrusted content requiring the same isolation as tool output; never let a provider's declared capability-description be trusted at face value). ✅

**UCAN — User Controlled Authorization Network** (ucan-wg, spec at v1.0.0-rc.1, ongoing) — JWT-shaped, delegable, publicly-verifiable capability tokens with DIDs as principals; late-bound certificate chains let an agent delegate a narrowed subset of its own authority to another agent/device, offline-capable. [ucan.xyz/specification](https://ucan.xyz/specification/) — Relevance: exactly the attenuated-capability primitive an opaque candidate-providing agent (MemPalace or otherwise) should be handed, instead of a broad API key. VERDICT: **TAKE** (adopt UCAN-shaped attenuated delegation for any external provider's grant, scoped to "propose candidates," never "decide order"). ◐

**SLSA / in-toto / Sigstore for MCP tools** (OpenSSF-adjacent, 2025 activity) — in-toto's attestation envelope (ITE-6) is becoming the common format Sigstore and SLSA both build on; emerging guidance says MCP clients should verify signed manifests and declared capabilities against this envelope before loading a tool description into planner context at all. [secure-pipelines.com](https://secure-pipelines.com/ci-cd-security/artifact-provenance-attestations-slsa-in-toto/) — Relevance: gives us a concrete, standards-track answer to "how does a household verify a ranking-recipe artifact or a provider's declared code came from where it claims" before ever running it. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (in-toto attestation envelope as the verification shape for content-addressed ranking recipes and declared providers; skip Sigstore's CI/OIDC-specific plumbing, which assumes a centralized CA). ◐

#### What surprised me / what we may be wrong about

- Bluesky's feed-generator boundary (skeleton-only proposal, client-side hydrate/render) is *already* almost exactly our "opaque providers propose, never decide order" contract, at real production scale — worth studying their auth/DID-scoping choices more closely than we have.
- Mwmbl's direct-write-to-shared-ranking model is a live, working counter-example to "reach earned before spread" — it works for a small enthusiast community precisely because it's small; it may be evidence that our reach-gating cost is what lets a commons pool scale past enthusiast-size without collapsing into spam, not just a governance nicety.
- The IETF is converging on preference *vocabulary* while explicitly refusing to standardize *enforcement* — meaning any commons-pool admission logic we build should expect to own enforcement ourselves even after the vocabulary question is solved externally.
- We may be underweighting UCAN — it already solves "capability delegation to an opaque agent" in exactly the attenuated, offline-capable shape our provider contract needs, and it's further along (spec v1.0.0-rc.1) than we assumed.
- Holo's own token-for-mutual-credit substitution (twice delayed) is a caution about our own compute-contribution accounting: don't let "the mutual-credit engine isn't ready yet" quietly become "so we bolted on a token."
- No project we found does the *specific* combination we need — content-addressed ranking recipe + household-outward ring walk + commons pool as governed utility + private-never-pooled query. The pieces exist scattered (Bluesky's boundary, Stract's optics, in-toto's attestation, UCAN's delegation) but nobody has composed them; that composition is still our actual novelty, not a solved problem we're duplicating.

### Horizon Scan — GIS, Ads, Data Coops, Economic Pools, Commons Compute (2026-09-11)

#### 1. Open/commons GIS stack

**OpenStreetMap Foundation + ODbL** (OSMF, ongoing) — UK nonprofit legal custodian of osm.org infra; contributions licensed ODbL 1.0 (share-alike, attribution required for substantial derivative works); 10B+ nodes, 2.25M+ contributors as of Aug 2025. [osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence) — Relevance: the base facts layer our GIS pool would consume/extend; ODbL's share-alike is the license posture question we must answer for commons-pool-derived geodata. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (adopt share-alike-commons framing, not the specific license text — REA/DHT provenance can do attribution natively). ✅ fetched primary (search of osmfoundation.org).

**Overture Maps Foundation** (Linux Foundation, founded 2022 by Amazon/Meta/Microsoft/TomTom; 50 members by 2026) — a paid-membership ($3M/yr top tier) consortium that assembles/conflates OSM + commercial + gov sources into unified open-licensed map layers (buildings, places, roads, addresses). [linuxfoundation.org/press/overture-maps-foundation-reaches-50-members](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/overture-maps-foundation-reaches-50-members-as-industry-converges-on-open-data-to-ground-ai) — Relevance: shows hyperscalers already commons-pooling raw geodata for AI-grounding reasons; their governance is pay-to-play, ours is contribution-to-play. VERDICT: **WATCH** — trigger: if Overture's GERS becomes the de-facto join-key across OSS mapping tools, we may need an interop bridge even without joining the foundation. ✅ fetched primary.

**Overture GERS (Global Entity Reference System)**, launched June 2025 — 128-bit stable IDs assigned to real-world entities (2.6B buildings, 61M places, 321M road segments, 447M addresses), kept stable across releases via conflation + monthly reference-map updates; "bridge files" map GERS IDs back to source-dataset IDs. [docs.overturemaps.org/gers](https://docs.overturemaps.org/gers/) — Relevance: functionally an entity-identity layer competing with our content-addressed (CID) approach, but centrally-assigned/curated rather than derived from content — direct comparison case for our p2p-design-gate's "is identity content-derived vs agent-composite vs slug" question. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (the *need* for a stable cross-dataset entity ID is real; the *mechanism* — central authority assigning + monthly reconciliation — is exactly what CID avoids). ✅ fetched primary.

**Protomaps / PMTiles** (ongoing, v3 current) — single-file tile archive format read via HTTP range requests; a client fetches only the byte ranges (tiles/metadata) it needs directly from commodity object storage (S3/R2/GCS) — no tile server process required. [github.com/protomaps/PMTiles](https://github.com/protomaps/PMTiles/blob/main/README.md) — Relevance: this is close to a working template for our "GIS hot path with no always-on server" — a peer or storage-pool node could seed range-addressable tile archives the same way. VERDICT: **TAKE** (adopt the range-request-over-content-addressed-archive mechanism directly; map it onto our storage/blob layer). ✅ fetched primary.

**MapLibre** (fork of Mapbox GL JS post-2020 relicensing, ongoing) — open-source (BSD-3) vector-tile rendering client, the de facto pairing with PMTiles/OSM data. Not independently verified this pass but corroborated across all PMTiles/Overture sources. VERDICT: **TAKE** (rendering client, no reason to build our own). ◐ secondary.

**Valhalla / OSRM / GraphHopper** (routing engines, active 2023-2026) — three architecturally distinct OSS routing engines: OSRM trades heavy preprocessing for fastest query time (best for continental-scale matrix queries); Valhalla operates on raw OSM tiles with multi-modal costing (drive+bike+walk+transit in one query) and modest RAM (4-8GB via on-demand tile loading); GraphHopper sits between the two, Apache-2.0, profile-flexible without full rebuilds. [gis-ops.com routing overview](https://gis-ops.com/open-source-routing-engines-and-algorithms-an-overview/) — Relevance: Valhalla's on-demand-tile-loading model is the closest fit to a per-household compute-commitment node (low RAM floor, no full-graph preprocessing needed per update). VERDICT: **TAKE** (Valhalla as the routing engine on committed household compute; OSRM as a heavier pool-tier option for matrix queries). ◐ secondary (search-only, not primary-fetched).

**Panoramax** (started 2022 by French IGN + OSM France FLOSS street imagery; 12 federated instances, 105M+ images, 2100+ contributors by mid-2026) — federated street-level imagery: each org runs its own instance, metadata is harvested into a shared federated catalog (api.panoramax.xyz) while images stay hosted on the origin instance; licensing restricted to LO V2.0 or CC-BY-SA 4.0 to keep the commons open (blocks proprietary derivatives). [docs.panoramax.fr/federated-catalog](https://docs.panoramax.fr/federated-catalog/) — Relevance: this is closest working precedent to our own model — independently-run nodes, shared discovery/metadata plane, custody stays local, license enforced at federation-join time. VERDICT: **TAKE** (adopt the federated-catalog-with-local-custody pattern near-verbatim for our GIS imagery pool). ✅ fetched primary.

**Mapillary (Meta-owned since 2020) / KartaView (formerly OpenStreetCam)** — Mapillary is Meta's proprietary-platform street imagery service (still active 2025) feeding OSM edits; KartaView is the independent, OSM-community-run alternative that a sync tool mirrors Mapillary uploads into (30M+ images / 55TiB transferred as of the mirror effort) specifically as insurance against Meta shutting Mapillary down. [Mapillary/KartaView search evidence] — Relevance: a cautionary tale for depending on a single corporate-hosted imagery silo even when it's "open" today. VERDICT: **REFUSE** (Mapillary itself — single corporate control point); **WATCH** KartaView as the community-owned fallback pattern worth tracking. ◐ secondary.

**Organic Maps → CoMaps fork** (May 2025) — contributors forked Organic Maps into CoMaps after governance/financial-transparency concerns (undisclosed proprietary partnerships, shareholder-controlled funds); CoMaps commits to transparency, community decision-making, not-for-profit status, full OSS, privacy. [comaps.app/news/2025-05-12](https://www.comaps.app/news/2025-05-12/3/) — Relevance: direct evidence that even "open-source, offline-first, privacy-respecting" mapping apps need REAL governance (sortition-style assemblies, not shareholder capture) to stay trustworthy — exactly the failure mode our sortition-governed commons pools are designed to prevent. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (the offline-first client shape is good prior art; the governance failure that caused the fork is the cautionary data point, more valuable than the app itself). ◐ secondary.

#### 2. Privacy-preserving / consent-first advertising

**Brave Ads / BAT** (ongoing, 100M MAU by Oct 2025) — ads are matched *entirely on-device* from local browsing history; neither Brave nor advertisers ever see who was shown what; BAT paid to ~2M verified creators. [brave.com/brave-rewards](https://brave.com/brave-rewards/) — Relevance: on-device matching (no server-side profile) is the single mechanism most directly reusable for an internally-governed ad network — the matching model can live inside the household's own elohim agent. What's murkier from search alone: independent long-run data on user retention/advertiser satisfaction/BAT token volatility as an incentive layer. VERDICT: **TAKE-DISC** (adopt on-device matching; the BAT-token layer is a REFUSE — we have no external mint, no token markets for this). ◐ secondary.

**Google Privacy Sandbox (Topics, Protected Audience, Attribution Reporting)** — Google fully retired all ten remaining APIs Oct 17 2025 after ~6 years, citing low adoption and regulatory pressure (having already abandoned third-party-cookie deprecation in Apr 2025). [adexchanger.com](https://www.adexchanger.com/privacy/google-pulls-the-plug-on-topics-paapi-and-other-major-privacy-sandbox-apis-as-the-cma-says-cheerio/) — Relevance: the highest-resourced attempt at "privacy-preserving ads administered by the same company that profits from surveillance ads" failed — strong evidence that trust-architecture (who runs the aggregator) matters more than crypto cleverness. **Mozilla + Meta Interoperable Private Attribution (IPA)** and **IETF PPM WG / DAP + Prio** (ongoing, draft-ietf-ppm-dap up to rev 19 in 2026) — IPA uses multi-party computation so no single party (site, browser vendor, advertiser) learns individual behavior, only aggregated attribution; DAP/Prio (same IETF working group) is the general-purpose "submit an encrypted share, only the aggregate is ever decrypted" protocol family, standards-track. ## 7. Data brokers and intermediaries — the elohim as consented broker

**The elohim is a broker in two senses, and the survey found a live precedent for each.** *Internally*, it is the household's only reader: it holds the records, mediates every read, contributes declared-public content into pools, and never logs the reader. The closest legal shape is the EU Data Governance Act's **data intermediation service** ✅ (Arts. 10–12; registered, structurally separate from any use of the data, forbidden to monetize the data itself, an EU trust mark; first Dutch registrant 2025). *Externally*, it may be the consented interface through which third parties are brought honestly to the table — and the best-matched precedent is not Open Banking but **India's Account Aggregator** ✅ (RBI-regulated consent managers that never see or store the payload; data flows institution-to-institution and the AA brokers only consent artifacts; 252.9M linked users by Dec 2025). Open Banking (12M+ users, FCA-accredited third parties, consent dashboards with revocation) supplies the *UX* to borrow; the AA supplies the *architecture*.

**What the bridge guarantees, in mechanism.** A third party submits a computation, never a request for records: **compute-to-data** (Ocean's one sound idea; OpenMined's shape) executed inside the household's or the pool's own envelope; only outputs cross, and outputs pass a **safe-outputs check** before leaving (OpenSAFELY's "code goes in, checked aggregates come out" ✅). Aggregates above a sensitivity threshold carry DP noise (Census TopDown ✅ — budget for real accuracy loss). Cross-household telemetry the protocol itself needs is **Prio/DAP secret-shared** ✅ so the audit trail cannot be un-split into per-household logs. Bulk access to *governed-public* pools is a **metered pipe, never a licence** (Wikimedia Enterprise ✅). The **FTC's sensitive-location categories** (health, worship, shelters; X-Mode/InMarket/Mobilewalla/Gravy orders 2024 ✅) are a hard-refuse floor for any bridge query, not a policy knob.

**Five warnings the survey makes load-bearing:**

1. **The consent artifact is itself personal data** (CJEU C-604/22 on IAB Europe's TC String ✅). Reach declarations, grants and receipts must stay per-holon and never centrally linkable, or the protocol becomes a joint controller of the very consent it brokers.
2. **A fiduciary label without dispersed power legitimizes capture** (Khan & Pozen on Balkin's information fiduciaries ✅). The elohim's trust framing is safe only because no seat is terminal: sortition, appeal upward, no hub that can be the fiduciary of everyone. Clean rooms show the drift — 2025 saw them re-captured by single vendors (InfoSum → WPP ◐); neutrality is a governance property, not a cryptographic one.
3. **Never frame the fruits as an individual payout.** Every "get paid for your data" consumer model failed independently (Datacoup 2019, Killi→Reklaim, Delphia — SEC-charged 2024 for AI-washing ✅). The fruits are commons-held value settled at the pool, exactly as the epic says; a "data dividend" instrument is the SEC exposure Delphia hit.
4. **The honest broker is the richest target** (Gravy Analytics 2025: root access to a store tracking >1B devices ✅). There is no elohim mothership to breach because raw records are never aggregated centrally, not even for operational convenience.
5. **Trust structures imposed top-down fail on legitimacy** (Sidewalk Labs' Urban Data Trust, Toronto 2020 ✅). Pools and bridges originate from a governed community or not at all.

**Two mechanisms to take.** California's **DROP** (one request fans out to 600+ registered brokers; live Jan 2026 ✅) is the shape of a network-native "revoke this content's reach everywhere" primitive — the Living Memory epic's submerge, made to fan out across every pool that holds a projection. And the EHDS **primary/secondary purpose split** ◐ is a usable governance concept for gating a household's own use of its data (primary) against commons or third-party aggregate use (secondary).

**Threat model, named.** The internal broker is an **honest-but-curious household node**: it follows the protocol and could still infer more than intended from legitimate traffic. Read mediation and logging discipline are designed against that adversary; a fully malicious insider is the confidentiality plane's problem (encrypt-then-erasure-code, ciphertext-only relay, blind custody — backlog rows 6, 7, 9).

**Where it lands in the tree.** The bridge is a `bridges/` crate (the outward translation seam); DGA registration, AA-style consent brokering and the FTC floor are its policy surface; the toll-finance bridge spec is where metered settlement already lives; and the compute-grants bounds (`epr_scope`, `reach_ceiling: commons`, rate, rotation TTL) are already the shape of a third party's compute-to-data grant.

Roku, Perion, Lotame in 2025. [unifiedid.com](https://unifiedid.com/), [adpushup.com uid2](https://www.adpushup.com/blog/what-is-unified-id-2-0/) — Relevance: UID2's "rotating token, no raw PII in the clear" pattern is directionally aligned with content-addressed, per-context identifiers, but it is still built to make a person re-linkable for ad targeting across every participant, the opposite of never-log-the-reader. — **REFUSE**: purpose (ad re-targeting) is the thing elohim's model exists to foreclose, even though the crypto shape is reusable. ◐

**Kochava / Gravy Analytics location-data brokers (2024-2025)** — Kochava (FTC suit filed 2022, motion to dismiss denied Feb 2025, settled 2025 — banned from selling precise location without affirmative express consent) and Gravy Analytics/Venntel (FTC order Dec 2024 barring sale of sensitive-location data; then breached Jan 2025 — hacker got root/S3 access to a broker tracking >1B devices, 17B signals/day, sold in part to the US government). [hipaajournal.com](https://www.hipaajournal.com/ftcs-amended-complaint-against-kochava-survives-motion-to-dismiss/), [techcrunch.com gravy-breach](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/13/gravy-analytics-data-broker-breach-trove-of-location-data-threatens-privacy-millions/) — Relevance: the canonical proof that a rich centralized broker is a single point of catastrophic failure — directly informs §5 below. — **TAKE-DISC**: the breach is the argument for the elohim's per-household data locality (no central store to breach) — cite as the founding case for "never centralize what you mediate." ✅

#### 2. Regulation and enforcement

**FTC location-data enforcement wave (2024)** — First-ever prohibitions on sale of sensitive location data: X-Mode/Outlogic (Jan 9 2024), InMarket Media (Jan 18 2024), Mobilewalla (Dec 2024, also first-ever ban on repurposing real-time-bidding auction data for anything but the auction), Gravy/Venntel (Dec 2024). [ftc.gov mobilewalla](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-takes-action-against-mobilewalla-collecting-selling-sensitive-location-data), [eff.org 2024-review](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/12/federal-regulators-limit-location-brokers-selling-your-whereabouts-2024-review) — Relevance: defines the exact "sensitive location" carve-out (health clinics, places of worship, shelters) elohim's own "never log the reader" guarantee should treat as floor, not ceiling. — **TAKE**: mirror the FTC's sensitive-category list as a hard-refuse set for any bridge query, regardless of consent. ✅

**California Delete Act + DROP (2026 live)** — DROP (Delete Request and Opt-out Platform) went live to consumers Jan 1 2026; >300k deletion requests filed by spring; brokers (600+ registered) must process DROP requests from Aug 1 2026 or face escalating per-consumer-per-day fines (SB 361 doubled the registry fine to $200/day). [bytebacklaw.com drop-live](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2026/02/californias-deletion-request-and-opt-out-platform-drop-is-live/), [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/california-delete-act-august-1-2026-data-brokers-drop-guide) — Relevance: DROP is the first working one-request-many-brokers deletion primitive — structurally similar to what a network-wide "hold" or "revoke reach" command needs to do across all commons consumers of a record. — **TAKE-DISC**: study DROP's fan-out/verification design for the "revoke this content's reach everywhere" primitive; don't copy its centralized-registry architecture. ✅

**Vermont/Texas/Oregon broker registries (2023-2025)** — All three (plus CA) require annual state registration; fees range $100 (VT) to $6,600+ (CA); Texas AG sent 100+ non-compliance letters (June 2024) and sued Allstate/Arity (Jan 2025) for processing 50k+ people's data while unregistered. [wilmerhale.com](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20231214-texas-and-oregon-adopt-new-rules-for-data-broker-laws), [privacyrights.org](https://privacyrights.org/resources-tools/reports/why-are-hundreds-data-brokers-not-registering-states) — Relevance: registries alone are weakly enforced (hundreds don't register); a network-native reach ledger is a stronger primitive than a jurisdiction-bound registry. — **WATCH**: track whether DROP-style deletion mandates get bolted onto TX/OR/VT registries — that would be the first cross-state deletion interop signal. ◐

**Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act (2024)** — Signed into law April 24 2024 (effective June 23 2024) as part of H.R.815; bans data brokers from selling/licensing/transferring Americans' sensitive PII to China/Russia/Iran/North Korea or entities they control; enforced by FTC as an unfair/deceptive practice. [ftc.gov PADFAA](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/protecting-americans-data-foreign-adversaries-act-2024-padfaa), [wiley.law](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-Federal-Data-Broker-Law-Will-Restrict-Certain-Foreign-Data-Sales-Effective-June-23) — Relevance: precedent for jurisdiction/adversary-scoped data-sale prohibitions — a shape elohim could reuse for "never bridge to an unaccountable/non-consenting jurisdiction," but is a floor, not a governance model. — **WATCH**: informs sortition-governed community rules for bridge counterparties, not a design to adopt directly. ◐

**CFPB FCRA data-broker rule withdrawn (2025)** — Dec 2024 proposal would have classified data brokers selling income/credit/debt info as "consumer reporting agencies" under FCRA (requiring consent, purpose limits); CFPB withdrew it May 14 2025, citing "not necessary or appropriate at this time" and a changed policy stance. [federalregister.gov withdrawal](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/15/2025-08644/protecting-americans-from-harmful-data-broker-practices-regulation-v-withdrawal-of-proposed-rule) — Relevance: the US federal path to "broker = regulated fiduciary-like entity" just closed; strengthens the case that only state law (CA) and voluntary/protocol-level design currently constrain US brokers. — **WATCH**: re-open if a successor FCRA rulemaking appears; until then, treat US federal broker regulation as inert. ✅

**IAB Europe TCF — CJEU ruling (March 7, 2024, Case C-604/22)** — The TC String (records a user's ad-consent choices) is itself personal data because IAB Europe has means to link it to an individual; IAB Europe was found a joint controller for defining the framework's purposes/means (subject to Belgian Market Court verification) but NOT a joint controller for what ad-tech vendors do downstream with the string. [hunton.com](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/cjeu-rules-on-iab-europes-transparency-and-consent-framework), [iabeurope.eu cjeu-analysis](https://iabeurope.eu/cjeu-judgement-of-7-march-2024-in-tcf-case-iab-europes-analysis/) — Relevance: a sharp warning that a consent-signaling protocol can itself be ruled to process personal data and incur controller liability for the standard it defines, not just for what members do with it. — **TAKE**: design the elohim bridge's consent-receipt/audit record to NOT be a durable cross-service linkable token — the TCF string's mistake exactly. ✅

**EU Data Governance Act — data intermediation services (2024-2025)** — Since Nov 19 2024 the Dutch ACM enforces DGA compliance; the EU register of "data intermediation services" opened, first Dutch registrant approved 2025; registrants must be structurally separate from data use, may not monetize the data itself (only the intermediation service), and can display an "EU trust mark." [acm.nl first-registration](https://www.acm.nl/en/publications/data-governance-act-first-dutch-registration-data-intermediation-service-now-official), [business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/regulations/mandatory-registration-data-intermediation-service-dga/) — Relevance: this is the closest EXISTING REGULATORY CATEGORY to "elohim as neutral bridge" — registered, structurally separate, forbidden from using the data for its own purposes. — **TAKE**: use the DGA Art.10-12 neutrality/structural-separation test as the compliance target if elohim ever brokers to EU third parties; the elohim already satisfies "may not use the data" by design. ✅

**EU Data Act (in force 2025) / EHDS (in force March 2025, applies from 2027)** — Data Act strengthens B2B/IoT data portability and switching rights; EHDS (Reg. 2025/327) mandates cross-border patient data portability (Art. 7) and phases in primary/secondary-use health-data infrastructure 2027-2035. [digitalpolicyalert.org](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/3347), [kennedyslaw.com](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/the-european-health-data-space-is-in-force-implications-for-healthcare-medtech-and-life-sciences/) — Relevance: EHDS's staged "primary purpose vs. secondary/research purpose" split is a usable model for how the elohim should gate a household's own use of its data (primary) vs. commons/third-party aggregate use (secondary). — **TAKE-DISC**: borrow the primary/secondary purpose split as a governance concept, not the EU's staged bureaucratic timeline. ✅

#### 3. Consented / fiduciary / infomediary alternatives

**Hagel & Singer, "infomediary" (Net Worth, 1999)** — Coined the term for a business acting as the customer's trusted agent: aggregates and protects personal data, then markets it to businesses ON THE CUSTOMER'S BEHALF, assuming the data is the individual's property. [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infomediary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infomediary) — Relevance: this is the canonical ancestor of the "elohim as consented interface, value held in trust" design — worth citing as prior art in any spec. — **TAKE**: canonical framing; cite explicitly, but note it never specified a technical enforcement mechanism (that's the gap Holochain content-addressing fills). ◐

**Balkin, "information fiduciaries" (2016) + Khan & Pozen critique (2019)** — Balkin proposed treating platforms as fiduciaries owing care/confidentiality/loyalty duties akin to doctors/lawyers. Khan & Pozen's Harvard L. Rev. piece argues this invites regulatory complacency toward platforms' structural/market power — a fiduciary label without breaking up market dominance just legitimizes it. [harvardlawreview.org 497-541](https://harvardlawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/497-541_Online.pdf) — Relevance: directly warns against elohim's own "trust" framing becoming a legitimizing veneer if the network itself accrues centralizing power (e.g., one dominant hub-cluster). — **TAKE-DISC**: adopt fiduciary-style duties for the elohim-to-household relationship, but pair with the sortition/no-terminal-authority governance design specifically to defeat the Khan/Pozen critique (no single fiduciary accrues unchecked power). ✅

**Solid Pods / Inrupt (2025-2026)** — Tim Berners-Lee's Solid protocol: personal data stores ("pods") the individual controls, apps request scoped access. Flanders (Belgium) is rolling out a Solid Pod to every citizen for government services. [inrupt.com/solid](https://www.inrupt.com/solid) — Relevance: closest live large-scale precedent for "household holds its own records, apps/readers request scoped access" — structurally close to the elohim-as-mediator design, but Solid has no economic/REA settlement layer and no sortition governance. — **TAKE-DISC**: study Flanders' pod-per-citizen rollout as a delivery-at-scale case; the missing piece elohim adds is economic settlement + commons governance, not identity storage itself. ◐

**Datacoup (2012-2019, defunct)** — Self-described "world's first personal data marketplace" — users sold their own social/financial data feeds directly to buyers; shut down Nov 2019 for reasons not publicly detailed (buyer-side demand and per-user data pricing were widely believed too thin to sustain the company). [tracxn.com datacoup](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/datacoup/__tI-INUeR0pbaQhKci1Wyth4Nf51FabaFUl5o8wPEw24) — Relevance: the first "sell my own data directly" marketplace failed — evidence that individual-level data-for-cash markets don't clear at a price that sustains the intermediary. — **REFUSE** (as elohim's OWN model): raw per-person data-for-cash is the anti-pattern the "aggregates/answers only, never raw data" design is explicitly built to avoid. ✅

**Datacoup / Killi / Delphia data-monetization pattern (2019-2023, all wound down or pivoted)** — Killi (renamed Reklaim) and Delphia both tried "get paid for your personal data," Delphia via a token users staked into its AI trading models; SEC charged Delphia (March 2024) for "AI washing," and it shut its retail data-reward/robo-advisor business in Dec 2023, re-focusing on institutional clients only. [thelogic.co delphia-cuts](https://thelogic.co/news/data-driven-investment-startup-delphia-cuts-30-per-cent-of-its-workforce/) — Relevance: a second, independent failure mode of the "cash-for-data" consumer model — regulatory (securities-law) risk compounds the weak-demand problem Datacoup showed. — **REFUSE**: never frame the elohim's fruits-in-trust model as an individual investable "data dividend" instrument — that is the exact SEC exposure Delphia hit. ✅

**UK Open Banking / Smart Data (2025-2026)** — FCA-regulated Third Party Providers (145+ onboarded), 12M+ active users; TPPs must provide consent dashboards for review/revocation of onward sharing; FCA's Open Finance Roadmap (due March 2026) extends the model beyond banking via a "Smart Data Accelerator." [openbanking.org.uk](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/event-summary-accelerating-open-banking-and-smart-data/), [regulatorycounsel.co.uk](https://regulatorycounsel.co.uk/insights/open-finance-uk-fca-smart-data-roadmap-firms) — Relevance: the single strongest LIVE precedent for "regulated neutral intermediary, consent dashboard, scoped read access, no raw resale" at national scale (12M+ users) — closest working analogue to what the elohim's doorway/bridge should look like for a third party. — **TAKE**: model the third-party bridge's consent-dashboard UX and TPP accreditation/scoping directly on Open Banking; it is the most-proven consented-intermediary pattern in the survey. ✅

**India Account Aggregator framework (2021-2025)** — RBI-regulated "Consent Managers" (Account Aggregators) sit between financial institutions purely as consent-routing infrastructure — never see or store the underlying data themselves (data flows FI-to-FI, encrypted, AA only brokers consent artifacts). 2.61B accounts enabled, 252.9M linked users by Dec 2025; embedded into the DPDP Act 2023 as the general-purpose "Consent Manager" concept. [sahamati.org.in](https://sahamati.org.in/media-article/indias-account-aggregator-framework-crosses-100-million-consents-in-three-years/), [hyperverge.co](https://hyperverge.co/blog/account-aggregator-framework-rbi/) — Relevance: THE best precedent for elohim's exact design goal — a regulated broker that mediates consent/access WITHOUT ever holding or seeing the data itself, at national scale. — **TAKE**: the AA "never touches the payload, only brokers signed consent artifacts" architecture is close to a direct blueprint for how the elohim mediates reads without itself becoming a data store of record for others. ✅

**Australia Consumer Data Right (2025)** — Reset in 2025 after high compliance cost / low uptake: consent flows streamlined, data retention window cut from 7 years to 2, expanding from banking/energy into non-bank lending (mid-2026). [ashurst.com](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/resetting-australias-consumer-data-right/), [treasury.gov.au](https://treasury.gov.au/media-release/developments-australias-consumer-data-right-response-community-feedback) — Relevance: a cautionary tale on regulatory-intermediary cost — even a well-designed consented-sharing right can stall on compliance burden if too heavy for smaller data holders. — **WATCH**: watch the non-bank-lending expansion (mid-2026) as a signal of whether the "reset, lighter-touch" version actually lifts uptake; informs how much governance overhead elohim's bridge protocol can bear before adoption stalls. ◐

**Streamr Data Unions / Swash / Ocean Protocol compute-to-data (2025)** — Streamr's data-union smart contracts pool many users' browsing/behavioral data streams (Swash) and split buyer payments pro-rata per contributor automatically; Ocean Protocol's "Compute-to-Data" lets a buyer run compute against private data without ever receiving the raw dataset. [medium.com streamr data-unions](https://medium.com/streamrblog/what-are-data-unions-how-do-they-work-which-ones-can-i-use-887e67fb7716), [streamr.network/case-studies/swash](https://streamr.network/case-studies/swash/) — Relevance: Ocean's compute-to-data is architecturally the same shape as "third parties get answers, never raw data" (see §4); Streamr's pooled-and-split payment is a REA-compatible settlement mechanic worth studying even though token-speculative data unions haven't proven durable non-speculative demand. — **TAKE-DISC**: adopt compute-to-data as the mechanism (§4); treat token-based data-union payout as a settlement-mechanics reference only, not a model to copy (crypto-speculative incentive layer is orthogonal to REA). ◐

#### 4. Bringing third parties honestly to the table

**Ocean Protocol Compute-to-Data** — Buyer submits an algorithm; it executes inside the data holder's environment against the private dataset; only the algorithm's output (never the raw data) leaves. [oceandec.devpost.com](https://oceandec.devpost.com/details/hackerguide) — Relevance: structurally the mechanism the elohim bridge needs — a third party submits a query/computation, the household's elohim executes it locally, only the result crosses. — **TAKE**: adopt compute-to-data as the literal bridge mechanism; the household's elohim is the "compute environment," never the data exporter. ◐

**Five Safes / Trusted Research Environments (UK ONS, SAIL, OpenSAFELY)** — Access is governed jointly across five axes (safe people/projects/settings/data/outputs); OpenSAFELY specifically ships researcher CODE INTO the NHS data environment and only checked, aggregate outputs come back out — "code to data," never data to researcher. [bennett.ox.ac.uk](https://www.bennett.ox.ac.uk/blog/2023/03/the-five-safes-framework-and-applying-it-to-opensafely/), [casrai.org](https://casrai.org/guides/trusted-research-environments-five-safes-framework) — Relevance: OpenSAFELY's "code goes in, only checked outputs come out" is the most rigorous real precedent for what the elohim bridge should guarantee for a third party's query. — **TAKE**: the "safe outputs" human/automated disclosure check before results leave is a control the elohim bridge should also apply to prevent aggregate queries from reconstructing individual records. ✅

**US Census 2020 TopDown Algorithm (differential privacy)** — Formal DP mechanism (zero-concentrated DP) adds calibrated statistical noise to every published statistic; the largest-stakes deployment of trusted-curator-model DP to date (apportionment, redistricting, federal funding all ride on the output). [hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu topdown](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/7evz361i) — Relevance: proof that formal DP can be deployed at national scale for high-stakes releases, but also the DP accuracy/privacy tradeoff is a live, contested cost — a useful realism check on how much noise commons-pool aggregates would need. — **TAKE-DISC**: adopt DP-style noise injection for commons aggregate releases above a sensitivity threshold, but budget for real accuracy loss, not a free lunch. ✅

**Prio / Divvi Up / DAP (ISRG, IETF)** — Each client splits a metric into secret shares sent to 2+ non-colluding aggregation servers; as long as at least one server is honest, no server learns anything beyond the final aggregate. Deployed in Firefox and Apple/Google Exposure Notifications. [divviup.org introducing-prio](https://divviup.org/blog/introducing-prio-services/), [lwn.net](https://lwn.net/Articles/983843/) — Relevance: exactly the "privacy-preserving aggregate audit" pattern named in the prompt — usable for the elohim's own internal audit/telemetry (proving the network is healthy) without the audit log itself becoming a surveillance record. — **TAKE**: adopt Prio/DAP-style secret-shared aggregation for any cross-household health/usage telemetry the protocol itself needs, so the audit trail cannot be un-split into per-household logs. ✅

**Wikimedia Enterprise metered bulk access (2025-2026)** — Not a licensing model — an access-based metered pipe: same CC-licensed content, paying customers (Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft) get QPS/throughput SLAs and egress-based billing rather than buying rights to the data itself. [enterprise.wikimedia.com/pricing](https://enterprise.wikimedia.com/pricing/), [wikimediafoundation.org cost-of-free](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/07/16/wikimedia-enterprise-protecting-wikipedia-ai/) — Relevance: a working precedent for "third parties pay for reliable access to already-public/commons content, never for exclusive rights to it" — maps directly onto declared-public commons content a third party wants at bulk/API scale. — **TAKE**: model third-party access to elohim's GOVERNED-PUBLIC commons pools on Wikimedia Enterprise's metered-pipe billing, not on a data-licensing sale. ✅

**Sidewalk Labs Urban Data Trust (Toronto, 2018-2020, failed)** — Proposed a fiduciary-style "civic data trust" for smart-city sensor data; collapsed May 2020 after Google/Sidewalk withdrew, following sustained public distrust. Post-mortems found it lacked clarity on what problem it solved, weak accountability/oversight mechanisms, and relied on "de-identification" as a privacy strategy instead of real fiduciary duties. [ojs.library.queensu.ca](https://ojs.library.queensu.ca/index.php/surveillance-and-society/article/view/14409) — Relevance: the clearest cautionary case that a "data trust" concept announced top-down by a single corporate sponsor, without a pre-existing governed community, fails on legitimacy grounds alone regardless of technical design. — **TAKE-DISC**: the lesson is procedural, not technical — elohim's sortition-governed communities must originate and own the trust structure themselves; a data trust imposed by the platform operator (even elohim's own core team) repeats Sidewalk's mistake. ✅

#### 5. Internal-broker security

**"Honest-but-curious" (semi-honest) adversary model in MPC** — Standard cryptographic assumption: participants follow the protocol exactly but try to infer extra information from legitimately received messages; weaker (cheaper) than the malicious-adversary model but the default for most deployed privacy-preserving-computation systems (e.g. JIFF-based federated query engines). [boazbarak.org lecture17](https://www.boazbarak.org/cs127spring16/chap17_sfe) — Relevance: this is the exact threat model an elohim node occupies toward its own household — it follows the protocol, but a compromised or curious node operator could try to infer more from legitimate traffic than the design intends. — **TAKE**: name "honest-but-curious household node" explicitly as elohim's threat model and design read-mediation/logging discipline against it (never against a fully malicious insider, which needs the heavier and costlier malicious-model guarantees). ✅

**Prio/DAP secret-shared aggregation (repeat cross-ref from §4)** — Its "no single server ever sees an individual's value, only aggregates, and only if at least one server stays honest" property is precisely an audit design that resists becoming surveillance. — Relevance: direct architectural answer to "audit logging that does not itself become surveillance." — **TAKE**: reuse (see §4 entry) for elohim's own health telemetry. ✅

**Gravy Analytics breach (2025) as the single-richest-target case (repeat cross-ref from §1)** — A centralized broker holding >1B devices' worth of location trails was root-compromised via its own cloud storage; this is the textbook "the honest broker becomes the biggest target" failure. [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/13/gravy-analytics-data-broker-breach-trove-of-location-data-threatens-privacy-millions/) — Relevance: direct justification for per-household data locality — there is no central "elohim mothership" store to breach the way Gravy's S3 buckets were breached. — **TAKE**: cite Gravy as the canonical argument, in any design doc, for why the elohim never aggregates raw records centrally even for its own internal operational convenience. ✅

**Clean-room insider-risk controls (repeat cross-ref from §1)** — k-anonymity thresholds + DP noise + (InfoSum) non-movement processing are the industry's current insider-risk mitigations inside a clean room; none of them fully stop a curious clean-room OPERATOR (as opposed to the querying party) from seeing raw joins, which is why 2025 saw clean-room consolidation into single-vendor-controlled stacks (InfoSum → WPP) rather than neutral parties. [adexchanger.com clean-room-era](https://www.adexchanger.com/measurement/why-2025-marked-the-end-of-the-data-clean-room-era/) — Relevance: warns that "clean room" branding alone doesn't guarantee neutrality — the operator/steward matters as much as the cryptographic technique. — **TAKE-DISC**: adopt the cryptographic techniques, but insist the neutrality guarantee comes from elohim's per-household execution + sortition governance, not from a trusted-operator promise the way commercial clean rooms rely on. ✅

#### What surprised me / what we may be wrong about

- The US federal path essentially died in 2025 (CFPB rule withdrawn); only California's DROP and four state registries carry real teeth right now — the design should not assume US federal broker law will constrain anything for elohim's own external bridge.
- The EU's Data Governance Act "data intermediation service" category is a much closer regulatory fit for elohim-as-bridge than I expected — worth an explicit compliance mapping if EU third parties are ever in scope.
- Every "get paid for your own data" consumer model surveyed (Datacoup, Killi, Delphia) failed independently — this is strong evidence AGAINST framing fruits-in-trust as an individual payout instrument, and FOR framing it as commons-held value, which is what the brief already proposes; treat that as validated, not just aspirational.
- India's Account Aggregator (not Open Banking) is the best-matched existing precedent — a consent-only broker that never touches payload — yet it wasn't in my prior mental model as the top comparison; it should probably outrank Open Banking as the primary cited precedent even though Open Banking has better consent-dashboard UX to borrow.
- Clean rooms are trending toward re-capture by single vendors (InfoSum/WPP) rather than staying neutral — a warning that "neutral third-party infrastructure" is not a stable equilibrium under commercial incentives; sortition governance is elohim's actual defense against this drift, more than any cryptographic technique.
- Sidewalk Labs' failure was social/legitimacy, not technical — a reminder that no amount of good cryptography (compute-to-data, DP, MPC) rescues a trust structure imposed top-down without a governed community that originates it.

## 8. Open questions — for co-authoring ❓

1. **Why run an announcement market at all?** Mastodon refuses ads entirely and lives on patronage; canon already models patronage as structurally equal to stewardship. *Recommend:* not "ads" — an **announcement market**: a seller pays the commons to be *announced* to people who declared an interest, matched on the person's own device (Brave shape), contextual only (EthicalAds shape), measured only in aggregate (DAP/Prio), with no engagement objective, the reader never logged, the fruits to the pool, and revenue capped. The justification is the epic's: attention is the reader's; a market that pays the *commons* rather than an *engine* is the announcement column of the manifesto's values-dimension, not the ad column it refuses. If that justification does not convince you, refuse it as Mastodon does and the design loses nothing structural.
2. **First pool to build.** *Recommend:* the street-imagery pool (Panoramax federated catalog + Observer-Protocol derivation at sensor + PMTiles-style archives), because it exercises contribution, provenance, derivation-as-privacy, the hot path, and the bridge in one corpus you already feel the loss of.
3. **Where does the pool primitive live?** *Recommend:* Collective EPR + `replicates-*`/`delegates-compute` commitments (Class A/A2, zero new entry types), `PoolConfig` generalized, scope `pool:<id>` on grants. Run the P2P design gate on it before the first line.
4. **The revenue cap number.** Wikimedia uses 30% of total funding. *Recommend:* declare it as a DNA-locked ceiling like the donut's, per pool kind, and make crossing it a printed red rather than a policy.
5. **Who may train on a pool, and on what terms.** *Recommend:* contribution terms carry reciprocity · recognition · sustainability (CC Signals axes) as first-class fields; a model trained on the pool is a pool asset with a CID; enforcement is ours (the IETF will not supply it).
6. **Per-holon provider grants now, or after station 3?** *Recommend:* after the standing reader is green — the per-agent allowlist is adequate for the sparring partner; the grant shape is already `compute_grants`' bounds, so the move is a rename, not a design.
