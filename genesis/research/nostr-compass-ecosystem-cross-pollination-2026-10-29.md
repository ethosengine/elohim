# Nostr Compass Cross-Pollination — October 2026

**[Nostr Compass](https://nostrcompass.org/en/about/)** describes itself as *"a technical resource for the Nostr protocol"*: a weekly newsletter (#1 on 2025-12-17, #46 on 2026-10-28) covering NIP proposals, client and relay releases, and notable code changes. Around it sit a weekly podcast with the developers each issue covers, a 134-page topic index linking to primary sources, a NIP reference, a kind registry and a tracked-project list of 440+ entries. It publishes in ten languages, is MIT-licensed, is *"not to make a profit"* and takes Lightning donations. Every issue is also signed as a NIP-23 long-form event (kind 30023) and broadcast to relays ✅ (`README.md`, `content/en/about.md`, `STRATEGY.md`).

We read it for two reasons, and the survey is split the same way:

1. **As a shape (Part I).** Compass is an *agent-run editorial pipeline*. Its repository holds `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, a `skills/_COMPASS/` package of ten agents, about forty scripts (ten fetcher families, the editorial gates, and publish, translation and build utilities), and a publish stage driven by receipts. It is the closest outside exhibit we have of our own development machinery pointed at a different product: a weekly, sourced account of a protocol's evolution. It has gates per stage, ledgers that refuse silent skips, logs derived from receipts, and rules born from incidents, many naming the issue that failed.
2. **As a map (Part II).** Compass's topic index and project list are the cheapest current index of the Nostr protocol surface. We used it to choose and clone the projects that bear directly on our seams. These are E2E-encrypted collectives with locally verified rosters (Concord, Marmot), team signing (Keycast, FROST/NIP-340), AI agents as owner-attested actors (Block's Buzz), MCP over Nostr (ContextVM), sandboxed applets that never hold keys (napplets), mesh routing on key identities (FIPS), set reconciliation (negentropy), typed kind schemas (schemata) and trade credit as signed bills (Bitcredit).

The one-line verdict: **take Compass's evidence discipline almost whole. Its rules (a skip is legitimate but a silent skip is not; the publication log is generated from the run's receipts and never hand-edited; only the deploy matched to the merge commit is credited; the `--really-*` switches confirm intent and grant no authority) are ours already, stated more sharply and gated more mechanically in places where we still rely on memory. Leave its trust root: the receipts gate Compass's own pipeline, but what a reader on Nostr can verify is only that one key signed, as everywhere in Nostr. Part II shows the same split across the ecosystem. Nostr ships group, agent and app forms we have designed but not shipped, on a substrate where an owner key is terminal.**

Method: `andotherstuff/nostr-compass` shallow-cloned at `3034ee8` (2026-10-28) and read directly (README, STRATEGY, CLAUDE, AGENTS, `skills/_COMPASS/`, `publish/README.md`, the gate scripts, 134 topic pages). The prior-art set was chosen from the topic index and `data/projects.yml` / `data/nip34_tracked.yml`, then shallow-cloned (heads listed under Outputs). Three parallel read-only passes each read one cluster of clones and grounded it against our tree. The orchestrating session synthesised the results, re-verified the security finding and the recovery stubs on disk, and read the takes through the p2p-design-gate, local-first and gradient-reading lenses and the epr / eprfs / brit / rakia foundations. Adversarial pass: a read-only Codex (gpt-5.6-sol) cite check of Part I tested 103 quoted and numeric claims against source and corrected 16, among them a misreading of Compass's readback rule (it reads from the relays that accepted, not from a non-writer) and a banned-phrase list that the in-repo script does not enforce. Part II received the same check: 187 claims, 19 corrected, among them our `Claim` (it has transfer vocabulary; what is missing is validation), the compute grant (it authorizes and carries no price), and our sync opener's converged cost (O(corpus) compute every round, not O(1)). **Verification key:** ✅ verified in primary source · ◐ single-source / plausible · ⚠ web-only or roadmap claim.

# Part I — Compass as a shape: the agent-run chronicle

## Anatomy

### 1. The pipeline: stages that hand off through files, read by their gate line ✅

`/newsletter <links + notes>` starts a Tuesday run. The stages are Pre-flight → **IntakeAgent** (parse submitted URLs, verify repos, dedup) → **Fetch** (`fetch_all.sh`: ten source families covering tracked-repo releases and merged PRs, NIP discussions on relays, NIP-34 repos, Zapstore listings, NIP-89 handlers, grantee heartbeats and a GitHub discovery sweep) → **TriageAgent** (GREEN / MAYBE / SKIP per item) → **NewsletterAgent** (select mode, then parallel section writers) → Assembly → **ReviewSwarmAgent** (five parallel reviewers: LinkChecker, ClaimCheck, ProseReview, TopicAudit, ContinuityValueCheck, looping back to the writers until all pass) → draft PR → Wednesday refresh windows (13:00, 14:30, ≥15:30 cutoff) → **PublishAgent** at ≥16:00 UTC → **TranslationAgent** (nine languages) (`skills/_COMPASS/SKILL.md` §Agent Architecture).

Three structural rules carry it:

- **Stage gate contract.** Every agent's workspace artifact ends `GATE: PASS`, `GATE: FAIL — <blocker>` or `GATE: HUMAN-INPUT-REQUIRED — <question>`. *"The Orchestrator stays lightweight because it reads only the `GATE:` line of each artifact."*
- **Context isolation.** *"Raw JSON fetch files are 1-2 MB and must never enter agent context directly. Always extract via `jq` or `grep`."* Each agent *"runs with fresh context and reads only the workspace files it owns."*
- **Lane separation.** The review swarm *"MUST run from the review lane. Never run this stage from, or reuse, the Stage 5 writing lane. Record the reviewer model"* (`agents/ReviewSwarmAgent.md`).

Each issue is drafted in its own git worktree on `newsletter/<date>`, and *"an ambiguous worktree or branch collision is a hard stop"*.

### 2. Coverage ledgers: every release gets a decision ✅

The motivating incident: newsletter #37 lost `formstr-hq/nail` v0.1.0. The release sat in the fetch JSON, and two independent sources had caught it, but *"the aggregate summary hid both"*. The fix is a **release digest** that names every release in the window, plus a blocking gate (`check_triage_coverage.py --strict`) that fails while any flagged release is named nowhere. *"Skipping a release is a legitimate editorial choice; skipping it silently is not."* A project may be omitted only when it was covered in the previous issue *and* shipped nothing substantive. The digest computes that as `suppression_allowed`, so the decision is mechanical. A project-family mention does not satisfy a specific release (CLAUDE.md §Every release…).

Selection then runs through a candidate ledger with no item cap. A candidate passes only on direct primary evidence, material in-window progress, a concrete Nostr surface and a distinct continuity delta, then needs ≥8/10 with no zero across five quality scores. `check_selection_coverage.py` binds the source-pass manifest, the decisions file and the draft into a receipt, so *"a stale input/decision hash blocks review."*

### 3. Prose gates: sourced claims, scope gates, no filler ✅

Blocking scripts check that every prose paragraph carries a primary-source link (`check_newsletter_paragraph_links.py`), that back-references resolve, that example events are valid NIP-01 events (*"Recompute the NIP-01 id and verify the BIP-340 signature"*), that topic pages backlink the issue, and that each Protocol-and-Spec H3 covers exactly one changed PR (`check_newsletter_style.py`). Banned meta-frames ("worth noting", "one to watch") and superlatives are policy (`CLAUDE.md` §Banned…), enforced by an external anti-pattern scanner run before each push, not by the in-repo blocking script. The **per-item Nostr-surface gate** cuts every Lightning-only, Cashu-only or dependency-bump line even from Nostr-aware projects: *"every Lightning/Cashu/Bitcoin-only line is a defect, not a stylistic choice."* A NIP deep dive may cover only *"a merged specification with verified use in multiple independent applications"* and must explain at least three implementations from their source.

### 4. Publication: authority from receipts, effects verified by independent readback ✅

*"Scheduled mutation is receipt-gated."* Merge requires the scoped edition authorization, the current hold version, a source digest, a feedback snapshot, review receipts and exact-head CI, all bound to one PR/head/base/prospective-tree identity. When the publisher lacks admin rights for strict status checks, the pipeline fetches GitHub's exact two-parent prospective merge commit, verifies base, head and tree, and fast-forward pushes that immutable commit, so *"Git rejects the push if the base advanced."* **DEPLOY** credits only the Pages run attributable to the merge SHA (*"a later unrelated deploy on `main` must not be credited"*). **SIGN** is refused until merge and deploy are confirmed, and signing goes through a NIP-46 bunker. Keys are never in the repo. **BROADCAST** records per-relay receipts, then *"independently recovers the exact events from the required durable relay floor"* (≥5), with the write-only NIP-66 relay excluded from readback evidence. **LOG** is derived: *"The publication log used to be written by hand, and both ways of getting that wrong happened within two issues… Do not hand-edit the log to make it pass."* *"The `--really-*` flags confirm execution intent; they never grant authority."* (`publish/README.md`).

One more boundary: *"Repository publication and translation code never sends user-facing milestone messages. The host-owned durable outbox/reconciler is the sole notification producer"*, so a retried stage cannot double-post.

### 5. State that must outlive a worktree lives outside it ✅

The discovery baselines (`seen_repos.json`, Zapstore's `publishers_seen.yml`) once lived under gitignored `data/`. Every issue runs in a fresh worktree, so *"for months the baselines never existed at run time"*. In #37 alone that discarded 622 Nostr-relevant releases as first-run noise and produced 603 discovery candidates where a primed baseline produced 14 (*"43× less noise"*). They now resolve under `$COMPASS_STATE_DIR` outside the repository. `migrate_discovery_state.py` takes the **union** across worktrees: *"Union is the safe direction: it can only suppress a stale 'new' flag, never invent one."*

### 6. Discovery has owner-shaped blind spots ✅

Per-repo tracking missed a new repo from a team with five tracked repos: it had no topics, was created five months earlier and its description named no recognised noun. The fix is an **owner sweep**: an owner who already ships a tracked project is evidence in itself. Siblings still arrive `evidence_status: unconfirmed`, *"candidate-only; never auto-add"*. Signed NIP-89/Zapstore descriptors prove *"only that a key published the descriptor"*, and relay multiplicity is *"transport-availability evidence only"*. Global kind-31989 recommendations are excluded outright because *"Compass has no explicit trusted-author/follow-graph set; a global recommendation sweep would be sybil-prone."*

### 7. The cost it pays (what our own CLAUDE.md warns about) ✅

The rulebook is incident-shaped. Thirteen sections of `CLAUDE.md` and seven of `SKILL.md` are marked CRITICAL, and the rules cite at least eleven past issues by number (#11, #12, #15, #20, #23, #24, #27, #28, #29, #36, #37). `scripts/` holds 40 tools, seven of them dated one-offs (`augment_final_selection_2026_09_23.py`, `build_selection_coverage_wednesday_2026_09_23.py`, …). `data/newsletter_workspace/` holds 255 files at its top level and 460 tracked files recursively. This is the accretion our root `CLAUDE.md` names as *"instruments with no reader"*. Compass is a second, independent exhibit that a receipt-gated agent pipeline grows its gates out of incidents, and that the dated one-off script is where that growth leaks first.

## Take / study / leave / watch — Part I

- **T1 (take): the triage-coverage gate as the mint pass's missing drain.** At close, a survey's graded verdicts are a digest. Every take must be named in a cluster row *or* in an explicit "dies here because…" line, and a script fails the close otherwise. This is the mechanical mint pass our `.epr-meta` `retire-when` is waiting for, and Compass shows it costs one small script plus one rule ("a family mention does not satisfy a specific item").
- **T2 (take): a readback separate from the write acknowledgement** for the blob-forward deploy proof. "Accepted" is the store's word at write time. "Served back by exact id on a fresh read, from the store itself rather than a cache in front of it" is the evidence. Feeds `blob-forward-confirmation-status-only-no-body-check`.
- **T3 (take): owner-shaped discovery for horizon scans.** Index external sources by *steward* (a team, author or forge account) as well as by repo, so a new repo from a known steward surfaces as a candidate. Candidate-only, verified before it is written up, exactly as Compass does.
- **S1 (study): worktree-resident baselines.** Check whether `ci-harvest` run from a `.worktrees/` session re-mints known fingerprints, and whether `.eprfs` sidecar state has the same exposure. Compass's union-merge rule is the safe direction if it does.
- **S2 (study): a uniform last-line gate contract** for agent workspace artifacts. It is cheap and readable by a thin orchestrator, but it may duplicate valueflow verbs. Adopt only where an artifact has no verb.
- **L1 (leave): one key as the publication authority.** Compass's whole receipt chain ends in signatures from *one* NIP-46 bunker key (two events: the kind-30023 article and the kind-1 announcement). That is right for a newsletter and wrong for a commons record, where recognition comes from governance-recognized standing and not from the key that signed.
- **L2 (leave): dated one-off scripts.** Seven in `scripts/`. Our analogue is an instrument with no reader. Fold a one-off into the gate it patched, or delete it at the next issue.
- **W1 (watch): Compass as a horizon-scan source.** It is the cheapest weekly feed of what the Nostr ecosystem shipped, already sorted and sourced. Our `.claude/horizon-scan-sources.md` is outside this session's write-set, so the addition is recorded under Outputs.

# Part II — The prior art Compass points at

Clones (shallow, 2026-10-29): concord `f84d555`, marmot `a3edd1e`, keycast `6c5a2fe`, buzz `e922dc0` (sparse: `docs/` plus root docs), contextvm-sdk `3949fe3`, naps `5aadf1b`, fips `26cfb4f`, negentropy `b368765`, schemata `e784d95` (ngit/GRASP-hosted), bitcredit-core `2ea20bf`. Compass topic pages that drift from their sources: `concord-protocol.md` stops at CORD-07, but the repo ships CORD-08 (disappearing messages). `keycast.md` describes a pre-rebuild architecture (NIP-98 management auth, one daemon per authorization), while HEAD refuses NIP-98 for management and uses private kinds 27236/27237 (`keycast/docs/security.md:24`). `nip-340.md` attributes FROST to `niccokunzmann/igloo`, which looks wrong ⚠. `nap-fs.md` cites a NAP-FS that is not at naps HEAD (open PR #88) ⚠.

## The common shape, read through our foundations

The Nostr-native projects below (all but negentropy, a general reconciliation protocol, and FIPS, whose Nostr discovery is optional) are built from the same three parts: a **signed event**, a **relay** that checks signature and tag shape plus whatever admission policy its operator sets, and a **client** that interprets everything else. Set beside our four foundations, the differences sort into four cuts. Most of the verdicts in this Part follow from them.

**Cut 1. Identity fuses authorship and clock; the EPR atom does not.** A Nostr event id is `sha256([0, pubkey, created_at, kind, tags, content])`, so the signer and the signer's clock are *inside* the identity (NIP-01, ✅ `nostr-compass/content/en/topics/nip-01.md`). An EPR's CIDv1 (dag-cbor) is computed over the canonical envelope *excluding* `cid`, `proof` and `supersededBy`. Two stewards signing identical content get the same CID, and signatures ride the detached `proof` (`elohim/epr/src/envelope.rs`, per the p2p-design-gate's anti-pattern note ✅). In gradient terms this is a **fused plane**: Nostr cannot hold "this content" apart from "this person said it". So every Nostr project ends up making a key the authority: Concord's owner, Marmot's admin pubkey, Keycast's hosted nsec, Buzz's owner key. Our content plane and authority plane are separate by construction. Authority comes from *governance-recognized standing*, and a key signature is evidence of it (`elohim/eprfs/epr-cli/src/device_key.rs:12-15`; *"a self-signature is not participant standing"*).

**Cut 2. Interpretation lives in every client; ours belongs in one validator.** Relays check signatures, tag shape and their own admission policy, but not the protocol's authority rules, so each client re-derives authority. Concord's clients fold a roster of editions locally, and Marmot's clients enforce the admin policy. That is *trustless and verifying*: the ceremony is in the right place, but it is paid by every reader and upgraded in lockstep (the ngit survey's L3). Our design puts the check in the integrity zome, once, at commit. **The grounding found that for collectives we currently have neither.** The imagodei integrity zome validates every `CreateLink` and `DeleteLink` unconditionally, including `HasMembership` (`imagodei_integrity/src/lib.rs:1175-1176`). A `Steward` Membership with the literal `sponsor_cid: "founder"` passes integrity for any collective (`qahal.rs:103-110`). The steward check is a list scan inside the hot-swappable coordinator (`qahal_coordinator.rs:554-570`). This is already canonized as rows 1, 4 and 14 of [arch-authority-in-integrity-backlog](epr:arch-authority-in-integrity-backlog). Concord is the outside exhibit of what our read-side verification would have to be if integrity stays silent.

**Cut 3. Ordering: signer clock vs declared heads.** Plain Nostr resolves addressable events by greatest `created_at`. **Concord independently arrived at our answer:** editions carry an explicit `version`, a `prev` hash chain and a downgrade refusal, and every reader drops whatever does not trace to the root (✅ `concord/04.md:11,54,81,103`). That is supersedes-with-expected-prior, the same shape as eprfs-peer's `ChannelHead {device, root, seq}`, where `at` is *"display only"* (`elohim/eprfs/eprfs-peer/src/head.rs:3-6`), and as the CAS the ngit survey exported. Buzz's NIP-OA likewise refuses to enforce expiry on the wall clock, because the agent controls `created_at` (✅ `buzz/docs/nips/NIP-OA.md:98-101`). The ecosystem is learning, one project at a time, that a signer's clock is not an order.

**Cut 4. Succession: when the key is gone for good, Nostr has no next step.** Concord: *"a lost owner key can never be replaced… nsec theft is terminal"* (✅ `concord/02.md:27`). Marmot: if every admin loses their keys there is *"no in-band succession, override, or automatic-promotion"*, and members start a new group (✅ `marmot/…/admin-policy-v1.md:156-160`). Both projects tie a group's life to a human-held key. Our anticipated commons pools have no key at all and no direct human steward (pool spec: *"Nobody holds a pool's key, because a pool has none"*). NIP-26 delegation is marked *unrecommended*. In local-first terms ("what still works when it is gone for good?"), Nostr's answer is *nothing that was governed*. Ours is a governance act backstopped by community witness. **We should say plainly that it is designed, not built:** three `recovery_v2` variants are still stub-rejected (`imagodei/…/recovery_v2.rs:74-101`). This is the cleanest differentiator in the survey and also our least-built one.

## Anatomy by seam

### Collectives and private collective data: Concord, Marmot (qahal × confidentiality plane)

**Concord** (CORD-01..08). `community_id = sha256("concord/community" ‖ owner_xonly ‖ owner_salt)`. Holding `community_root` makes someone a member. A separate staff-held `control_root` gates writes to the control plane, and it is *"a spam gate, never authority"* (✅ `02.md:9-11,41,162-167`). Authority is in kind-3308 editions (entity, version, `prev`, and a `vac` tag naming the grant the actor claims rank under). Rank is strict, with no all-powerful bit: an actor must outrank its target (✅ `04.md:107-128`). Removal bumps the key epoch through kind-3303 rekey blobs, and a **Refounding** rotates the whole community (✅ `06.md:1-13`). Relays see ciphertext under **rotating labels**, so an observer outside the community cannot read who belongs to it ◐ (Compass page plus CORD-02 reading).

**Marmot** (MLS, RFC 9420, over Nostr). Each device is its own MLS leaf, and MLS state is never copied between devices. A kind-450 proof binds the account to each leaf's signing key (✅ `foundation/identity.md:5-33`, `account-identity-proof-v2.md:12-17`). Group component 0x8003 holds a sorted, non-empty admin list, and every admin must still be a member. That is a **whole-state invariant**, checked on the result and not just on the delta (✅ `admin-policy-v1.md:13-41,84-110`). Wire kinds: 445 messages, 30443 key packages, 1059 welcomes. The device group and device roster are still drafts, and *"your nsec is still everything"* (✅ `ideas/multi-device.md:30`).

### Delegated authority: Keycast, FROST / NIP-340, NIP-4E, NIP-26 (imagodei device and stewardship)

**Keycast** (self-hosted NIP-46 team signer). Team → stored key → policy → grant → invitation → session. Policies are explicit and default-deny (✅ `core/src/v2/policy.rs:9-46`). **No grant can sign the management kinds**, so a hosted key can never approve its own administration (✅ `policy.rs:103-112`). Management approvals are bound to instance, revision, nonce, method, URL, body hash and a fresh reply key (✅ `docs/security.md:21-35`). **Policies are saved values, not live references**, so a catalog edit cannot silently widen an existing grant (✅ `docs/policies-and-access.md:67-69`). There has been no independent audit (✅ README). **FROST / NIP-340**: t-of-n Schnorr signing under one group key ⚠ (open NIP PR). **NIP-4E** ⚠ (open draft) gives a user's devices a shared encryption key separate from the identity key, so a FROST- or enclave-held identity can still read its own encrypted lists. **NIP-26** delegation tokens are marked unrecommended, the field's own admission that a bare signature is not proof of delegation.

### Agents as accountable actors: Buzz (eprfs actor plane × brit covenant)

Block's Buzz writes a project NIP for each question our actor plane is working on. **NIP-OA**: one `["auth", owner, conditions, sig]` tag. The owner signs `SHA256("nostr:agent-auth:" ‖ agent_pubkey ‖ ":" ‖ conditions)` with conditions limited to `kind=`, `created_at<` and `created_at>`, which are verified byte-for-byte. **The agent stays the author**, clients must not merge its events into the owner's timeline, and self-attestation is rejected (✅ `docs/nips/NIP-OA.md:15,32-70,84-87`). **NIP-AA**: an agent can hold temporary relay membership through its owner, but role checks still look at the agent's own key and it can never become an admin (✅ `NIP-AA.md:113,131,137`). **NIP-AM** (`kind:44200`): one usage record per agent turn, NIP-44-encrypted to the owner. **A missing count is null, never zero**, and a `pricingIdentity` appears only when the publisher can prove it, never guessed from the model name (✅ `NIP-AM.md:46-120`). **NIP-AO** (`24200`): live session telemetry that relays *must not* store, index or audit-log (✅ `NIP-AO.md:34,130-133`). **NIP-AP** (`30175`): a public persona blueprint that carries no secrets (✅). **NIP-FI**: an agent identity token must name an owner key different from the agent's own, and a ban on either one denies the agent (✅ `NIP-FI.md:137-148,931`). *"Discovery alone does not authorize a message"* (✅ `docs/owned-agent-discovery.md`).

Our counterparts. Agent claims (`agent:<role>@<model>`) are **unsigned**, and only the human claim is device-signed (`elohim/eprfs/epr-cli/src/actor.rs:129`). Our NIP-OA analogue is the **brit session covenant**: clause kinds `affirmsStewardOfRecord`, `declaresPurpose`, `variesRule` and `revokesClause` (`epr-cli/src/covenant.rs:19-34`), each recorded with a signature, a validity window and the human's own words. This session's two clauses came back from `epr covenant record` as `bafyreieauiwctv…` and `bafyreidr2u7ed…` (our record output, not a repo file). Its root is a covenant a human declared, not an owner key. Our compute grant is *stronger* than NIP-OA as an authorization: a notarized `delegates-compute` record pinned by action hash, with a lifecycle written by the provider (`elohim-storage/src/api/compute_tasks.rs:209-285`). **Per-turn usage has no home at all.** No token or cost field exists in `elohim/eprfs`, `.claude/hooks` or `genesis/agentic` (grep), and only the model id is recorded.

### Agent traffic and apps: ContextVM, napplets (doorway × EPR app bundles)

**ContextVM** carries MCP JSON-RPC in `kind 25910`, optionally gift-wrapped (`1059` / ephemeral `21059`), with responses correlated by an `e` tag (✅ `src/core/constants.ts:9-22,83`). Server announcements are explicitly *not* access control (✅ `nostr-server-transport.ts:78-90`). Authorization is a key allow-list with per-capability exclusions. **The inner event's signature is re-verified after decryption** (✅ `event-pipeline.ts:113-118`). CEP-8 attaches Lightning prices to capabilities (✅ `payments/cap-tags.ts:22-27`). Our MCP is stdio only (`elohim-agent/mcp-servers/elohim-content/src/index.ts:12,478`). The eprfs peer tier has its own ALPN and gossip (`eprfs-peer/src/daemon.rs:333-341`), and no MCP rides it.

**napplets (NAP specs)**: verified artifacts run in `srcdoc` iframes with `sandbox="allow-scripts"` only. The shell binds every message to the napplet's *verified* identity through `MessageEvent.source`, never one the napplet claims. Napplets never receive keys, wallet credentials or raw network access. NAP-IDENTITY lets an app *"learn about the user but cannot act as the user"*, and signing goes through the shell. A shell's readiness signal is untrusted input (✅ `projections/web.md:18-22,84-92`, `NAP-IDENTITY.md:19`, `NAP-SHELL.md:136`). **Reading this against our tree found a live defect.** lamad frames html5-app bundles same-origin with `allow-scripts allow-same-origin`, and the doorway bearer token sits in that origin's `localStorage`, so any bundle author's script can read it. The chain is verified on disk and now has its own entry: [security-html5-app-iframe-same-origin-reads-session-token](epr:security-html5-app-iframe-same-origin-reads-session-token). The relying-party half of the rule already holds: our login component is a pure OAuth redirector with no password field (`imagodei/components/login/login.component.ts:1-10`).

### Substrate: FIPS, negentropy (T2 × sync plane)

**FIPS**: `node_addr` = SHA-256 of the secp256k1 key truncated to 16 bytes. A spanning tree is rooted at the lexicographically smallest `node_addr`. Reachability travels as 1 KB bloom filters (k=5, split horizon), and routing is greedy by tree distance (`fips-architecture.md:221`, `fips-mesh-layer.md:51`). Discovery publishes endpoint adverts as signed Nostr events (off by default; `fips-nostr-discovery.md:38`) or uses LAN mDNS. MMP measures each link's RTT, loss and jitter in-band. Ancestry is accepted on **transitive trust, with no per-entry signatures** (✅ `docs/design/fips-architecture.md:115,128`, `fips-spanning-tree.md:44,233-239`, `fips-bloom-filters.md:44-45,146`, `fips-nostr-discovery.md:25-26,437-446`, `fips-concepts.md:90-93`).

**negentropy**: both sides sort by `(timestamp, id)` and exchange ranges in Skip, Fingerprint or IdList mode, recursing where fingerprints differ. The fingerprint is SHA-256 of the sum mod 2²⁵⁶ of the ids, truncated to 16 bytes. Because it is **additive**, sub-range fingerprints can be cached and updated incrementally. Ordering by id alone (timestamp 0) is allowed (✅ `README.md:47-118`, `docs/negentropy-protocol-v1.md:73-80`). How ranges are split is left to the implementation (`README.md:92-100`). **Our sync opener is negentropy's first message without the recursion.** `ListDocumentsSince{corpus_digest}` is one SHA-256 over sorted `doc_id=heads` lines, refolded from scratch every round (`p2p/sync_round.rs:142-153`, `p2p/reconcile_rails.rs:26`). On a match we answer `InSync`. On any mismatch we **enumerate the whole corpus** (`p2p_iroh/sync_backend.rs:554-590`). On the wire, converged costs one message and one changed doc costs the whole corpus. In compute, every round costs O(corpus) on both sides even when converged, because the digest is rebuilt by sorting the whole corpus (`sync_round.rs:134-153`, `reconcile_rails.rs:27-34`).

### Schema: schemata (view contract × the epr Manifest resolver)

362 draft-07 YAML schemas for Nostr kinds, 206 of them with both `samples/valid*.json` and `invalid.*.json` beside them. **LIMITATIONS.md** lists about 270 semantic rules that JSON Schema cannot express, in 8 classes. **DIVERGENCES.md** records where observed wire practice departs from the spec (✅ `LIMITATIONS.md:9-22`, `DIVERGENCES.md:1-30`). We formalize more per schema. Our view contract requires a source-of-truth declaration, `additionalProperties:false` (only 7 of schemata's 362 set it), honest-absence envelopes and Rust-side drift tests (`elohim/sdk/schemas/v1/views/CONVENTIONS.md:12-70`; `elohim-storage/tests/schema_contract.rs`). We carry fewer *shared artifacts*. No sample instances sit beside our schemas: the contract tests build their instances inside the Rust test file (`elohim-storage/tests/schema_contract.rs`). Two draft-07 stragglers remain among 323 files (`views/put-blob-response.schema.json`, `manifests/discovery-resolvers.schema.json`). On the epr side, payload-schema validation against `schemaRef` is still *"Phase 2+"* (`elohim/epr/README.md`).

### Value: Bitcredit (shefa × mishpat)

Each e-bill is a hash-linked, Schnorr-signed block chain whose blocks each carry an opcode (`op_code()`, `crates/bcr-ebill-core/src/protocol/blockchain/mod.rs:102-118`; Issue, Accept, Endorse, Sell, Mint, Recourse…). The roles include Drawer, Payer, Holder, Buyer, Contingent and Recoursee, and **recourse runs back along past endorsers** (✅ `docs/concepts.md:8-39`, `bill/validation.rs:38-52`). Blocks are encrypted under per-bill shared keys and published as public Nostr events carrying encrypted payloads. NIP-17/59 gift wrap carries private messages and invites (✅ `protocol/event/blockchain_event.rs:10-16,67-73`, `protocol/event/mod.rs:23-34`). Mint liquidity paid in ecash is ⚠ (only the opcode is in code). Our VF `Claim` has optional `provider`/`receiver` strings and a free-string `action`, and the shared REA vocabulary already has `transfer`, `transfer-custody` and `transfer-all-rights` (`sdk/schemas/v1/enums/rea-action.schema.json:14-16`). **The gap is validation, not vocabulary:** both `Claim` and `Commitment` fall through to `_ => Valid` in integrity (`dna/elohim/zomes/content_store_integrity/src/lib.rs:1383,1450-1472,4322-4323`), so nothing checks who may transfer a claim or in what order. None of the 8 commitment schemas covers transfer of a claim, and mutual credit appears only as an enum label (`shefa/models/exchange.model.ts:193`).

## The three lenses

### Local-first reading (what each design leaves a person who is alone)

| Project | Alone, key on device | When the host goes away for good | Our reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concord / Marmot | Messages are readable from local MLS/epoch state. Admin acts wait for a relay | Relays are interchangeable. **Key loss is terminal** | Take the relay-is-a-cache shape. Leave the terminal answer (Cut 4) |
| Keycast | Nothing: the key lives on the team server | The team loses signing | Leave as an architecture (it is a hosted-custody pattern). Take its policy invariants for our *doorway-hosted* case, where the doorway does sign as the person (local-first §2) |
| Buzz | The agent signs with its own key. Usage records queue | Discovery and relay-membership via owner degrade, and authorship stays intact | Take *agent remains author*. Our agents run in the person's own workspace, so the "owner" is present by construction |
| napplets | The shell runs local verified bundles with no network | Nothing is lost, because the shell held the keys all along | The doorway-hosts-does-not-own rule, at app scale |
| FIPS | **Two devices on one LAN find each other by mDNS, with no internet** | The relay-discovery path degrades and the LAN path survives | **Red flag on our side.** iroh has no LAN discovery, and its peer book is empty at boot under sovereign defaults (`p2p_iroh/peer_book.rs:3-7`). libp2p has mDNS (`p2p/behaviour.rs:114-115`). A household on iroh-only with the internet down cannot find its own devices |
| negentropy | — | — | Applies to every sync between a person's own devices: the common case is "nearly converged", and that is exactly what the recursion makes cheap |

### Gradient reading (whether each cost should exist on that path)

- **negentropy is a unit conversion, not a trust compression.** Per-corpus becomes proportional to the differing ranges on the sync path (given balanced splitting, which negentropy leaves to the implementation), with a background lane and the *every-round* phase. It is owed at every trust level and weakens no check, because fetched bytes are still verified locally then served. That makes it the safest take in the survey. Its additive, 16-byte-truncated fingerprint trades some collision resistance for efficiency (the README calls it a security/efficiency compromise). Under verify-locally-then-serve a collision costs a missed sync (liveness), never integrity.
- **Concord's per-reader fold is trustless-and-verifying.** The ceremony is where it belongs, and it is paid on every client (`unit-per-reader`). Our design pays it once in integrity. The compression is already ours on paper and missing in code (Cut 2).
- **FROST fuses custody and witness.** A threshold signature is one Schnorr signature, so *who* co-signed is invisible (`friction-blind` to any auditor). Custody k-of-n is a separate dial from the witness record, so FROST may serve as a custody **accelerator** (key-share recovery), never as the evidence of standing.
- **Buzz NIP-AO vs NIP-AM is the passing/record split.** Live telemetry goes on an unstored channel, and the durable usage record is one per turn. This is local-first's *"a heartbeat hints; the record decides"* as a wire rule.
- **ContextVM's per-call Lightning price** puts a payment ceremony on the hot path of every tool call (`unit-per-call`). Our compute grant *authorizes* once per relationship and carries no price at all (`sdk/schemas/v1/commitments/delegates-compute.schema.json`), which is the compression the gradient asks for. Leave.

## What we have that the ecosystem lacks (export direction)

Content identity without author or clock (Cut 1). Authority validated once at commit rather than by every client (Cut 2, *once built*). Heads elected and moved by CAS with an expected prior (Cut 3). Succession by community witness (Cut 4, *designed, three variants still stubbed*). Compute delegation as a notarized grant that authorizes once per relationship. Each of these is half-built in places, and the survey names where. Nostr has shipped the group, agent and app *surfaces*. We are designing the *substrate* those surfaces need.

## Questions for the design review

1. **Read-side or write-side authority for collectives?** Either the imagodei integrity zome validates Membership authorship and `HasMembership` links (DNA-HASH-MOVING, rows 1/4 of the authority cluster), or a Concord-style deterministic fold runs in the storage projection. Today we have neither.
2. **What is the private-collective primitive?** Concord epochs and Refounding, MLS, or a NIP-4E-style shared key per collective, built on `private_replica.rs`? And what does a withdrawal trigger?
3. **Should an agent hold a device-local key** and sign what it does, citing the covenant clause that permitted it (NIP-OA with the covenant as root)? Or does that turn agents into credentialed principals, against *a claim never gates work* (`actor.rs:24-25`)?
4. **Where does per-turn usage live** so cost is attributed to the steward without making the vendor a payee: a private session record (NIP-AO-like) or a witnessed REA flow (NIP-AM-like)?
5. **Range reconciliation id:** `H(doc_id ‖ heads)` for Automerge docs, the blob CID for inventory, or both? Does it replace `corpus_digest`, or ship as a new variant under the one-release rule?
6. **EPR app bundles:** separate origin plus a NAP-style shell contract, or keep same-origin for service-worker delivery and move the token? (Security entry N7.)
7. **Authority across the holon graph (operator, 2026-10-29).** A holon's acts are checked today only against its own roster. Canon asks for *"maximum local autonomy within constitutional bounds"* and nested layers *"subject only to constitutional constraints from higher layers"* (`governance-layers-architecture.md:78-95`), so enforcement must also come from above: a holon cannot vote itself into an atrocity its enclosing framework forbids. It must also come from bodies drawn to the reach of a decision, for example for autonomous commons pools with no direct human steward. Row 19 names the missing nodes in the architecture's own terms: holon links, a constitution head per holon, acts validated up the CID chain, and `established_by` turned from a label into a citation. Left open: the sortition method, whether and how a holon may leave its enclosing framework (and whether leaving can ever escape a floor already binding it), and how the outermost constitution is itself governed.
8. **Claim endorsement and recourse:** a shefa EconomicEvent rule set, a mishpat standing question, or both? Validating `Claim` moves the elohim DNA hash.
