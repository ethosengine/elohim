**A correction the operator supplied, recorded because it reversed a claim.** The first draft of
§5.8 read GPU depreciation as a *disanalogy* to fibre — rails and fibre lasted, accelerators do not,
so the inheritance is weaker. That was wrong, and the operator's correction inverts it. Fibre was
"the gift that kept on giving" not because glass is durable but because a strand is a **passive
medium**: capacity is set by the transponders at either end, and those endpoints are silicon on a
Moore's-law clock. The same buried strand carried orders of magnitude more traffic for decades
without re-trenching. **The transponders depreciated too — that was the engine, not a defect.** A
datacenter has the identical two-part structure (durable permitted siting and power; fast-turning
accelerators), so depreciation is the *same pattern in the same place*, and the right conclusion is
the opposite of the tempting one: a buildout leaves the expensive permitted half behind, waiting for
cheaper endpoints. It also unified the section on **one force rather than two** — the endpoint
dynamic that makes the trench valuable is the dynamic that dissolves metering, which is why *Enron
did not lose to a competitor; it lost to the transponder.* Canon §5.8 now carries three *genuine*
disanalogies in its place (power as an operating cost fibre did not have; compute requiring recurring
silicon capital rather than scaling on the same medium; rising density forcing re-upgrades of the
durable half). Provenance: Friedman's lecture, via the operator.

**The historical claims in §5.8 are ◐ and recalled rather than researched this pass** — Enron
Broadband's bandwidth-trading venture and the Blockbuster video-on-demand contract (~$110m of future
profit booked, cancelled within months, service never meaningfully shipped); Friedman's reading of the
fibre overbuild as an accidental gift to the next economy, and the transponder/DWDM mechanism behind
it (a late-1990s strand lit at single-digit gigabits now carrying many terabits on the same glass —
directionally solid, exact figures unverified, and note there IS an eventual nonlinear ceiling in the
fibre the industry is only now approaching); and the post-crash trajectories of the
search and retail firms that inherited cheap bandwidth. **The Enron Broadband detail is the most
load-bearing single historical claim in the document** — it is the precedent for *metering a resource
about to become abundant* — and it should be checked at source before any outreach use. Operator
provenance: the dot-com reading is the operator's, from Friedman's lecture on the subject and on
Enron's bandwidth-metering collapse.

*One note on the generational-transfer claim.* Canon states it as an argument rather than citing a
source, deliberately. The original draft attributed it to a specific TED talk, which a blind reader
flagged as *"a research-process note embedded in what is supposed to be a canon companion"* — and the
claim is well-established enough not to rest on one talk. The reading behind it is Scott Galloway's,
and it is recorded here rather than there.

## 2. Build-state adjudication

House rule: verdicts adjudicate against **current build-state** with `file:line`, never the durable
spec. This section is the evidence behind canon §9.3, and the reason that paragraph is worded as
pessimistically as it is.

### 2.1 The token plane ships — dormant, unnotarized, unconsumed

The place a careless reading goes wrong in **both** directions.

- A token ledger **does** exist in shipped Rust — balances, mints, transfers, decay:
  `elohim/elohim-storage/src/{api/token.rs, services/token_{mint,ledger,decay}_service.rs,
  db/token_{balances,transfers,mint_events,decay_events}.rs}`. ✅
- It is **never DHT-anchored.** Every write site hardcodes `dht_anchor_hash: None`
  (`token_ledger_service.rs:111`, `token_mint_service.rs:95,167`), and the module declares itself
  *"Category B (agent-scoped): source of truth is the agent's local chain; storage is a materialized
  projection used for fast reads"* (`db/token_balances.rs:4-5`). A null anchor means local-only and
  unverifiable by any peer. ✅
- The whole `/api/v1/token/*` surface — 13 routes — has **zero consumers repo-wide**: not the Angular
  app, not a2o, not the seeder, not doorway. ✅
- Balances are `Float` in the schema. Any claim of financial-grade accounting fails on the arithmetic
  alone. ✅
- Last substantive touch: **2026-04-02**, roughly five months before HEAD. ✅

**The two senses of "token" must not be conflated.** Canon "token" (Stance I.2) means a tradable
ownership claim over the commons — refused. Code "token" means a unit of a decaying per-layer
currency-of-remembering — dormant scaffolding. Reading the code as hypocrisy is as wrong as reading
the crate README as architecture.

### 2.2 The `elohim-token` crate contradicts Stance I.2, and the stance wins

`elohim/elohim-token/README.md` declares a *"Default economic rail for the Elohim Protocol… a single
fungible medium of exchange minted from the aggregate of witnessed economic activity"*; `src/lib.rs`
is a *"Settlement Bridge Interface… for bridging elohim-tokens from the Holochain P2P substrate to any
settlement chain"*; the bootstrap table in `research/theory-of-value.md` ends at *"the token IS the
economy for most daily exchange."* ✅

Stance I.2 — later, and canon — says: *"Not a blockchain, and not a token… There is no coin to buy, no
allocation to farm, no ledger position to hold. If you came for a token, there isn't one, and there
will not be one, by design."* ✅

**Adjudication.** The stance's *reasoning* settles it rather than its seniority: a single fungible
rail is the "one asset whose price becomes the system's true objective function" the stance names, and
a settlement bridge to an external chain re-creates the ownership surface Stance I.1 exists to refuse.
The crate README and the settlement bridge are **superseded and should be retired.** What survives is
the decay kernel and the per-layer unit machinery — in the shape the Hypha survey specified:
**recomputed on read from notarized contribution events, never a stored bank-like balance**, with the
explicit instruction *"do NOT mint a `VoiceBalance`/`StandingToken` entry."* ✅ Today's SQLite
`token_balances` is exactly that stored balance, and this corpus owes an argument for why a
projection-layer balance is not the ledger that survey warned against. Position offered here, and minted as a design invariant in §6: **a projection is legitimate only
while it is *derivable*; the moment it is authoritative for anything it *is* the ledger** — and the
null `dht_anchor_hash` means no peer could check it regardless.

*One locator:* the quote canon §2 attributes to "elsewhere in the project" is
`elohim/elohim-token/research/theory-of-value.md`, predating canon §6's mechanism.

### 2.3 The governance gap that blocks the whole monetary posture

A "Mishpat policy" ships as a **lens**: a `Mishpat::Commitment` with `action: "author-lens"`, six
required fields (`action`, `governs_epr`, `school`, `role`, `rule`, `telos`), `role` a closed enum of
`{lens, floor, ceiling}`, validated at
`elohim/holochain/dna/mishpat/zomes/mishpat/src/commitments.rs:557-583`. The projection ships
end-to-end — a `lenses` table (`migrations/2026-06-27-120000_lenses/up.sql`) served over
`GET /api/v1/epr/:scope/lens-market`, fail-closing a NULL `dht_anchor_hash` out of the market. ✅

But **`binds-policy` is absent** from the coordinator dispatch (`commitments.rs:189-206`), so a
commitment declaring *which* lens governs an EPR is hard-rejected as "unhandled action." ✅ A lens can
be authored and read; nothing can declare one binding. **A currency you can propose but not enact is
not yet a currency**, and this is the single blocking item for the sibling monetary posture.

The lens market's ranking signals are also dormant: `lens_selections` (affinity) and `lens_verdicts`
(contention) have no production writer, and the migration says so itself — the folds degrade to
affinity=0 / contention=0. ✅ `genesis/a2o/features/qahal/plural-mishpat-lenses.feature` is 6
scenarios, all `@wip`. ✅

### 2.4 The countervailing find — the primitive is already right

`elohim/holochain/dna/mishpat/zomes/mishpat_integrity/src/lib.rs:18` defines `Precedent` with
`binding: constitutional | binding-network | binding-local | persuasive`, a `scope_json`, a
`citations` count, and a `superseded_by` amendment path — *"Precedents form the case law of the
governance system."* The entry enum at `:311` carries `Precedent`, `Discussion`, `GovernanceState`,
`GraduatedFeedback`, `OpinionStatement`, `Place`, `StringAnchor`, `ChallengeOutcome`, `Commitment` —
**no currency, unit, or balance type, and none is needed.** ✅

New commitment actions are **DNA-hash-neutral by construction**: the integrity zome's per-action
requirements table ends `_ => None` (`:812-887`), so only four actions carry integrity-level checks
and everything else hot-swaps via `update_coordinators`. ✅

And the enforcement machinery already exists in another costume. The bounds validator
(`elohim/elohim-storage/src/services/bounds_validator.rs`) runs **seven checks** against the
commitment named by an event's `bounded_by`: found · not revoked · inside `[valid_from, valid_until]`
· scope match · event reach ≤ `bounds.reach_ceiling` on the 8-level total order · sliding-60-minute
rate window · rotation TTL. ✅ That is a credit-limit enforcement engine, currently metering
authority rather than value.

*(Reach total order, since it is counter-intuitive and canon does not enumerate it:
`private(0) < self(1) < intimate(2) < trusted(3) < familiar(4) < community(5) < public(6) <
commons(7)`. **`commons` is the most permissive, not the most restricted.** ✅)*
