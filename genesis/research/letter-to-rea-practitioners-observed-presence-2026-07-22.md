# A Letter to REA Practitioners: The Observer Arrives

*Companion to the [ontology-systems survey](epr:ontology-systems-survey-reach-reconciliation-2026-07-22). Written to the ValueFlows/hREA lineage — the people who kept McCarthy's insight alive as an open commons — about what we believe the next evolution of the ontology must absorb.*

Dear stewards of REA,

Bill McCarthy's claim has aged into prophecy: *"REA endures because it treats explanation as a first-class requirement of economic systems, rather than as a by-product of computation."* We write from a working P2P protocol (Elohim — Holochain DHT + REA substrate, a cousin of hREA) to report what happened when we applied that claim to a problem REA was never asked to solve — and what we think it reveals about REA's next evolution.

## The problem that found us

Our protocol models "reach" — who may see and serve which content across relational, custodial, and geographic dimensions. We did what engineers do: we froze it into enums. Five subsystems each froze a *different* enum, and they drifted. The diagnosis, once we saw it through REA's eyes, was exact: **we had stored answers instead of facts.** Reach was never a scalar property of content; it is the standing outcome of a conversation between parties — declarations, relationships, consents, custody commitments — compressed, five different ways, into columns. Your own purchase/sale lesson names the cure: store one neutral event; *derive* every perspectival label. Vocabulary drift is what happens when perspectival projections are mistaken for stored truth.

That much is REA applied faithfully. Here is what REA could not give us.

## Three organs the ontology is missing

**1. A check algebra.** REA can say what happened and what was promised. It cannot say *who may see*. Authorization has its own mature lineage (Google's Zanzibar and descendants): declared relationship tuples, verdicts derived at query time by composable rules — including *exclusion*, which REA's duality algebra has no way to express — with explanation exposed as an API (the chain of relations that granted access, enumerable and testable). This is REA's own philosophy — facts stored, meaning derived, derivation explainable — grown independently in another field. The two should be introduced. An economic ontology that meters flows of goods and services but cannot derive-and-explain flows of *visibility* is incomplete in an economy where attention and access are the scarcest resources moving.

**2. A clock.** REA quietly assumes a shared ledger context. Distributed systems serve from replicas, and a revocation must be respected *before* the thing it protects is served (the authorization world calls this the "new enemy problem"). Every derived verdict therefore needs a freshness term — how stale may the evidence be? — as a first-class parameter, not an implementation detail. ValueFlows on agent-centric substrates (hREA, ours) already lives in this world; the vocabulary should name it.

**3. The observer.** This is the heart of the letter. REA's agents *transact*; they do not *arrive*. But the world now filling with dispatched agentic intelligence is a world of constant arrival: software agents presenting themselves at every interface, acting on behalf of principals. The surveillance economy's answer is to observe the visitor silently and say nothing. We believe the REA-native answer is better, and it was in the ontology all along: **make presence itself an economic flow.** An arriving agent *announces* — who I am, on whose behalf (a citable, revocable delegation commitment), and with what intent. That announcement is an **Intent**. Its subsequent traffic is witnessed **Events**. The diff is **fulfillment variance** — *what you said you would do versus what you did* — and that variance stream is the richest trust signal a network can own, computable with the commitment/fulfillment machinery you already maintain. Anonymity remains a right (it earns the public commons surface, honestly served, with safety-metadata logging only); announcement is a privilege that *buys depth* — the contact point where a system can infer, or better, negotiate what to disclose. The observer discloses to earn disclosure: surveillance's exact inversion, achieved with your algebra.

## Why the edge, why now

The classical objection — derivation is expensive, precompute the labels — is dissolving from two directions. At the open end, fully-public verdicts are viewer-independent and cost nothing (compute once, serve statically). At the intimate end, the gate is a key, not a computation. Derivation's real cost lives only in the middle bands — and the authorization lineage has shown that *derived* verdicts hold at two trillion relationships with sub-10ms checks. Meanwhile inference itself is leaving the datacenter: the household node that can *reason locally over its own witnessed evidence* is nearly here. The Cyc failure everyone cites against "ontologies that reason" was a *centralization* failure — one closed KB, one inference chokepoint, federation forbidden. The inversion is an ontology whose facts live at the edges that witnessed them, whose verdicts are derived where the evidence and the keys already are, and whose explanations are computed in the home they concern. That is McCarthy's requirement, radicalized: **at the intimate edge, an intelligence that cannot explain its verdict is not governed — merely obedient.** Explanation-first is what makes ubiquitous agency *safe to live with*.

## What is already yours vs. what is genuinely new

We owe you precision here, because most of what we described above is *not* a proposal — it is your existing vocabulary, used as intended. The honest split, organ by organ, with today's term against tomorrow's extension:

| Organ | Already in REA/ValueFlows today | The genuine gap | Proposed extension (the "tomorrow" column) | Natural standards home |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Check algebra** | The declared facts exist: `vf:AgentRelationship` (+ `AgentRelationshipRole`) already stores exactly what Zanzibar stores as relation tuples — *Alice is-member-of Choir*. Knowledge-level type-images/policies (Geerts–McCarthy) already claim the "rules that restrict legal configurations" role. | No vocabulary for **derivation rules over relationships** — how a visibility classification *composes* from relationship facts (union, intersection, and especially **exclusion/negation**, which REA's duality algebra cannot express). Today each implementation invents this privately. | A Knowledge-level **DerivationPolicy** object: named, composable rules deriving a classification from AgentRelationship/event patterns. *Example — today:* `AgentRelationship(Alice, member-of, Choir)` is storable but inert. *Tomorrow:* `visible-to: members(Choir) ∪ directors(Choir) ∖ suspended(Choir)` is a declared Knowledge-level object, so any conformant implementation derives the same verdict — and the rule itself is the explanation. | ValueFlows vocabulary extension first; expression-language alignment with W3C ODRL rather than a new formalism. |
| **Clock** | `EconomicEvent` already carries time (`hasPointInTime`, `hasBeginning/End`); events are immutable with correction-by-new-event — the append-only substrate the clock needs is your existing design. | Derived views carry no **evidence horizon**: nothing in the vocabulary says *as-of-when* a derived classification was true, or orders a **revocation** ahead of what it protects (the "new enemy problem"). | (a) A norm, not a new class: any derived view/verdict MUST carry `asOf` + derivation provenance — best done by **aligning with W3C PROV-O** (`prov:wasDerivedFrom`, `prov:generatedAtTime`) rather than minting terms. (b) A first-class **revocation action** in the action vocabulary, with the ordering requirement stated normatively. *Example — today:* a mutual-credit balance is derived from events, but nothing records against which event-horizon. *Tomorrow:* the balance (or visibility verdict) states "derived from events ≤ T, rules version R" — cacheable, contestable, and safely serveable from replicas. | ValueFlows + PROV-O alignment; the ordering semantics belong in ISO/IEC 15944-4's next revision as a conformance requirement for distributed implementations. |
| **Observer** | Almost everything: announcement is `vf:Proposal`/`vf:Intent` (Plan level); attention and access are resources *today* via `resourceClassifiedAs` (no new resource class needed); said-vs-did is `Commitment`→`Fulfillment` (+`Claim` for the unfulfilled case). This organ is 90% your machinery pointed at a flow it never metered. | **Variance is derivable but unnamed** — the computable delta between Plan and Observation levels has no first-class term, so no two implementations exchange it as a trust signal. And arrival/presence has no action term. | (a) Name **Variance** (Plan-vs-Observation delta, per agent, per scope) so reputation systems interoperate on it instead of each deriving privately. (b) A small **presence/arrival action** term for announcement events. *Example — today:* an agent's crawler fetches 10k documents; nothing in the ontology relates that to anything it declared. *Tomorrow:* the agent's arrival Intent ("indexing, on behalf of P, scope S") is a Proposal; its fetches are Events; the standardized Variance object between them is the trust signal any network can consume. | ValueFlows extension for Variance + action term; the wire-level announcement handshake itself belongs at **IETF (the emerging Web Bot Auth work)**, not in REA — REA standardizes the *meaning*, IETF the *transport*. |

And beneath all three organs sits a fourth discipline — for the **'A' itself**. Today `vf:Agent` is an opaque identifier plus a name; every claim about *who an agent is* lives outside the ontology. The observer organ cannot work that way: announcement is only worth trusting if the announced identity is **resolvable** (dereference the identifier to its live key material and service endpoints) and its claims are **inspectable** (issuer-signed, selectively presentable, revocable — verifiable without phoning the issuer). This is not something REA should invent; it is the W3C **DID + Verifiable Credentials** stack, adopted by reference: an announcement is then simply a *Verifiable Presentation attached to an Intent*, and delegation ("I act for P, within bounds, revocable here") is a capability credential (UCAN/ZCAP-shaped) any verifier can inspect. *Example — today:* `vf:Agent "crawler-7"` asserts it acts for a research lab; nothing can check this. *Tomorrow:* the agent's Intent carries a presentation resolving to its DID, containing the lab's signed delegation credential — identity and authority inspected at the door, variance accrued against an identity that cannot be costlessly shed. One warning from our own practice: the DID ecosystem's dominant framing makes the individual keyholder the apex of identity. Take the *mechanics* (resolvable identifiers, verifiable claims) and leave that ontology behind — claims are strongest when communities and institutions issue and backstop them, recovery must survive key loss, and a key alone is custody, not personhood.

The pattern across all three rows: **the facts layer is already yours; what needs standardizing is the derivation layer** — the rules, the freshness/provenance norms, and the names for derived objects (verdicts, variance) — so that independent implementations derive *the same* meanings and can exchange them. That is, we think, the precise sense in which McCarthy's explanation-first principle becomes a standard rather than a philosophy: the next REA doesn't add nouns; it standardizes *how conclusions are derived, dated, and explained*.

## What we ask

Nothing but attention, and perhaps conversation. We are implementing this as an extension-in-spirit of ValueFlows: presence-announcement as Intent; access-verdicts as derived, explained, freshness-stamped classifications over declared facts; said-vs-did variance as the trust primitive. If the pattern proves out, the vocabulary questions — how announcement, verdict, and variance should be named so independent implementations converge — are exactly the kind of stewardship this community does best. The observer has arrived at the ontology's door, announcing itself. We think REA should answer.

With respect for forty years of kept insight,
— the Elohim Protocol project
