# A Letter to the Shapes and Policy Practitioners: Where the Rule Ends

*Companion to the [letter to REA practitioners](epr:letter-to-rea-practitioners-observed-presence-2026-07-22) and the [OWL 2 graduation evaluation](epr:owl2-graduation-floor-ceiling-ontology-2026-07-23). Written to the SHACL / ODRL / RDF lineage — the people who kept validation and policy expression alive as open standards — about one verdict value their vocabularies cannot say, at the moment both communities happen to be holding the pen.*

Dear stewards of the shapes,

We write from a working P2P protocol (Elohim — Holochain DHT + REA substrate) that spent this month evaluating your stack with the respect of actually running it. We encoded our canonical access rule in OWL 2 DL and put it through HermiT; we read the SHACL 1.2 Core Working Draft published this very day; we follow the ODRL Community Group's draft formal semantics. We took disciplines from you that we now hold as law: the one-abstract-structure-many-syntaxes doctrine, the annotation-inertness lesson, JSON-LD's proof that vocabularies travel farthest in developers' native shapes. This letter is not a critique arriving from outside. It is a report from a deployment your specs never promised to serve — verdicts that govern *people*, computed on household hardware, consumed increasingly by dispatched software agents — and one organ that deployment needs which no vocabulary in the current pipeline can express.

## The problem that found us

Our protocol derives verdicts: may this content be served to this context, may this claim be cited as settled, may this delegated agent act. We built the derivation layer on your lessons — declared facts, derived conclusions, explanation as a first-class artifact. And then we hit a wall your specifications do not name: **some questions are not the rule's to answer.** A claim under active dispute. A situation the policy authors never imagined. An authority boundary. Our constitution requires that such cases route to a named human layer with a stated reason — and we found that every formalism in the stack is *total within its scope*. SHACL's `sh:conforms` is `true` or `false`. ODRL evaluation yields permitted or prohibited. OWL entailment yields entailed or not-entailed, and "unknown" is a property of the reasoner's silence, not a term that can carry a reason or a routing target.

We did not merely argue this; we demonstrated it. In OWL 2 DL we defined `Pending ≡ ¬Visible ⊓ ¬NotVisible` and asked HermiT: `Pending` is provably equivalent to `owl:Nothing` — *uninhabitable by construction*, because complement is total and partitions the domain. There is no residue in which a third value can live. The closure axioms we had to write to defeat the open world are the same axioms a late fact detonates. (We owe you a confession here, because precision is the register you deserve: our first argument against OWL-as-policy-engine was the classical ex falso one — poison the graph, receive universal permit. Our own adversarial panel refuted us: a guarded reasoner like HermiT *throws* on inconsistency rather than answering. The true failure is blast-radius and zero attributability — every query against the whole graph fails, naming no subject, no rule, no witness — and it becomes fail-open only if the caller maps exception to allow. We corrected our documents. The demonstration stands on the corrected ground.)

## What is already yours

Most of what we built is your machinery, used as intended, and we want that on the record. The SHACL validation report is the finest explanation artifact in any W3C Recommendation — a *structured, typed account of why the answer is what it is* — and it shaped our witness types directly. The target/shape split taught us where validation belongs. RDF 1.2's triple terms (now at Candidate Recommendation) are the natural home we cite for statement-level provenance the moment they reach Recommendation. PROV-O gave us the derivation vocabulary we adopt by reference. And ODRL is the *only* mature vocabulary that made prohibition first-class with a declared conflict strategy — `deny-overrides` as a policy-level commitment is a thing we hold as constitutional law, and you standardized its name a decade ago.

## The honest split, organ by organ

| Organ | Already in your stack today | The genuine gap | Proposed extension (tomorrow) | Where, and why now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The referral verdict** | ODRL has permit/prohibit + conflict strategies; SHACL has conforms true/false; the ODRL CG is drafting evaluator semantics right now (draft report 2026-03-10, prototype evaluator live). | **No vocabulary can say "not decidable by rule — routed to a named human authority, for this reason."** Silence means unknown (open-world) or deny (default-deny); neither carries a reason, a routing target, or evidence. As dispatched agents become the primary consumers of verdicts, a binary vocabulary **structurally erases the escalation path** — the agent cannot represent "escalate" because its input format cannot say it. This is a safety property, not a convenience. | A third evaluation outcome — call it *refer* — carrying (a) the authority layer it routes to, (b) a reason class (novel-situation, insufficient-authority, contested-evidence), (c) evidence. We run this today as `Decision{permit, refuse, refer(layer, reason)}` across three verdict surfaces, with a law-test that no code path may collapse *refer* into *refuse*. | **ODRL CG formal semantics — while the pen is up.** An evaluator semantics that ships binary will freeze the gap into the first generation of conformant engines. This is the cheapest moment there will ever be to admit a third value. |
| **The positive witness** | `sh:ValidationReport` — but it reports only violations. A conformant `sh:conforms true` emits **no results at all**. | An unexplained *yes* is as ungoverned as an unexplained *no*. When verdicts gate people's access, speech, or standing, the permit must be contestable too — which requires knowing *which checks passed on what evidence*. | Allow validation reports to carry passed-check results: per-check identity, outcome, and the observed evidence. We run this as `CheckWitness{checkId, outcome: passed|failed|skipped, observed}` — our reports state why the answer was yes. | **SHACL 1.2 Core, at Working Draft as of today (2026-07-23).** A WD is exactly when a report vocabulary can still grow a result class without breaking anyone. |
| **Derived, never stored** | SHACL-AF's rules (now graduating to the Recommendation-track SHACL 1.2 Rules) already model derivation; RDF 1.2 gives derived statements an annotation home. | No standard way for a *vocabulary itself* to declare: "this term's values are outcomes of a declared derivation over witnessed facts — never writable columns," with the determinism obligation stated (canonical ordering, so independent replicas derive identical conclusions). Stored copies of derived labels are how vocabularies drift — we know because five of ours did. | A `sourceOfRecord` declaration on vocabulary terms: derived-from-what, by-which-declared-rule, reproducible-how. We ship this as an axis-card field (`sourceOfRecord: {kind: "derived-fold", ...}`) beside each derived vocabulary. | SHACL 1.2 Rules is the natural anchor — a rules Recommendation is precisely where "this class is rule-derived, do not assert it manually" belongs as a declarable, checkable property. |
| **The clock** | RDF 1.2 triple terms can *carry* an as-of annotation; PROV-O can name generation time. | Nothing *norms* it: no requirement that a derived conclusion served from a replica state its evidence horizon, and no ordering guarantee that a revocation is respected before what it protected is served (the authorization world's "new enemy problem"). | A freshness norm on derived, replica-served conclusions. **We flag this at a lower confidence tier than the rows above: we have identified and evidenced this gap in our own system, but we have not yet built the clock — we name it as a gap, we do not yet claim a practice.** | RDF 1.2 + PROV-O usage guidance once triple terms reach REC; the ordering semantics likely belong with the ODRL evaluator work. |

And one framing note, offered in the same spirit as the identity warning in our REA letter: as Verifiable Credentials 2.0 (a full Recommendation since 2025) and DID 1.1 (now at CR) meet policy evaluation, please hold the door open for **subjects who hold standing but no key** — the unregistered person a participating relative's actions affect, the creator whose work arrived before they did, the holder whose key was recovered by their community rather than possessed continuously. A verdict vocabulary whose subject term assumes an unbroken keyholder quietly excludes exactly the people most in need of its protections. Take nothing from the mechanics; the mechanics are excellent. The assumption is the leak.

## What we ask

Nothing but attention, and perhaps conversation — the Community Groups are open and we intend to show up rather than merely write. We are implementing all of the above as extensions-in-spirit: the referral verdict, the positive witness, and the derived-vocabulary declaration are running code with adversarial tests in a live protocol; the clock is a named gap we will build against our own stated norm. If the pattern proves out, the naming questions — what *refer* is called, how a passed-check result is typed, where a `sourceOfRecord` declaration lives — are exactly the stewardship your communities do best. The verdicts are leaving the datacenter and arriving in households, carried by agents that will faithfully represent whatever the vocabulary can say — and faithfully erase whatever it cannot. We think the vocabulary should be able to say *"this one is yours to decide."*

With respect for two decades of kept shapes,
— the Elohim Protocol project
