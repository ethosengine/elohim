# Agentic-harness borrows backlog (research mint pass, 2026-08-13)

Externally-sourced **context and state disciplines for long-horizon agent runs**, harvested from
[the survey](epr:context-engineering-primary-sources-cross-pollination-2026-08-13) — a claim-by-claim
trace of the "progressive context shaping" transcript back to its primary sources (OpenAI harness
engineering, Anthropic's science/harness/managed-agents/400k-session posts, Arize's Alyx, OpenAI
Symphony). The survey's core finding sets this cluster's shape: **the gap is projective, not
representational** — we already hold the richer durable workflow object (REA valueflows over EPRs,
with `habits.yaml`'s admission-controlled WIP fence, which the field has no analog for at all) and
the poorer loop (we render it once at session start and then run on conversational memory). Sibling
of [agentic-context-tooling-consolidation-queue](epr:agentic-context-tooling-consolidation-queue)
(internal consolidation of our own `.claude/` tooling — this cluster is *external* borrows; compose,
don't duplicate) and of [measure-family-borrows-backlog](epr:measure-family-borrows-backlog) (row 7
below composes with its landed rows 12–14). Every row carries its survey verdict and section
pointer; the survey's grades and corrections are preserved verbatim, not softened.
**Fold new survey-sourced agentic-harness borrows here — do not mint siblings.**

| 1 | **Per-turn run-state projection** | Survey **TAKE-1**, the highest-leverage item. Source: Arize's **PlanMessage** (survey §1.5) — the plan is lifted out of conversation history entirely, re-derived from durable state on *every* loop iteration and injected at a fixed position `[System prompt] → [PlanMessage] → [Session history] → [Current turn]`, so it cannot drift deeper into context as tokens accumulate. **This is the corpus's ONLY per-call injection precedent** (survey WATCH-10 restates it: Arize's PlanMessage remains the only evidence for the third loop). Second source: Anthropic's **managed-agents session log** (§1.8) — the window is a *projection* of a durable append-only event log, so **compaction and handoff are the same operation**, two slices of one log. Symphony contributes the **around-the-run reconciliation cadence only** (poll 30s → reconcile → re-check issue state after every turn) and explicitly **NOT** per-call injection: its `WORKFLOW.md` renders the full state block on the *first* turn only, continuation turns carry guidance, and `WORKFLOW.md` edits apply to future launches, not in-flight sessions (§1.6). Do not promote its evidence into the injection claim — survey **WATCH-10** names that exact collapse as the first draft's error. What to build: re-derive a short block from state we already keep — `habits.yaml` top red + active habits (the max-2 WIP fence) + the saga frontier + this run's open `commitment:claim:` rows — and inject it per-turn and post-compaction. **Per-harness projection**: a Claude hook for Claude; Codex arrives via row 3's root map (its instruction chain is built once per run, so a Codex run gets the projection only if the root map fits its budget). `habits-status.py` already computes most of the block and `epr flow project` most of the rest. **Adds NO new register** (survey LEAVE-11: a `current.md` would be a fifth register with no reader; the function is real, the container is a projection of state we already keep). **MUST ship with a `retire-when:`** — Anthropic's sprint scaffold was load-bearing on one model tier and dead weight four months later (§1.4, survey §4.6); composes with [agentic-context-tooling-consolidation-queue](epr:agentic-context-tooling-consolidation-queue) item 16's landed `retire-when:` mechanism. | Design pass + a load-bearingness stress test written **before** the projection ships (the retire-when condition is the deliverable, not a footnote) | agentic-tooling shift |
| 2 | **Write-path discipline: corrections outlive turns only in the EPR plane** | Survey **TAKE-2**. Zero tooling, adoptable today, and the **precondition for row 1** — a projection loop over state nobody writes to projects nothing. The rule: a correction or constraint that must survive compaction or reach the next session gets **written to the commitment graph or `habits.yaml`**; conversation is history by definition. Survey §4.4 names the precise hole: a mid-run operator correction lands in the conversation, compaction is lossy summarization of exactly that region, and nothing re-injects it afterward because habits/commitments were never told — so survival depends on the summarizer's judgment ("an outdated judgment left in charge"). Survey §4.2 types it: the operator's mid-run correction **is the algedonic channel**, and if the pain signal only reaches the current draft and not the state, *it was absorbed by the thing that caused it*. This row is that channel's repair. Lands as an `.epr-meta` inject rule and/or a gospel line — a discipline, not a tool. | **None — mintable now** (survey §5 TAKE-2: "adoptable today", zero tooling; the precondition for row 1) | elohim-epr-metafile authoring pass |
| 3 | **Root `AGENTS.md` Codex instruction-budget repair** | Survey **STUDY-7**, upgraded by the Codex pass from a soft comparison to a **mechanically reproduced live defect**. Codex builds its instruction chain once per run, root-to-cwd, against a combined project-doc budget defaulting to 32 KiB (`DEFAULT_PROJECT_DOC_MAX_BYTES`, [`openai/codex`](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/main/codex-rs/config/src/config_toml.rs)); once the root spends the budget, deeper files get nothing. Our generated root `AGENTS.md` is **42,327 bytes / 364 lines** with **no `.codex/config.toml` override** — a Codex run receives exactly **32,768 bytes** and truncates mid-line at **`AGENTS.md:308`**, so the View Schema Contract, Critical Gotchas, CI/CD, and Code Style **never reach a Codex run at all**. (The Codex-local governance bridge *does* load — it is front-loaded at lines 1–83.) Existence and boundary of the defect are ✅ verified. Repair choices, for the design pass to pick among: shrink the root to a **≤100-line map** (OpenAI's harness-engineering finding, §1.1 — `AGENTS.md` as a *table of contents*, not an encyclopedia, with progressive disclosure into a docs tree; the named failure mode is "context is scarce, and a bloated instruction file crowded out the actual task", and what a monolith becomes is verbatim "a graveyard of stale rules"), relocate scoped guidance to subtree files, raise the budget via `.codex/config.toml`, or combine. **HARD constraint: the file is a `plant-eprfs-agentdoc` projection — the repair goes through package authority, never a hand-edit of `AGENTS.md`.** | Design pass to choose the repair shape; then the plant path (projection is verbatim passthrough, so the doc must change at its package root) | elohim-package-authoring shift |
| 4 | **Commitment-to-dispatch puller: delivery-stasis reads/writes the commitment plane natively** | Survey **STUDY-5**. Symphony's mature shape (§1.6): poll the tracker (30s default) → reconcile → dispatch; issue state re-checked after **every** turn (up to `agent.max_turns`, default 20, on one live thread); per-issue isolated path-confined workspace; and `Human Review` as a **first-class successful handoff boundary** — "a successful run can end at a workflow-defined handoff state, not necessarily `Done`" — explicitly **NOT a terminal state** (it is not in the spec's terminal list; moving there stops work without terminal-state cleanup). Applied to our control plane: 556 active commitments / 539 unfulfilled, measured 2026-08-13 (`epr flow status`, reproduced by the Codex pass). **PLANE-TYPING CAUTION (survey §4.1a — do not soften):** Symphony's claim states `Unclaimed → Claimed → Running → RetryQueued → Released` are **ephemeral scheduler reservations** — anti-duplication leases owned by one orchestrator, not restored on restart (the service reconstructs by re-polling the tracker and reusing workspaces). Ours are **durable REA promises**: `commitment:claim:<gap>` is a `Commitment` (`Proposed → Active → Fulfilled/Revoked`) whose fulfillment is carried by economic events (`elohim/epr-rea/src/model.rs:90`; minted `Active` only from a textually `CLAIMED` decomposed item, `elohim/eprfs/epr-cli/src/flow/project.rs:526`). **The puller design JOINS an ephemeral claim lease to the durable commitment — it never conflates them.** The measured gap is exactly one row of §4.1a's table: `epr flow status` counts but does not dispatch (`elohim/eprfs/epr-cli/src/flow/walk.rs:405`). **Do not build new: `/delivery-stasis` IS the hand-cranked puller** — refine it to read and write the commitment plane natively rather than minting a scheduler beside it. The operator gate maps to our ratification-at-dev-merge, not to a mid-run interrupt. | 5 | **Run-scale failed-approaches field** | Survey **TAKE-3**, and the **cheapest item in the mint**. | 6 | **Memory-ceremony Phase-1 pick de-escalation** | Survey **TAKE-4** (§4.5) — an operator observation of 2026-08-13, corroborated by Anthropic's ~400k-session study (§1.7): users made ~70% of **planning** decisions (*what* to build, what counts as done), Claude ~80% of **execution** decisions (*how* — which files, what code, which commands). | 7 | **Dev-system equilibrium as rates-against-rates** | Synthesis-born in the 2026-08-13 session — a Meadows lens laid over the survey, not a claim from a primary. The honest measure is **drain rate vs inflow rate, per loop, weekly** — controllability is *response rate vs drift growth rate*, and equilibrium becomes a runnable check: **every stock in the development system has drain ≥ inflow.** This is the same discipline the survey demands of harness parts (§1.4/§4.6: scaffolding depreciates, keep only what is load-bearing) applied to our own maintenance loops rather than to a model tier. **CANDIDATE HABIT — covenant-gated**: `habits.yaml` is capped at max 12 with max 2 `active`, so admission either **displaces an existing habit or waits**; that is an operator decision, not an agent's. refreshing an in-memory work object, and placing current state into the next inference request are
three different operations, and evidence for one must never be silently promoted into evidence for
another; this **lives in survey prose** (§1.6, §4.1a) and is enforced inside row 1's wording.

omits the two mechanisms that actually matter — the PlanMessage's per-call re-derivation and the
durable session log.
