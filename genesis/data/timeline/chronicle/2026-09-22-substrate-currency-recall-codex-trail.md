# Recall — Codex's trail, and the memory it was supposed to reach

Codex left six observations on 2026-09-13 about resuming interrupted work. The sprint turned four of
them into the recall entry: it now reaches non-markdown and root-scope authorities, resumes a named
habit with its newest evidence dated and the later commits marked unverified, and says when a
config comment contradicts the value beside it. The deterministic question bank went from 1 of 6 to
5 of 6.

The ceremony run on that entry found the memory it serves in worse shape than the code. The index
projected past its hard bound, so the projection refused and every session loaded a truncated
index; twelve lessons — two in the operator's own words — had sat uncommitted in the shared tree
for up to ten days. They are now recorded, sixteen entries folded into their umbrellas with every
file kept whole, and the index is 19,775 B across 94 rows. The idempotence check that had been
silently skipping on dev now runs and passes.

Two lessons. A green check can hide a skip: the index test never failed on dev, because the thing
it checked refused to exist. And a ledger's direction is not a convention to assume — the resume
view's first defect was trusting position over date, caught by the first fresh reader to use it.

Frontier: the habit stays red on its 91-day window (9 of 14 journeys); the standing reader's weekly
runs decide it. Held for the operator: pre-existing findings on the ceremony feature, S4's classes
reaching dispatched readers, the build-receipt artifact chain, and installing the new `epr` binary.
